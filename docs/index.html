MMVistaReason: Toward Open-Data and Post-Training Recipes for Multimodal Reasoning
Authors: Juekai Lin, Honglin Lin, Yuqian Yuan, Xiaolong Wu, Jie Cao, Liang Liang, Yunqi Cao, Yun Zhu, Wenqiao Zhang, Lijun Wu
First: 2026-10-01T09:22:00+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-22T16:54:37+00:00
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Open multimodal reasoning models have benefited from large-scale reasoning supervision, yet reliable post-training remains challenging due to uneven data quality, inefficient supervision construction, imbalanced difficulty, and cross-domain interference. We introduce MMVistaReason (MVR), an open-data post-training recipe with three components: (1) broader capability coverage across complementary Analytical and Real-World reasoning groups, emphasizing structured reasoning versus visual perception and spatial grounding; (2) efficient SFT and RL data construction, standardizing heterogeneous open data through staged cleaning and annotation, combining difficulty-aware cascaded teacher distillation with answer-likelihood-based trajectory selection to construct MVR-SFT-528K, and applying scale-specific frontier filtering for MVR-RL-63K; and (3) specialize-then-integrate training, which trains complementary RL experts and consolidates their capabilities through multi-teacher on-policy distillation (MOPD). Our analyses reveal a capacity-dependent interaction between supervision difficulty, trajectory quality, and model capacity: smaller students benefit more from selected supervision, while larger students are robust to trajectory variation and mixed-domain interference. Mixed-domain RL introduces benchmark-level negative transfer, whereas MOPD provides consistent capability integration, with the preferred KL direction varying across model scales. Across 15 multimodal benchmarks, MVR-4B achieves an average score of 72.8, outperforming Qwen3.5-9B (Instruct) and MMFineReason-8B while using about 70% fewer samples than MMFineReason. Scaling to 9B improves the average to 74.4, surpassing Qwen3.5-35B-A3B (Instruct). Overall, MMVistaReason demonstrates that systematic open-data construction and capacity-aware post-training provide a practical and scalable path toward reliable multimodal reasoning.

Predictable performance is suboptimal, and what it means for model routing
Authors: Guanhua Zhang, Moritz Hardt
First: 2026-10-22T15:27:27+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-22T15:27:27+00:00
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Large language models (LLMs) differ in capabilities and costs, motivating routing methods that adaptively choose a model for each query. These methods rely on performance prediction: given a query, estimate whether a model will answer it correctly. Most routing methods train separate predictors of candidate models' performance to guide model selection. Our work shows that performance prediction only beats a simple baseline when the candidate model is suboptimal on the task or the performance predictor adds significant capacity relative to the candidate model. Specifically, we take a model's self-confidence, the probability it assigns to its chosen answer, as a baseline and study how much a performance predictor can improve on it. First, we show that any performance predictor that outperforms a model's self-confidence can be used to improve the model's loss: the predictor's advantage transfers exactly in log loss through recalibration. Second, accounting for the predictor's additional parameters, we bound its advantage by the model's suboptimality plus the gain from those parameters. Third, we extend our theoretical results to two-model cascades, which route queries to a more capable model when the first model is predicted to fail. Experiments with 16 language models on four benchmarks show that predictors gain a larger advantage over self-confidence on more suboptimal models. This advantage shrinks as models improve through finetuning. Cascading exhibits the same two patterns: predictor-based routing offers larger gains over confidence-based routing for more suboptimal models, and these gains diminish after finetuning.

Multilevel Stochastic Plug-and-Play for Sparse-View CT Reconstruction
Authors: Antoine De Paepe, Alexandre Bousse, Dimitris Visvikis
First: 2026-06-23T13:34:30+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-22T14:52:19+00:00
Comments: 13 pages, 7 figures, 3 tables
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2 · Code3
Abstract
Sparse-view computed tomography (SVCT) reduces radiation exposure and acquisition time, but the limited number of projection views makes the reconstruction problem severely ill-posed and leads to streak artifacts when analytical methods are used. Plug-and-Play (PnP) methods combine data fidelity with learned image priors, while stochastic PnP methods use re-noising to reduce the mismatch between denoiser training inputs and reconstruction iterates. However, these methods often require many iterations, which limits their practical efficiency. In this work, we propose Multilevel Stochastic Plug-and-Play (ML-SPnP), a multilevel (ML) stochastic PnP method for SVCT reconstruction formulated on wavelet multiresolution analysis (MRA) approximation spaces. The method combines coarse-to-fine initialization with a finite sequence of multilevel corrections using resolution-specific denoisers. Under suitable assumptions, the fine-level iterations following the finite multilevel phase inherit the convergence guarantees of the underlying stochastic PnP algorithm. Experiments on two CT datasets with 20-50 projection views show that ML-SPnP achieves reconstruction quality comparable to the evaluated stochastic PnP methods, while generally requiring less reconstruction time. These results support multilevel acceleration as an effective strategy for reducing the computational cost of stochastic PnP reconstruction. The code will be made publicly available at https://github.com/antoinedepaepe/ml-spnp.

Reinforcing intra-operative tumour localisation with weak supervision from pre-operative appearances
Authors: Sidhart Sami, Ali Sharjeel, Sri Atluri, Miao Zhao, Caroline Roney, Eirini Marouli, Shaheer U. Saeed
First: 2026-10-22T13:33:10+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-22T13:33:10+00:00
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Limited tumour visibility on intra-operative images complicates surgical navigation, including liver hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC) targeting on computed tomography (CT) imaging. This makes labelled datasets difficult to curate for deep learning, and transferring pre-operative delineations through registration remains challenging due to intra-operative deformation and lack of paired pre- and intra-operative data. We propose to transfer tumour appearances learned from pre-operative magnetic resonance (MR) images to guide localisation on intra-operative CT through weak supervision. A classifier is trained to classify tumour presence on pre-operative images. This is applied to windows on intra-operative images, and despite the domain gap, it assigns higher tumour-presence probabilities on average to tumour-containing windows. These weak cross-domain signals guide a reinforcement learning agent to localise tumours on intra-operative images. We compare direct transfer, few-shot adaptation and synthetic transfer (trained using unpaired MR and CT), which translates CT into synthetic MR before localisation. On an external HCC cohort, synthetic transfer outperformed other tested methods and CT-only baselines. Our framework enables cross-modality tumour localisation without paired MR-CT training images.

Abstract
Practical autonomous driving requires models that generalize by reasoning through spatial-temporal possibilities to exclude unsafe outcomes. While state-of-the-art (SOTA) methods use parallel planning architectures, they fail to explicitly couple speed decisions with agent behavior along the driving path, leading to suboptimal coordination. To address this, we propose a cascaded framework that transforms longitudinal planning from an independent prediction task into a path-conditioned reasoning process. On the model side, we introduce an anchor-based regression design that conditions longitudinal prediction on the lateral drive path, and reformulate longitudinal planning as 1D displacement prediction along the path. This reduces geometric uncertainty and sharpens the model's focus on interaction-driven dynamics. On the data side, we introduce a planning-oriented data augmentation strategy that simulates rare safety-critical events by programmatically inserting agents and relabeling longitudinal targets to enforce collision avoidance. Evaluated on the challenging Bench2Drive benchmark, our method achieves SOTA performance with a driving score of 89.07 and a success rate of 73.18%, demonstrating significantly improved coordination and safety. Further evaluation on Fail2Drive confirms strong generalization to rare edge cases where parallel formulations typically fail. Project page:https://yanhaowu.github.io/AlignDrive/.

ReactiveBFM: Reactive Closed-Loop Motion Planning Towards Universal Humanoid Whole-Body Control
Authors: Xiao Chen, Weishuai Zeng, Xiaojie Niu, Zirui Wang, Jianan Li, Huayi Wang, Furui Xu, Jiahe Chen, Weixiang Zhong, Lihe Ding, Kailin Li, Jiangmiao Pang, Tai Wang, Tianfan Xue, Jingbo Wang

Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
While current Behavior Foundation Models (BFMs) provide robust control priors for humanoids, they only execute pre-defined reference motions. As a result, they are vulnerable to environmental shifts and incapable of reactive whole-body coordination. Naively cascading them with generative motion planners fails to achieve true reactivity, as inevitable tracking discrepancies induce fatal cumulative exposure bias. To bridge this gap, we propose ReactiveBFM, a real-time closed-loop planning-control framework. At its core, we effectively mitigate exposure bias via a scheduled prefix sampling curriculum, forcing the generative planner to actively learn error-recovery behaviors from imperfect physical states rather than ground-truth trajectories. Systematically, to reconcile the severe latency mismatch between auto-regressive planning and high-frequency tracking, we introduce an asynchronous replanning mechanism. Combined with trajectory chunking to temporally ensemble spatial references, our system guarantees spatio-temporally fluid execution without physical jitter. Deployed on the Unitree G1 humanoid, ReactiveBFM demonstrates unprecedented physical agility across a vast repertoire of text-conditioned closed-loop motions. Notably, ReactiveBFM achieves zero-shot moving target reaching, showcasing intricate whole-body coordination and on-the-fly replanning. In sim-to-sim benchmarking under severe perturbations, ReactiveBFM achieves a 93.1% success rate, significantly outperforming cascaded open-loop baselines by 28.6%.

H-2DGS: Height-Field Gaussian Splatting for Sharp and Deformable Neurosurgical Surface Reconstruction
Authors: Zhang Zichuan, Bart Brouwers, Rembrandt Bakker, Maud Landers, Geert-Jan Rutten, Maxime Chamberland
First: 2026-10-22T08:33:14+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-22T08:33:14+00:00
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Neurosurgical navigation commonly presents magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) as 2D slices, making it difficult to relate the anatomy in those slices to the cortical surface observed through the surgical microscope. We seek a common 3D visualization that combines a photorealistic reconstruction of the exposed cortex during tumor surgery with whole-brain MRI context and diffusion tractography pathways. This requires the reconstructed cortex to form a clear boundary around the brain anatomy rather than a diffuse volume that obscures internal structures. Conventional 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) can reproduce observed views but, under limited surgical view coverage, may distribute opacity through a thick and geometrically ambiguous band. We introduce height-field 2D Gaussian Splatting (H-2DGS), a three-stage pipeline that concentrates a robust 3DGS reconstruction, fuses its depth into a shared truncated signed distance field (TSDF) surface, and represents that surface with curved, surface-oriented Gaussian primitives. Separate height, color, and opacity bases provide local geometry and appearance, while a neighbor-transition objective coordinates overlapping patches. The explicit surface further supports postoperative alignment: MRI-derived depth deforms the reconstruction, and tracked-color supervision with alternating optimization preserves its appearance. Across five real neurosurgical cases, H-2DGS gives low multi-view depth inconsistency, a compact 7.97MB representation, and interactive rendering at 29--42ms per frame. The resulting sharp, deformable surface integrates clearly with tractography and MRI anatomy, including a postoperative resection cavity.

Bifurcations from Weight Space: JEPAWG on Parametric Dynamical Systems
Authors: Tobias Göbel, Miranda C. N. Cheng
Venue: NeurIPS 2026
First: 2026-10-22T07:22:44+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-22T07:22:44+00:00
Comments: 13+9 pages, 5+8 figures, 3+4 tables. A version of this work appears at the NeurIPS 2026 Workshop on Neural Network Artifacts as a New Data Modality
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Trained network weights are increasingly treated as data, but there is rarely ground truth about what a population of models should encode, which hampers systematic validation of weight-space methods. This makes parametric dynamical systems a natural test-bed: the dynamics change qualitatively at submanifolds of parameter space that are, in well-studied families, known analytically or numerically. Fine-tuning a forecasting network at each point of a parameter grid yields a model zoo indexed by a physical parameter, against which weight-space diagnostics can be checked. We build such model zoos for four dynamical systems and analyse them with JEPAWG, a recently proposed joint-embedding hypernetwork built around a learned parameter--weight representation. It was first introduced for neural samplers for lattice field theories, where its latent geometry located the phase transition and estimated the 2D Ising critical exponent. Applied to dynamical systems, JEPAWG is competitive on forecasting with a hypernetwork trained directly for that task, despite being supervised on the parameter--weight correspondence rather than on forecasts. Beyond forecasting, the norm of the parameter-derivative of the decoded weights locates Hopf points and curves, tracks a period-doubling cascade closely enough to give a qualitative estimate of the Feigenbaum constant, and detects a global bifurcation, which has no eigenvalue signature and is therefore invisible to linearisation. As such, JEPAWG provides interpretable insight into the dynamics that is not available from other hypernetworks. Together, these results indicate that JEPAWG is a workable instrument for reading physical structure off a population of trained networks.
Summary / 总结
Trained network weights are increasingly treated as data, but there is rarely ground truth about what a population of models should encode, which hampers systematic validation of weight-space methods.
WovenAnchor Matcher: Specialized Intra- and Inter-Image Context Modeling for Feature Matching
Authors: Zhiyang Li, Ruijiang Jin, Thibaut Klenke, Yusuke Sekikawa, Nakamasa Inoue

Abstract
Semi-dense local feature matching commonly aggregates contextual information before coarse-to-fine correspondence estimation. However, intra-image and inter-image aggregation serve different roles: the former should propagate spatial evidence within each image while retaining structured two-dimensional dependencies, whereas the latter should gather information from plausible corresponding regions while limiting noise from unrelated locations. We propose WovenAnchor Matcher (WAM), a semi-dense matching framework based on role-specialized context aggregation. WAM introduces two complementary operators. WovenMamba performs intra-image aggregation through horizontal-then-vertical state-space propagation, allowing vertical updates to operate on horizontally contextualized features and thereby encouraging a structured two-dimensional receptive-field bias. AnchorCrossAttention performs inter-image aggregation by using cross-attention to gather context from plausible corresponding regions across the image pair. It performs this exchange over local representative anchors that aggregate feature evidence within each window. On MegaDepth, WAM achieves 66.1 pose AUC@5$^\circ$, improving on the JamMa baseline by 2.0 AUC points under the same evaluation setup. Ablations show that removing or replacing either specialized component reduces accuracy, providing evidence that intra-image propagation and inter-image retrieval benefit from role-specific aggregation designs. Code and pretrained weights are available at https://github.com/mmaiLab/WovenAnchorMatcher.

DeXplicit: Making Physical State Explicit in Dexterous Manipulation World Models

Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2 · Project1
Abstract
Before acting on an object, a robot needs to anticipate how its hand motion will change the physical world and what the resulting scene will look like. Existing approaches typically entangle action-conditioned dynamics, camera motion, and visual synthesis within a video generation model, or represent dynamics as point trajectories without explicit object structure. We introduce DeXplicit, a dexterous manipulation world model that instead predicts how the world evolves explicitly in metric 3D before rendering its visual consequences. Given an initial scene and a future hand trajectory, a lightweight 3D dynamics network predicts object-centric motion and deformation, providing structured and geometrically grounded future world states. These predictions then serve as a strong scaffold for video generation: appearance is transported through the predicted 3D motion and projected into future views, allowing the renderer to focus on completing uncertain and newly visible content rather than implicitly learning world dynamics from pixels. This explicit-3D-first decomposition also enables efficient causal generation, requiring only two denoising steps per block while conditioning on generated history. Across HOT3D, OakInk2, and PhysTwin, DeXplicit reduces moved-point error by 27-40% relative to PointWorld and improves object fidelity IoU+ by 47% on HOT3D under a shared renderer. Our design with two-step causal generation achieves a 57x speedup from our 14B bi-directional configuration in video synthesis.

Abstract
Class labels offer compact supervision for describing cultural-heritage images when expert text annotations are scarce. Abstract
Deep neural networks often struggle to provide reliable confidence estimates, making post-hoc calibration essential for reliable decision-making. Existing post-hoc methods largely rely on score-based transformations, which improve marginal calibration but often fail to capture spatially varying miscalibration in local regions of the learned feature space. To address this gap, we propose Local Adaptive Probability Estimation (LAPE), a post-hoc residual calibration framework. Specifically, LAPE treats the global calibrator output as a stable anchor and refines the individual prediction by leveraging non-parametric local evidence from neighboring calibration samples. As a plug-and-play refinement module, LAPE effectively utilizes the information about feature-space geometry and can reuse the calibration data that are used to construct the global calibrator. Furthermore, we extend LAPE to the setting with multi-view inputs through Cascade Feature Augmentation Fusion (CFAF). Extensive experiments demonstrate that the proposed methods can improve the calibration quality of various existing baselines and remain efficient and robust across different settings.

Asking Less, Adapting Better: Feedback-Efficient Test-Time Adaptation for Vision-and-Language Navigation
Authors: Zixu Wang, Huaquan Zhang, Shoukai Xu, Jinwu Hu, Zhiquan Wen, Mingkui Tan
First: 2026-10-22T03:05:25+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-22T03:05:25+00:00
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Vision-and-Language Navigation (VLN) requires agents to follow instructions and navigate to target locations. However, current VLN agents often suffer severe performance degradation in unseen environments due to distribution shifts. Test-Time Adaptation (TTA) offers a promising paradigm to bridge this gap, yet existing methods that rely on unsupervised entropy minimization can propagate errors through the trajectory, causing cascading failures in navigation. Recent methods mitigate this by incorporating human feedback, but they apply trajectory-level supervision uniformly across all steps without localizing the actions is truly informative, leading to inefficient feedback utilization and mismatched optimization granularity. To address these limitations, we propose FETA, a Feedback-Efficient Test-Time Adaptation method that improves both feedback efficiency and optimization reliability. To reduce unnecessary human interventions, we introduce a hierarchical sample selection strategy to localize informative samples. Specifically, we first filter trajectories whose normalized policy entropy exhibits large fluctuations. Within each selected trajectory, we further select the critical action via an entropy-gradient saliency criterion and query a single binary feedback on its correctness. A discrepancy-aware entropy optimization objective then updates the policy only at the queried step, with adaptive scaling that assigns larger relative weights to samples with greater prediction-feedback discrepancy. Extensive experiments across multiple benchmarks demonstrate that FETA achieves state-of-the-art performance with fewer human interventions, while remaining robust under noisy feedback and continual adaptation settings.

Abstract
Zero-shot cross-lingual voice cloning often carries the reference speaker's accent into the target language. Abstract
Real-world data often exhibits cluster structure at multiple resolutions, yet existing methods typically learn a single partition or construct an explicit hierarchy. Inspired by Matryoshka Representation Learning, we introduce NEST, an unsupervised framework that encodes coarse-to-fine structure within a single embedding. NEST jointly optimizes clustering and separation across nested prefix subspaces, enabling progressively finer distinctions as embedding dimensionality increases, with rank and within-cluster regularization that keep the embedding from collapsing. By making clustering granularity an intrinsic property of the representation, NEST provides a unified approach to multiscale structure discovery.

Abstract
Reproducing a published 3DGS paper takes weeks of expert engineering before any extension can be proposed. Prior autonomous-research systems read papers, draft hypotheses, and write code, but are limited by short fixed templates, low executability, and weak traceability, and none has been demonstrated end-to-end in a live computer-vision subfield with intense activity. On PSNR-targeted runs, SplatLab's manuscripts beat their reproduced targets by +0.18 to +3.78 dB, including +3.78 dB on astrophotographic nebula rendering, for which no prior 3DGS method exists. The research cycle compresses from months of graduate-student time to days of mostly autonomous compute. Code, all manuscripts, experiment ledgers, and the cross-run paper store are available on the project page.

Abstract
Recent work in additive manufacturing and neural inverse design enables the creation of complex three-dimensional structures to manipulate physical fields, yet these designs must also adhere to discrete, non-differentiable fabrication constraints. Current methods limit the design space by imposing differentiable soft manufacturability loss penalties, or by fixing structures through post-processing, causing a discrepancy between the geometry optimized by the physics model and the final fabricated result. Experiments show manufacturable acoustic structures with enhanced focal accuracy and over density-based and phase-based baselines, demonstrating a generalized methodology for neural design through non-differentiable manufacturing constraints and differentiable physics.

Abstract
The explore/exploit trade-off is the core problem of reinforcement learning (RL). We show that a minimal foraging swarm approaches its optimum - the ideal-free / Nash allocation of a multi-agent resource-sharing game (an equilibrium, not a return maximum) - with no learner and no reward signal. Agents explore by heavy-tailed (Levy) flights, recruit neighbours to a discovery through a threshold cascade on their proximity network, and disperse as a patch crowds; this cascade cycle balances exploration and exploitation and drives foragers toward the ideal free distribution. The swarm attains the shape of this optimum (committed foragers track richness with slope 0.94) but not its exact equilibrium - it stays measurably exploitable - reaching it dynamically (~150 steps) through the coupling of recruitment to movement: remove the coupling and matching collapses (0.94 -> 0.08). The RL sits at the level of the problem and the allocation reached, not the micro-rule: no step evaluates payoff (reward-free by construction, though the crowd signal is informationally tied to payoff), and the swarm reaches the allocation without learning it (quality plateaus and re-settles after resources move, no cumulative gain). Against a trained baseline the comparison is two-sided: in an easier, disembodied game a learner is more precise, while in the same embodied environment the reward-free swarm reaches ~65% of a validated oracle ceiling at zero training and leads until trained PPO and centralised-critic (CTDE) learners overtake it (~74-75%, 16 seeds, all above the swarm mean) after ~4,000 of 15,000 training steps - its advantage is in budget, not asymptote. In this one setting we read it not as beating RL but as a strong zero-shot, reward-free operating point: a demonstration that the self-organizing physics of a minimal swarm can carry a collective close to the optimum RL seeks, with none of RL's machinery.
