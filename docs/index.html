Abstract
Structured state-space models (SSMs) achieve linear-time inference and logarithmic-time parallel training via associative scans, yet homogeneous diagonal transitions generate solvable semigroups unable to track non-solvable group words or 3D rigid-body manifolds. We develop a theoretical and empirical framework for Non-Commutative Lie-Algebraic State-Space Models (Lie-SSMs), parameterizing recurrent updates on Lie algebra tangent spaces via the matrix exponential. First, via an o-minimal multi-rate cylindrical flag collar, we establish an unconditional single-layer finite-division criterion for contracting $SO(3)$ and $SU(2)$ heads, proving odd-prime Sylow abelianity, odd-$p$-core centrality $[u^2,x]=e$, exponent-30 commutator laws ($[u^{30},v^{30}]=e$ in $SO(3)^m$ and $[u^{30},v^{30}]\in Z(G),[u^{30},v^{30}]^2=e$ in $SU(2)^m,SO(4)^m$, excluding $S_5$), and sharp threshold $d^*(S_5)=d^*(A_6)=5$ in $SO(5)$. Second, Depth-2 semidirect and Schreier $2$-cocycle cascades resolve $S_5$, $2I\cong SL_2(\mathbb{F}_5)$, and $\mathrm{Heis}(\mathbb{F}_p)$ in $\mathcal{O}(\log T)$ depth, whereas unnormalized contracting coupling collapses exponentially without Directional RMSNorm. Third, we characterize the $O(\varepsilon^{c-1})$ Chen--Magnus--Hall--Witt commutator saddle and $1/|[G,G]|$ coset-centroid dilution, proving $\varepsilon=0$ symmetry breaking and local Polyak--Łojasiewicz convergence ($θ_{\max}<π$), solving $S_5$ ($99.90\%/99.40\%$ at $T=128$) and $D_{16}$ ($100.00\%$) via dense prefix loss and factored readouts. Across $Q_8,A_5,S_5,D_{16}$, Dyck-2, $SE_2(3)$ Left-Jacobian odometry, GPU scans ($26.03\times$ speedup), and three 3D IMU cohorts (UCI HAR, HAPT-PT, REALDISP), Depth-2 Lie-$SO(3)$ cascades reach $95.69\%\pm 0.14\%$ on HAR, $87.16\%\pm 0.64\%$ ($86.23\%\pm 0.64\%$ uniform $SO(3)$) on HAPT-PT, and $84.23\%\pm 1.00\%$ under displacement on REALDISP.

SeeRF: Learning to See Beyond Fields of View via Generative Image Registration and Fusion
Authors: Xunpeng Yi, Yibing Zhang, Qinglong Yan, Yifan Lu, Han Xu, Jiayi Ma
First: 2026-10-26T15:46:35+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-26T15:46:35+00:00
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2 · Code3
Abstract
Due to the inherent discrepancies in imaging viewpoints and field-of-view mismatches among multi-modal sensors, existing fusion methods have long struggled to achieve complete scene-level information integration beyond the limitation in sensor fields of view. Consequently, the generated fusion results often contain modality-specific regions and fusion artifacts, limiting the comprehensive representation of scene structures and semantic information. To address this challenge, we propose a generative registration-fusion framework, termed SeeRF. Specifically, it first introduces a deformation generation strategy based on the diffeomorphic stationary velocity field, combined with visual foundation model-guided feature extraction and a coarse-to-fine progressive multi-modal image registration module, to achieve accurate cross-modal flow estimation and generate valid field-of-view masks. Subsequently, the registration module is tightly coupled with the generative diffusion module for multi-modal completion and fusion, where the cross-modal control encoding module and the control residual feature encoder-decoder mechanism provide effective guidance. By leveraging the powerful prior modeling capability of generative diffusion models, the proposed framework overcomes the limitations imposed by shared fields of view and enables effective completion and natural fusion of non-overlapping field-of-view regions. Extensive experiments on multiple public datasets and real-world scenarios demonstrate that the proposed method achieves superior performance, validating its effectiveness, superiority, and robustness. The code will be available at https://github.com/XunpengYi/SeeRF.

Synthetic Deformable Tubular Benchmark: A mechanically annotated dataset for evaluating deformable 3D reconstruction
Authors: Íñigo Cirauqui-Viloria

Comments: 34 pages, including 5 pages of Supplementary Methods; 10 main-text figures and 1 supplementary figure

Abstract
Evaluating deformable 3D reconstruction requires ground truth linking camera motion, surface deformation, material correspondence and image appearance. We introduce the Synthetic Deformable Tubular Benchmark, generated with LumenMorph, an open-source toolkit for creating, annotating and evaluating tubular scenes. The release contains 5,580 RGB images. Its baseline crosses three surface-motion conditions with moving and stationary cameras in six scene families; matched appearance variants cover only moving-camera finite-element actions in four families. Documented public and evaluation interfaces expose camera poses, geometry, depth, deformation fields, persistent material correspondences and discrete finite-element records. Persistent triangle identities and material weights let users track the same surface location across frames and query its three-dimensional trajectory. Separately implemented geometric and mechanical checks assess annotation consistency with the generating models. Structure-from-motion, optical-flow and supplied-geometry displacement-interpolation studies demonstrate how the resource distinguishes registration coverage from geometric accuracy, exposes camera- and surface-motion effects, and tests the benefits and mismatch of mechanical priors. Public readers, evaluation contracts and the configurable generator support reuse and extension. Data integrity, fixed-environment repeatability and retained-prediction score replay have distinct verification scopes. The benchmark provides a controlled synthetic reference without claiming anatomical or clinical realism.

Generative Structure from Motion with Native 3D Diffusion
Authors: Haobin Duan, Binbin Huang, Mochu Xiang, Yiqun Zhao, Zibo Zhao, Yi Ma, Shenghua Gao

Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2 · Project1
Abstract
Recovering an object's 3D structure and camera motion from a few uncalibrated views is a fundamental problem in computer vision. Classical structure from motion methods recover 3D geometry consistent with the input images but cannot recover unseen regions; native generation methods produce plausible 3D objects which are related but not necessarily faithful to the input images. Methods simply concatenating reconstruction and generation allow error to cascade across the two stages, instead of producing a 3D object consistent with both the distribution and the input. We propose GenSfM, which integrates image-based 3D reconstruction with a native 3D diffusion model. In particular, we use a multi-modal diffusion transformer to jointly denoise the shape latent and per-view pose latents under shared self-attention, grounding generated geometry in the observed views while using object-level priors to infer unobserved regions. A compact 3D-native UV-volume tokenization keeps the joint sequence small as views are added, and a pose-aware stage refines geometry and texture by sampling per-voxel image features at the recovered camera poses. On Toys4k and GSO, for $4$ uncalibrated views, GenSfM improves novel-view PSNR by $3.2$-$4.5$ dB and reduces median Chamfer distance by $2.8$-$3.8\times$ over a strong native-3D generation baseline, while recovering input-view poses over $96\%$ Acc@$5^\circ$ without any external pose-estimation backbone, post-hoc optimization, or known intrinsics. Authors: Renjie Wu, Hongdong Li, Jose M. Alvarez, Miaomiao Liu
Venue: CVPR 2026
First: 2026-03-30T06:09:37+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-26T14:19:45+00:00
Comments: Accepted to CVPR 2026. Project Page: https://github.com/RyansLearning/4DSurf
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2 · Code3
Abstract
This paper addresses the problem of dynamic scene surface reconstruction using Gaussian Splatting (GS), aiming to recover temporally consistent geometry. While existing GS-based dynamic surface reconstruction methods can yield superior reconstruction, they are typically limited to either a single object or objects with only small deformations, struggling to maintain temporally consistent surface reconstruction of large deformations over time. We propose ``\textit{4DSurf}'', a novel and unified framework for generic dynamic surface reconstruction that does not require specifying the number or types of objects in the scene, can handle large surface deformations and temporal inconsistency in reconstruction. The key innovation of our framework is the introduction of Gaussian deformations induced Signed Distance Function Flow Regularization that constrains the motion of Gaussians to align with the evolving surface. To handle large deformations, we introduce an Overlapping Segment Partitioning strategy that divides the sequence into overlapping segments with small deformations and incrementally passes geometric information across segments through the shared overlapping timestep. Experiments on two challenging dynamic scene datasets, Hi4D and CMU Panoptic, demonstrate that our method outperforms state-of-the-art surface reconstruction methods by 49\% and 19\% in Chamfer distance, respectively, and achieves superior temporal consistency under sparse-view settings.

Abstract
Deploying large vision-language models (LVLMs) on resource-constrained embodied agents for 3D visual question answering necessitates cloud-edge collaboration. However, offloading massive visual data to the cloud over bandwidth-limited and noisy channels introduces severe latency and transmission distortion. To address this, we propose TP-JSCC, an efficient token pruning and joint source-channel coding method for LVLMs-based 3D-VQA in Cloud-Edge Collaboration. First, we develop a Local and Global Semantics-based Hybrid Token Pruning (LGS-HTP) module to significantly reduce the visual token count. By decoupling foreground-background tokens and jointly selecting local and global semantics, LGS-HTP effectively reduces redundancy while preserving reasoning-critical tokens. Second, we introduce a lightweight Token Joint Source-Channel Coding (T-JSCC) module to further compress the pruned tokens and ensure their robust transmission over error-prone channels. By employing lightweight cascaded linear layers and Signal-to-Noise Ratio (SNR)-adaptive mechanism, this module adapts to fluctuating channel conditions while maintaining minimal computational overhead. Extensive experiments on ScanQA and SQA3D datasets indicate the exceptional efficiency and robustness of our approach. Specifically, on ScanQA validation set, the LGS-HTP module could achieve 90% pruning ratio while incurring less than a 1% CIDEr loss. Building on this, the T-JSCC further compresses the tokens by 18 times, resulting in an overall 180 times data volume reduction for robust transmission, while saving 90% in computational complexity for LVLM deployment. Our code is available at https://github.com/yuanhui0325/TP-JSCC.

SG-Splat: Stable Geometry Learning under Pose Uncertainty for Pose-Free 3D Gaussian Splatting
Authors: Xiaohan Lu, Jiaqi Zhang, Weijia Jiang, Siwei Ma
First: 2026-10-26T12:50:21+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-26T12:50:21+00:00
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) has become a promising representation for real-time novel view synthesis and 3D scene reconstruction. For practical deployment, recent studies have explored pose-free feed-forward 3DGS, which reconstructs Gaussian representations directly from unposed input images. However, these methods remain sensitive to residual pose errors, which perturb canonical-space Gaussian transformation, introducing noisy supervision for geometry and attribute learning. To address this problem, we present SG-Splat, a pose-free feed-forward 3DGS framework that improves robustness to pose uncertainty and stabilizes the learning of Gaussian primitives. SG-Splat decouples geometry estimation and Gaussian attribute prediction into two dedicated branches, reducing error coupling between pose-sensitive geometry and Gaussian attributes. We further introduce a coarse-to-fine refinement pipeline, consisting of Flow-Guided Pose Refinement (FGPR) and Residual-Guided Geometry Correction (RGC), to improve geometric alignment before Gaussian attribute reconstruction. Specifically, FGPR refines camera poses with flow-based correlation features and differentiable bundle adjustment. RGC then corrects initial point maps using depth residuals derived from FGPR, producing more reliable Gaussian centers. To stabilize training, we introduce a dual-path strategy that mixes ground-truth and predicted poses for canonical transformation. It provides clean supervision for Gaussian attributes while improving geometry robustness to residual pose errors. Extensive experiments on DL3DV and RealEstate10K show that SG-Splat achieves state-of-the-art performance in both novel view synthesis and pose estimation.

Abstract
Monocular occupancy prediction in indoor scenes aims to recover a scene's 3D geometry and semantics from a single RGB image. Abstract
Emotion recognition in artworks remains challenging because affective meaning is often subjective, implicit, and shaped by relationships between visual form and semantic associations. While vision-language models provide strong multimodal representations, direct image-to-emotion prediction may overlook explicit relations among formal, semantic, and affective cues that support emotional interpretation. This paper proposes Visual-Semantic Affect Graphs (VSAG), a graph-based framework that represents artworks through interpretable feature nodes, including color, brushstroke, composition, light-shadow, line quality, and extracted visual entities. The graph learns feature-emotion associations for dominant emotion prediction, with valence-arousal structure modeled through an optional hierarchical auxiliary affect module. We evaluate VSAG and VSAG-TopK against direct VLM inference using Qwen3-VL and InternVL3.5 on EmoArt-5k. Results show that direct VLMs provide useful but modest baselines, while graph-based models improve dominant emotion prediction. In particular, the hierarchical valence-arousal variant of VSAG achieves the best overall performance, improving accuracy, macro-F1, and weighted-F1 over both direct VLM inference and the emotion-only VSAG baseline. Per-class results indicate that graph-based models and VLMs capture partly complementary signals, while valence-arousal structure is most useful when integrated as a coarse-to-fine affect hierarchy rather than as a simple post-hoc correction. These findings suggest that explicit visual-semantic structure can support both prediction and interpretation in artwork emotion recognition.

GaussianSpline: Adaptive Bézier Splines for Highly Dynamic 4D Gaussian Splatting
Authors: Tom Yachil, Andrei Sharf
First: 2026-10-26T11:14:09+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-26T11:14:09+00:00
Comments: 8 pages, 4 figures
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
We present GaussianSpline, a dynamic 4D Gaussian Splatting method that models per-Gaussian motion using adaptive, non-uniform piecewise cubic Bézier splines. Unlike approaches based on global deformations, fixed-order polynomials, or uniformly parameterized splines, GaussianSpline jointly learns the Bézier control points and the temporal partition of each Gaussian's trajectory. This adaptive representation allocates greater temporal capacity to highly dynamic regions while maintaining compact trajectories for near-static regions, enabling high-fidelity reconstruction of complex and rapidly changing motion. To jointly achieve globally smooth motion and local adaptivity, we enforce $G^1$ continuity between consecutive Bézier segments using a reflection-based constraint on their control points. This produces smooth, fully differentiable trajectories while preserving the flexibility of non-uniform temporal parameterization. Experiments on the Neural 3D Video benchmark, the Google Immersive dataset, as well as on synthetic scenes demonstrate that GaussianSpline substantially improves reconstruction quality in scenes with highly non-uniform and challenging dynamics, while maintaining competitive performance on general dynamic scenes compared with state-of-the-art 4D Gaussian Splatting methods.

Abstract
Spatial intelligence lets an agent perceive and act in the physical world, and many of its judgments must be made at interaction rates: which spatial relation holds, how far an object is, or where to place it. Vision-language models (VLMs) produce these answers by free-form text generation, paying for autoregressive decoding and answer parsing even though the output space is compact: one of a few options, a scalar, or a 2D point. Abstract
Recovering dynamic 3D scene geometry, appearance, and the physical motion states that govern scene evolution from multi-view videos is important yet challenging. A key difficulty is that appearance can be deceptive. Under rendering supervision alone, a model may fit the observed images by changing color or opacity to compensate for inaccurate deformation, rather than recovering the true evolving geometry and motion. This issue is especially severe in scenes with fast or locally complex motion, where inaccurate trajectories can still produce plausible images over the observed frames, but lead to unreliable reconstruction and discontinuous future prediction. In this paper, we propose PhyMo, a framework for learning evolving dynamics from multi-view videos. We introduce a Motion-Aware Trajectory Representation that drives the model to recover accurate motion from deformation, rather than relying on appearance shortcuts to explain observed motion. On top of this, we propose a Continuous Motion Evolution module with bidirectional kinematic continuity constraint to promote temporally consistent evolution of displacement, velocity, and acceleration. Together, these designs yield geometrically faithful interpolation and continuous motion extrapolation. Experiments on four dynamic scene benchmarks demonstrate state-of-the-art performance.

Abstract
Blendshape generation for 3D models has been extensively researched, but very few works address generating blendshapes for models with multiple disconnected parts. Factorized Gradients for Scalable Highly-expressive Parametric Diffeomorphisms
Authors: Amit Aflalo, Eran Treister, Chaim Baskin, Oren Freifeld
Venue: NeurIPS 2026
First: 2026-10-26T03:11:58+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-26T03:11:58+00:00
Comments: Accepted by NeurIPS 2026
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Diffeomorphisms provide a flexible, topology-preserving mathematical tool for modeling complex spatial or temporal deformations and are used in various scientific fields. However, making these deformations simultaneously highly expressive and computationally efficient remains challenging. Continuous Piecewise-Affine Based (CPAB) transformations (Freifeld et al., ICCV 2015; IEEE TPAMI 2017) offer an attractive solution by parameterizing a family of diffeomorphisms via continuous velocity fields that are piecewise affine with respect to a chosen tessellation of the domain. Importantly, $d=\dim(θ)$, the dimension of the parameter vector $θ$, depends on the fineness of the tessellation rather than on the data resolution. Despite this compact parameterization, CPAB optimization has long been hindered by the tight coupling between trajectory integration and gradient computation with respect to $θ$. We introduce FG-CPAB, a factorized-gradient formulation for scalable CPAB transformations. By separating integration from parameter-space projection, our method reduces gradient computation complexity from $\mathcal{O}(dTN)$ to $\mathcal{O}(TN+dC)$, where $T$ is the number of integration steps, $N$ is the number of transformed points, and $C$ is the number of tessellation cells. This reformulation yields speedups of up to four orders of magnitude (e.g., $\approx 10^4\times$ in 2D), substantial memory savings (e.g., $\approx 500\times$ reduction in peak GPU memory usage in 2D), and dramatically higher expressiveness. This makes fine-tessellation CPAB practical in 2D and 3D for the first time, enabling highly expressive parametric diffeomorphisms.

WAVE: Wavelet-Controlled Coarse-to-Fine Guidance for Depth Super-Resolution
Authors: Tayyab Nasir, Daochang Liu, Ajmal Mian
First: 2026-08-26T02:16:41+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-26T03:05:00+00:00
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Guided depth super-resolution (GDSR) typically extracts RGB guidance features through convolutional hierarchies, inheriting their fine-to-coarse bias. Thus, low-level spatial cues surface in early layers, leaving the deeper layers to suppress those that do not correspond to true depth boundaries, which risks artifacts and blurred edges. The same fine-to-coarse bias persists in semantics-based methods that consume low-level tokens early and global tokens late. We present WAVE, which introduces a multi-level discrete wavelet transform (ML-DWT) as an explicit and interpretable feature-control mechanism, enabling a coarse-to-fine reconstruction by consuming sub-bands and semantic tokens in reverse of their generation order. WAVE further exploits these sub-bands to treat high- and low-frequency content separately, isolating the misleading RGB color and texture cues before any feature is learned so that their admission can be controlled explicitly, offering an intuitive alternative to the suppression learned implicitly by an opaque network. WAVE separates structure and detail reconstruction into dedicated modules that: i) model interactions within and across wavelet sub-bands, depth features, and semantic priors, ii) apply semantic gating to the high-frequency bands, and iii) fuse modalities through an invertible coupling mechanism that prevents collapse onto a single modality. Extensive experiments across multiple benchmarks demonstrate that WAVE matches or outperforms existing methods, with the largest gains at high upsampling factors, where low-resolution depth contains the least structure.

Abstract

Abstract
The explore-exploit trade-off is the core problem of reinforcement learning (RL). In its multi-agent, resource-sharing form the optimum is not a scalar-return maximum but an equilibrium allocation -- the ideal-free distribution (IFD), the Nash equilibrium of a congestion game. We show that a minimal foraging swarm reaches the shape of this equilibrium as a self-organized attractor -- foragers in proportion to resource richness (matching slope 0.94) -- with no learner, no trained parameters, and no payoff or reward read anywhere in its dynamics. Agents explore by heavy-tailed (Levy) flights, recruit neighbours to a discovery through a threshold cascade on their proximity network, and disperse as a patch crowds; removing the coupling of recruitment to movement collapses the matching (slope 0.08). The reinforcement learning is at the level of the problem and the allocation reached, not the micro-rule. The swarm reaches this equilibrium rather than learning it, attaining its shape but not the exact equilibrium: it stays measurably exploitable (Nash-gap 0.58 versus ~0.01 for a converged learner). Against PPO learners (feedforward, centralized-critic, and graph message-passing) trained in the same sensing-limited environment, the reward-free swarm at zero training matches the IFD gradient more faithfully than any learner (slope ~1 vs 0.69-0.91) and ties the best learner's harvest where clustered (both plateau at ~0.80 of an oracle ceiling), while the learner collects more where resource is scarce (0.33 vs 0.22) and holds tighter per-capita intake. Learning thus buys precision and transfer, not a better allocation; neither message passing nor a centralized critic exceeds it at this budget. We read this not as beating RL, but as evidence that a minimal swarm's physics reaches the equilibrium RL seeks without a learner, a reward, or a trained parameter. A companion paper develops the mechanism.
