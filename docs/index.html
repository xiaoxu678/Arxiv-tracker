Transport-Free Lower Bounds for Ollivier--Ricci Curvature in Higher-Order Networks: Scope, Tightness, and Computational Applications
Authors: Wonwoo Kang, Heehyun Park
First: 2024-05-22T02:44:46+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-30T15:27:49+00:00
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
We study how the Bauer--Jost--Liu curvature bound applies to hypergraph Ollivier--Ricci curvature (ORC) and how its accuracy depends on local mass and geometry. An explicit weighted-graph representation shows how the graph theorem applies. A transport construction transfers the bounds to monotone pairwise hyperedge aggregations. We analyze endpoint cancellation under laziness, quantify the exact improvement over the earlier mixture bound, and characterize the extremal geometry at fixed endpoint and overlap statistics. Further results quantify worst-case slack, stability, and exactness on isolated hyperedges. A capacity-constrained short-detour refinement uses additional geometric information and improves monotonically as the query budget increases. As a computational application, a threshold cascade preserves exact transport decisions. Numerical verification and public-hypergraph experiments examine tightness, walk dependence, and aggregation, including evaluations on 42,940 finite pairwise comparisons and 64,620 public-data instances. Together, the results specify the scope, equality mechanisms, and computational uses of BJL-type hypergraph bounds.

Abstract
Accurate BEV map prediction is a critical component of autonomous driving systems, enabling downstream planning and control. Recent approaches such as Mask2Map achieve strong performance by predicting vectorized, instance-level map elements directly from multi-view images. However, existing BEV map prediction models operate largely independently of object-level scene understanding, despite the fact that traffic participants frequently occlude or implicitly encode information about road geometry. In this work, we propose a multitask architecture that jointly predicts BEV maps and 3D objects, explicitly enabling information exchange between the two tasks. Building upon Mask2Map, we introduce an object detection branch and incorporate cross-task feature fusion at both the BEV feature level and the instance-query level via deformable attention and cross-attention mechanisms. This design allows map instances to leverage object context while remaining robust to occlusions caused by traffic participants. Experimental results on the nuScenes and Argoverse 2 datasets demonstrate that our approach consistently improves BEV map prediction accuracy, with particularly significant gains of 21% mAP in scenes containing heavy object occlusion.

Peeling the Matryoshka: Extracting Ontological Structure from Retrieval Embeddings
Authors: Eunchan Kim, Inwhee Joe
Venue: EMNLP 2026
First: 2026-10-30T14:19:53+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-30T14:19:53+00:00
Comments: Accepted to EMNLP 2026 (Main Conference). 13 pages, 6 figures, 7 tables. Code: https://github.com/Eunchan24/PEEL
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2 · Code3
Abstract
Taxonomy construction is essential for knowledge-intensive NLP yet remains costly: clustering-based methods (i) need careful per-corpus tuning and yield unstable hierarchies, while LLM-based pipelines (ii) depend on expensive task-specific training that does not transfer across domains. We report an empirical finding that bears on both: in Matryoshka Representation Learning (MRL) embeddings, where each leading prefix is itself a usable, coarser vector, prefix length aligns with the coarse-to-fine granularity of gold taxonomies; shuffled- and reverse-dimension controls isolate the alignment to the learned early prefix dimensions. The pattern holds across three architecturally distinct MRL models. To test whether this regularity is usable, we introduce PEEL (Peeling Embeddings to Extract Latent ontologies): a minimal probe over these frozen embeddings, trained once with an order-embedding objective that represents hierarchy as vector containment; a minimum spanning arborescence then decodes taxonomic DAGs. Using only a concept vocabulary (no documents), PEEL recovers taxonomic DAGs with high structural fidelity on two OLLM benchmarks, transferring to a new domain with no target-edge supervision. The end-to-end OLLM pipeline, which jointly discovers concepts and induces structure from document corpora, is reported as a cross-task reference. Pre-trained retrieval embeddings encode a decodable granularity gradient; a minimal probe renders it as explicit parent-child (is-a) structure.

Authors: Daoyi Gao, Xinyi Zhang, Alexey Bokhovkin, Lei Li, Angela Dai

Abstract
High-resolution 3D indoor scene generation is crucial for immersive applications such as virtual reality, spatial computing, and robotics simulation. We introduce VoxLution, a coarse-to-fine transformer-based approach that generates high-fidelity indoor scenes with rich details, overcoming the overly smooth geometry typical of existing generative models. First, we generate a low-resolution spatial layout; then, based on this structural information, we progressively refine the scene geometry locally in chunks. Key to our approach is formulating 3D super-resolution as a sequence modeling problem over multi-resolution sparse voxel grids. To achieve this, we introduce a training-free, resolution-invariant tokenization scheme that compactly encodes the geometric information of eight octants into a single decimal number. We formulate the transformer sequence to include multiple resolutions, allowing the coarse layout to guide the generation of fine local details. To ensure coherence across local chunks, we incorporate adjacent chunk boundary information directly into the transformer sequencing. VoxLution achieves scene generation at a finer voxel resolution than existing baselines, demonstrating state-of-the-art performance on standard benchmarks with 59.6% lower FPD and 51.1% lower FID on 3D-FRONT, and 70.5% lower FPD and 73.8% lower FID on Scannet++.

BDNet: Fast-slow thinking is all you need
Authors: Chenzhi Zheng, Zhuoran Zheng
First: 2026-10-30T09:41:01+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-30T09:41:01+00:00
Comments: 7 pages, 2 figures, this manuscript is an early draft
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
We present BDNet, a hierarchical image-classification backbone built around fast image features and slow statistical references. Its baseline deformation operator (BDO) transforms the deviation of a feature from a stage-specific reference through a low-rank projection and a learned channel scale. Rather than computing pairwise token similarity, the branch operates directly on baseline-relative residuals with complexity linear in the token count. Each of four reference buffers accumulates detached stage statistics during training and remains frozen at inference. A four-stage pyramid with depths $(2,2,6,2)$ and widths $(96,192,384,768)$ yields a 20.70M-parameter classifier at $224\times224$ resolution. We organize an ImageNet-1K study around clean classification, eight controlled ablations, and Gaussian-noise, contrast, and brightness perturbations. The central hypothesis is that a slow reference can improve the robustness of a compact representation while retaining a simple tensor implementation. We distinguish this training-time statistical mechanism from input-dependent attention and make its linear inference behavior explicit.

RetryRoute: Retry-Aware Model Routing for Agentic Image Editing Services
Authors: Hanshuai Cui, Zhiqing Tang, Zhi Yao, Jun Luo, Weijia Jia
First: 2026-10-30T09:12:21+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-30T09:12:21+00:00
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Web agents increasingly edit images by calling open editing models in a critique-and-retry loop, and these models differ in cost by more than an order of magnitude. After every rejected edit, a service must decide whether to retry the same model, escalate to another or give up. Existing routers pick one model per request, and cascades escalate in a fixed order. However, both treat a retry as an independent draw. We identify sticky failures, meaning that a retry after a failure succeeds only 17-53% as often as a first try across six open models, three benchmarks and two judges. Based on this finding, we propose RetryRoute, a retry-aware router that estimates each model's chance of success given the request's failure history. It plans retries, escalations and abandonment over failure states and adds live queue waits to the cost, so the same plan becomes load-aware dispatch. At the main budget, RetryRoute's success under the in-loop judge exceeds the strongest of eleven prior baselines by 2.8 points on average over eight groups. On a GPU serving testbed it raises the share of edits delivered within the service-level objective by 5.0-6.3 points over the best prior baseline at every load.

Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2 · Code3 · Project1
Abstract
GUI grounding maps natural-language instructions to click locations and is essential for reliable GUI agents. The task remains difficult on high-resolution, densely populated interfaces because a vision-language model (VLM) may recognize a requested control without locating it precisely enough for interaction. Most existing methods provide various forms of localization assistance, but still rely on a direct click prediction, allowing visual ambiguity or an inaccurate initial estimate to propagate to the final result. In this paper, we introduce GUI-Lens, a coarse-to-fine grounding framework that allows a general-purpose VLM to determine the target through active visual observations. Specifically, GUI-Lens extracts OCR text and detected UI components from the screenshot and presents their positions as coordinate references. Using the instruction, the current view, and these references, the VLM selects the region and scale of the next view, which is cropped and enlarged to provide finer visual details. This process continues over successively focused views until the target is determined. Proposed crops and clicks are checked against the instruction throughout the process, and the final local position is mapped back to the original screen coordinates. Experiments on five GUI grounding benchmarks and six general-purpose base models show that GUI-Lens consistently improves grounding accuracy, achieving up to 9.3 times the accuracy of direct grounding on ScreenSpot-Pro.

Abstract

TensorSSM: Memorizing Spatial Position in Dynamic State Space for Tensor Core Generation

Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
High-dimensional tensors are ubiquitous in real-world applications but lead to massive storage overhead. Functional tensor decomposition addresses this by employing neural networks to map element coordinates to tensor cores, significantly reducing the parameters for data compression. However, existing functional methods typically flatten high-order tensors into 1D sequences. This folding operation causes highly overlapping local coordinate sequences and struggles to capture complex cross-mode spatial dependencies, severely limiting their expressiveness on diverse datasets. To tackle these challenges, we propose TensorSSM, a novel Implicit Neural Representation (INR) for functional decomposition method that memorizes spatial positions within a dynamic State Space Model (SSM). Specifically, TensorSSM first decomposes mode-wise coordinates into sequences spit by K-ary rule, encoding spatial tokens from coarse to fine, which effectively captures spatial variations. Furthermore, we extend the SSM's state space to explicitly map cross-mode spatial dependencies, allowing inter-mode interactions to enhance the updates of each mode. By integrating cross-mode information from previous hidden states into the current state transitions, TensorSSM effectively expands tensor representation along two dimensions: the sequence dimension for coarse-to-fine spatial tokens, and the state dimension for cross-mode memorization. This design effectively integrates spatial information into the state space and the tensor core, substantially advancing the state adaptability and enhancing the expressiveness of the data compression. Extensive experiments demonstrate that TensorSSM achieves significantly high reconstruction accuracy and storage efficiency, exhibiting robust expressiveness across diverse tensor datasets without relying on strict spatial assumptions.

Abstract
Language models have proven to be capable of learning the intricate latent structures in language. However, the dynamics of how such models acquire the components of that structure remain poorly understood, leaving them open to the criticism that models just remix training data. In this work, we use the Alchemy benchmark as a controlled setting in which we investigate how models acquire the different latent structure components of the data. We train a small decoder-only transformer on three controlled task variants: 1) inferring missing steps from partial contextual information, 2) composing simple rules to solve multi-step sequences, and 3) decomposing complex multi-step examples to infer intermediate states. Abstract
