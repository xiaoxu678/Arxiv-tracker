PhySGA: A Physics-Space Grounded Agentic Framework for Generalizable and Interactive 3D Composition
Authors: Weichen Lyu, Chong Xia, Mingyu Dong, Long Xu, Shengjun Zhang, Yueqi Duan
First: 2026-10-14T17:47:24+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-14T17:47:24+00:00
Comments: Project Page: https://agenticei.github.io/PhySGA-page/
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2 · Project1
Abstract
3D object composition is pivotal to digital content creation and robot simulation, while also serving as a critical testbed for physical intelligence in foundation models. Previous methods either leverage large language models to directly predict object coordinates, or utilize generative diffusion priors to synthesize composite images for visual alignment, both of which rely on static priors in digital spaces. However, these methods consistently struggle with complex compositions such as precise hanging, gravity-driven settling and hidden containment, as these tasks require interactive construction processes involving global planning, fine-grained alignment, and physical simulation. To address these challenges, we propose PhySGA, a Physics-Space Grounded Agentic Framework for 3D composition via agent exploration and physical simulation, enabling the universal interactive assembly of rigid, articulated, and deformable objects. Directly applying vanilla LLMs or VLMs to this task proves insufficient, as current models inherently lack grounded physical understanding and interactive construction experience. To overcome this limitation, we design a collaborative feedforward-feedback architecture: an Assembly Agent focuses on geometric and kinematic reasoning for global step planning and coarse adjustment, while a Critic Agent reasons over contact dynamics for simulator state diagnosis and fine-grained closed-loop correction. Furthermore, Action Routers map semantic planning and analysis onto executable physical primitives within the simulator, while a task-specific Self-Evolving Skill Library distills historical trial-and-error feedback into reusable experiences, boosting success rates and minimizing exploration attempts. Extensive experiments within Isaac Sim demonstrate that PhySGA significantly surpasses existing methods in relation fidelity, physical plausibility, and visual quality.

Abstract
It has been difficult to accurately model the physics of the interaction between a robot and the objects it manipulates, including contact mechanics, coupled contact dynamics, deformations, stiffness and friction. We present RoboSimStudio (RSS), an interactive toolkit that makes choices of solver, contact model, and physical parameters explicit and editable, allowing both humans and coding agents to quickly create and iteratively refine simulations based on observed physical behavior. We implement RSS with the open Newton physics simulation system and describe experiments with human-guided real2sim using (1) visual evaluation, (2) force-feedback and (3) agentic Real2Sim2Real. Selective Growing Q-Networks (SGQN): An Analysis of Adaptive Action Grids in Decoupled Q-Learning
Authors: Parham Nooranbakht, Seyed Mahdi Basiri Azad, Joschka Bödecker
First: 2026-10-14T16:45:10+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-14T16:45:10+00:00
Comments: 19 pages, 6 figures, 6 tables. Under review at TMLR
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Value-based Q-learning for continuous control can discretise the action space, but the resolution of the discretisation trades early exploration against final precision. Growing Q-Networks (GQN) grow the resolution uniformly from coarse to fine. We introduce Selective Growing Q-Networks (SGQN), which keeps decoupled Q-learning and replaces uniform growth with an adaptive action grid: bins are added next to bins with high estimated Q-value, bins with low Q-value are pruned, and actions are chosen epsilon-greedily. On eight DeepMind Control Suite (DMControl) tasks with 2 to 12 action dimensions, SGQN has the highest mean final return among the compared methods on four, is within 1.1% of the best method on three more, and is 3.4% below Bang-Bang MPO on Walker Run, using on average 7.5 of 9 available bins per dimension. We then test whether the adaptive grid explains these results, and find that it does not. Growth activates every candidate bin and behaves like uniform growth, and removing pruning changes the return by at most 1.1%. Two other choices matter: starting from a coarse grid, since starting with all 9 bins produces collapsed seeds, and epsilon-greedy selection, since an annealed softmax lowers the return by 23% to 100%. SGQN is insensitive to its growth and pruning hyperparameters. It also differs from the GQN and DecQN baselines in other respects, notably one gradient update per environment step instead of one per eight, which we did not control for, so we do not attribute its advantage to a single component. We present SGQN as a competitive value-based method and as an analysis of which ingredients of a growing action grid matter.

MolHop: Scaffold-Hopping Molecular Design with Agentic Reinforcement Learning
Authors: Yanyi Su, Chenyan Wang, Yulei Gong, Yeyun Chen, Xiaohui Yang, Jun Cheng
Venue: ICLR 2027
First: 2026-10-14T10:11:10+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-14T10:11:10+00:00
Comments: Under review at ICLR 2027
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Molecular design is often formulated as an optimization problem over existing molecular spaces. However, molecular optimization and the discovery of structurally distinct replacement molecules represent two fundamentally different search problems. LLM-based molecular design agents typically perform the former, conducting local refinement within the neighborhood of existing structures to improve target properties through structural modifications. In contrast, the latter requires exploration of a broader chemical space to discover molecules with entirely different scaffolds while preserving the functional characteristics of reference molecules. We propose MolHop, an agentic framework for cross-scaffold molecular discovery that enables models to navigate and edit within a pre-organized chemical space. MolHop organizes approximately 1.6 million molecular scaffolds from commercial molecules into a coarse-to-fine hierarchical action space. The agent selects new exploration regions through scaffold navigation actions and adjusts candidate properties through molecular editing. The framework maintains historical trajectory memory to support long-horizon search processes. On drug molecule and commercial compound tasks, connecting Grok-4.6 and DeepSeek-Flash-V4.1 to the MolHop framework substantially improves their cross-scaffold joint success rates. We develop MolHop-9B through agentic reinforcement learning, and MolHop-9B outperforms generative baselines including GPT-5.6-sol, Claude-5-Opus, and Gemini-3.1-Pro, while generating candidate molecules with larger scaffold differences from the reference molecules. These results demonstrate that effective molecular discovery beyond the traditional optimization paradigm requires structured chemical and action spaces, enabling agents to explore new structural regions rather than merely refine existing molecules.

Tracking large chemical reaction networks and rare events by neural networks
Authors: Jiayu Weng, Xinyi Zhu, Jing Liu, Linyuan Lü, Pan Zhang, Ying Tang
First: 2025-12-11T05:55:44+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-14T09:28:20+00:00
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Chemical reaction networks are widely used to model stochastic dynamics in biophysical processes. Their exact probabilistic description via the chemical master equation becomes intractable for high-dimensional systems due to the exponential growth of the state space. This difficulty is further exacerbated when metastable transitions and rare events dominate the dynamics. Here, we present an advanced neural-network approach that effectively tackles large reaction networks and rare-event dynamics within a unified framework. Building on autoregressive representations of the full joint probability distribution, we achieve efficient training through second-order optimization methods, including natural gradient descent and the time-dependent variational principle. We leverage enhanced-sampling strategies that enable accurate resolution of low-probability regions governing rare transitions. We demonstrate the method on a multistable genetic toggle switch and a mitogen-activated protein kinase (MAPK) cascade consisting of 16 species and 35 reactions with dense coupling. We further quantify rare events in spatially extended Schlögl models in both one- and two-dimensional lattices, reaching system sizes with extremely large state spaces up to $3\times10^{15}$. These results highlight the potential of neural modeling as a flexible and scalable framework for studying high-dimensional chemical reaction networks and rare events.

Abstract

Across the different timesteps in the denoising chain, high-noise timesteps present distinct optimization and prediction challenges, which can increase the cost of real-world applications. Inspired by the varying stroke sizes used in human painting, in this work, we ask whether a similar stroke-size variation (i.e., rough stroke applied early) in diffusion can simplify existing image-generation algorithms. A finite-element-inspired bipartite graph learned simulator for manufacturability assessment in large-deformation sheet forming
Authors: Yingxue Zhao, Haoran Li, Haosu Zhou, Tobias Pfaff, Nan Li
First: 2026-05-14T17:12:44+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-13T23:27:46+00:00
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Explicit dynamic finite element (FE) simulations are widely used for large-deformation analysis but remain expensive for design-space exploration. In explicit FE analysis, nodal kinematics and element-level deformation measures evolve through a coupled node-element update process. An FE-inspired learned simulator can preserve this structure and learn nodal kinematics and elemental deformation on their native domains. Nodal displacement describes geometry evolution, while elemental thinning identifies local deformation and critical regions. Feeding both outputs back during rollout allows each field to inform subsequent updates of the other. This work proposes CAtt-BiGNN, a cross-attention-based bipartite graph neural network for coupled nodal-elemental learning. The bipartite graph encodes FE-guided state organisation and information flow without imposing equilibrium or constitutive constraints. Mesh nodes and elements are represented as distinct graph entities connected by directed node-element edges, enabling prediction of nodal displacement increments and elemental deformation on their native domains. An edge-aware cross-attention processor dynamically updates edge representations and weights messages using the receiving state and evolving local relations. For larger graphs, CAtt-BiUGNN uses separate nodal and elemental downsampling and upsampling to enhance long-range propagation while preserving their interaction across graph levels. The method is evaluated on dome-shaped cold-forming and corner-shaped hot-forming benchmarks. Comparisons with node-centred baselines, an FE-inspired hypergraph model, a Physics-Attention-based neural operator, a graph Transformer, and attention ablations show improved joint accuracy in nodal displacement and elemental thinning prediction during autoregressive rollout.

Abstract
Monocular 3D object detection methods increasingly rely on depth-guided transformers, but they operate entirely in the perspective image plane where spatial relationships are distorted by foreshortening. We propose MonoMIP, a dual-decoder framework that adds complementary 3D geometric reasoning through Bird's-Eye View (BEV) features constructed via Multi-Height Inverse Perspective Mapping. We perform IPM at K elevation planes spanning ground to rooftop, producing an ordered stack of height-conditioned metric BEV features that reduces above-ground misprojection and exposes depth-dependent appearance variation across planes. A BEV decoder with multi-scale deformable attention aggregates this multi-height evidence per query, while a depth-guided 3D decoder provides complementary perspective-space predictions. Without extra training data, MonoMIP attains 25.84% Moderate AP3D|R40 on KITTI val and 21.66% on the official test set for Car, outperforming recent published monocular methods on both the KITTI and Waymo benchmarks.

Abstract
Raw image intensities are a poor surrogate for similarity across images in correspondence problems. Further, while auxiliary segmentation labels can provide semantic identification and improve matching, they can assign only coarse identities to fine-grained anatomy and often cover only a subset of structures, particularly in whole-body settings. We present an approach that instead establishes correspondence using complementary representations of anatomy and demonstrate its utility for longitudinal whole-body PET/CT registration in the Learn2Reg 2026 PSMAReg Challenge. Our 3D foundation model, anatomix, is used to derive fine-grained, per-voxel semantic representations that are consistent across volumes, and we further employ off-the-shelf segmentation models to obtain coarse anatomical identities. Together, these representations drive a GPU-accelerated iterative registration solver (FireANTs) in a hybrid multistage framework: the first stage estimates alignment from dense multi-channel features, after which a second refines the deformation using both anatomix- and segmentation-based losses. Lastly, because registration should not introduce non-biological tumor volume changes across time, we develop lesion-specific component-wise volume preservation schemes. Without any task-specific training, our system won first place in the PSMAReg Challenge leaderboard. Code and interactive visualizations are available at http://www.neeldey.com/reps-for-reg.

Abstract
3D semantic occupancy prediction is crucial for autonomous driving, but camera-based methods degrade in low-light conditions and LiDAR-based methods are sensitive to adverse weather. 4D radar is more robust, yet its sparsity and the high cost of radar tensor processing limit its use. We propose Radar Geometric-Semantic Occupancy Prediction (RadarGSOcc), an efficient framework that directly processes 4D radar tensors by separately extracting geometric and semantic features, including Doppler information, with a deformable attention-based geometric refinement module as its key component. We also introduce the K-Radar 3DSemOcc benchmark, the first large-scale benchmark for 3D semantic occupancy prediction using 4D radar tensors, built on the K-Radar dataset with refined LiDAR-based annotations. To assess robustness without per-frame ground truth in adverse weather, we further propose a novel proxy metric, Environment-Based KL Divergence (EBKL), under which 4D radar proves a reliable and efficient modality for 3D semantic occupancy prediction. The dataset and code will be publicly available.

Abstract
Reinforcement learning (RL) has emerged as the primary paradigm for optimizing Large Language Model (LLM) agents, yet efficient critic-free algorithms (e.g., GRPO) suffer from a fundamental uniform credit assignment dilemma that broadcasts trajectory-level advantages equally to all actions. Abstract
A patient's pathology case spans tens of slides from distinct specimens and anatomical sites, each carrying non-redundant, site-specific evidence. Forming a patient-level conclusion therefore requires locating the slides relevant to a given question and integrating complementary findings across them. However, existing pathology agents analyze one slide at a time and describe morphology rather than measure it, leaving evidence acquisition across a patient's specimens and patient-level quantification underexplored. To address this gap, we propose PlanPath, an agentic framework for patient-level pathology visual question answering (VQA). PlanPath turns the question into an exploration plan that specifies the evidence required, routes to the slides likely to carry it, and drives coarse-to-fine acquisition from tissue architecture down to individual cells. Tissue- and cell-level tools supply the counts, proportions, and extents, which PlanPath aggregates across slides into a traceable answer. To enable evaluation, we build two patient-level VQA datasets for renal and gastric cancer, averaging 15 WSIs per patient, with pathologist-verified question-answer pairs derived from diagnostic reports. Experimental results show that PlanPath outperforms the strongest baseline by 9.4% and 12.1% in overall accuracy on the two datasets and further improves existing slide-level agents when plugged into our framework. Abstract
Grammatical Error Correction (GEC) and feedback play a vital role in supporting second language (L2) learners, educators, and examiners. While written GEC is well-established, spoken GEC (SGEC), aiming to provide feedback based on learners' speech, poses additional challenges due to disfluencies, transcription errors, and the lack of structured input. SGEC systems typically follow a cascaded pipeline consisting of Automatic Speech Recognition (ASR), disfluency detection, and GEC, making them vulnerable to error propagation across modules. This work examines an End-to-End (E2E) framework for SGEC and feedback generation, highlighting challenges and possible solutions when developing these systems. Cascaded, partial-cascaded and E2E architectures are compared, all built on the Whisper foundation model. A challenge for E2E systems is the scarcity of GEC labeled spoken data. To address this, an automatic pseudo-labeling framework is examined, increasing the training data from 77 to over 2500 hours. To improve the accuracy of the SGEC system, additional contextual information, exploiting the ASR output, is investigated. Candidate feedback of their mistakes is an essential step to improving performance. In E2E systems the SGEC output must be compared with an estimate of the fluent transcription to obtain the feedback. To improve the precision of this feedback, a novel reference alignment process is proposed that aims to remove hypothesised edits that results from fluent transcription errors. Finally, these approaches are combined with an edit confidence estimation approach, to exclude low-confidence edits. Experiments on the in-house Linguaskill (LNG) corpora and the publicly available Speak & Improve (S&I) corpus show that the proposed approaches significantly boost E2E SGEC performance.

Abstract
We forecast clot growth from sparse early measurements with incomplete biochemical information. Using 1,000 simulated trajectories, we compare inverse search, which weights candidate inputs by their agreement with early observations, with a direct multilayer perceptron (MLP) predicting future clot size. Inverse search is more accurate when tissue factor alone, or tissue factor and fibrinogen, are unknown. When all five inputs are unknown, direct prediction achieves 2.16% pooled relative $L_2$ forecast error, outperforming search with 8,192 candidates using a forward MLP (3.25%) or latent neural ordinary differential equation model (3.36%). An independent reaction cascade verifies candidate integration against tensor quadrature, with mean normalized forecast-integration error below $10^{-5}$ at 32,768 candidates. These results demonstrate accurate clot-growth forecasting without full biochemical identification and support selecting prediction strategies and additional measurements according to accuracy, reliability, and computational cost.

Authors: Tingjun Li, Yuxi Wang, Guoqiang Zhong, Zhaoxiang Zhang

Abstract
Dynamic 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) provides an efficient framework for dynamic scene reconstruction. Most existing approaches model scene dynamics with deterministic deformation fields, assuming each Gaussian follows a distinct geometric trajectory over time. This assumption breaks down underwater, where light scattering, refraction, marine snow and low visibility create substantial physical ambiguity in captured images. We present PUGS, a physics-guided uncertainty model for underwater dynamic 3DGS. PUGS learns inherent uncertainty induced by underwater image formation and leverages it as a physical signal for dynamic scene modeling. Rather than estimating a single deterministic displacement, it parameterizes each Gaussian's motion as a probability distribution with expected deformation and physical uncertainty. We adopt stochastic deformation optimization to sample Gaussian trajectories via local reparameterization, exploring multiple physically plausible solutions during training. We further propose uncertainty stochastic annealing and a dual-path self-consistency distillation objective, converting stochastic exploration to deterministic reconstruction while preserving temporal coherence. Extensive experiments on underwater dynamic datasets and physics-aware benchmarks demonstrate superior rendering quality, temporal consistency and reconstruction robustness. Our method achieves 1.16 dB PSNR gain over the strongest baseline on UVEB and 3.99 dB on DRUVA.

Abstract
