Dense4D: Pixel-Level 4D Human Correspondence
Authors: Rıza Alp Güler
First: 2026-10-15T17:52:14+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-15T17:52:14+00:00
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Reconstructing human motion in 4D requires identifying the same surface points through articulation, deformation and changes in visibility. We introduce Dense4D, to our knowledge the first feed-forward model dedicated to dense 4D correspondence across the body, clothing and hair from a set of monocular RGB images, without a body template. Trained specifically for human geometry, Dense4D jointly processes person-centred crops with known image-to-crop transforms to predict Human Point Maps (HPMs) and depth for each image. An HPM assigns each person pixel the 3D position of its observed surface point at a common reference time. These maps support amodal correspondence: points hidden in the reference image can be localised from observations in other frames. Matching HPM coordinates across crops establishes correspondence without predicting camera poses or large scene displacements; per-image depth then lifts the matches into 3D tracks. Experiments on real people demonstrate substantially higher tracking accuracy and closer agreement with scanned surfaces than the evaluated baselines. Points from different frames assemble into coherent human geometry in the reference pose, providing visible evidence of consistent dense correspondence through articulation and clothing deformation. Additional qualitative results, videos, and an interactive viewer are available at https://alpguler.com/dense4d.

AVC-Reward: Event-Centric Audio-Video Consistency Evaluation
Authors: Yuruo Wu, Kaihang Pan, Kai Shen, Jiacheng Li, Shuyuan Tu, Jisheng Dang, Li Tuoyu, Juncheng Li, Siliang Tang
First: 2026-10-15T17:51:56+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-15T17:51:56+00:00
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2 · Project1
Abstract
As joint audio-video generation models become more capable, they can generate audio-visual scenes exhibiting three forms of complexity: multi-event composition, multi-layer audio mixture, and asymmetric cross-modal relations. However, current evaluations largely reuse metrics from earlier simple and unidirectional tasks. They typically evaluate an audio-video clip as a whole and rely on simple predefined relations, making them unsuitable for these increasingly complex scenes. We propose an event-centric evaluation framework that disentangles audio and visual events at the event level, analyzes each event independently, cross-validates relations across modalities, and aggregates the judgments into a coarse-to-fine, three-level consistency metric. The metric assesses global narrative harmony, event-level temporal synchronization, and fine-grained attribute alignment, providing a general formulation for audio-video consistency evaluation across diverse generation settings and cross-modal relations. We instantiate the metric as AVC-Reward, a trainable audio-video reward model. To evaluate the agreement between automatic judgments and human perception across generation paradigms and relation complexities, we construct AVC-Benchmark, which is primarily built from diverse positive and negative samples generated by various types of audio-video generation models. AVC-Reward achieves state-of-the-art alignment with human judgment among automatic methods, provides interpretable rationales that localize failures, and improves the audio-video consistency of a joint generation model when used as an online reinforcement-learning signal. MJev-Spatial: System One Spatial Intelligence via Native Attention Matching
Authors: Luozheng Qin, Yuhan Zhu, Changhai Zhou, Qian Qiao, Jun Gao, Yuhua Zhou, Harry Zhang, Xiangyu Zeng, Yuxuan Liu, Kaijie Chen, Andrew Chen, Pony Ma
First: 2026-10-15T17:37:50+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-15T17:37:50+00:00
Comments: 37 pages, 7 figures, 27 tables. Code: https://github.com/Fr0zenCrane/MJev-Spatial
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2 · Code3
Abstract
Spatial intelligence lets an agent perceive and act in the physical world, and many of its judgments must be made at interaction rates: which spatial relation holds, how far an object is, or where to place it. Vision-language models (VLMs) produce these answers by free-form text generation, paying for autoregressive decoding and answer parsing even though the output space is compact: one of a few options, a scalar, or a 2D point. In light of this, we introduce MJev-Spatial, a System 1 multimodal decision model that tackles all three types of spatial understanding tasks within a unified classification framework. Specifically, MJev-Spatial treats each candidate as a class and scores it using the backbone's pretrained query and key projections, without adding parameters. The candidates are the given options for choice tasks, or value bins and image regions refined through coarse-to-fine classification for numerical and point prediction, respectively. At inference, each classification decision takes a single forward pass and generates no answer tokens. To train on heterogeneous spatial data efficiently, we supervise all coarse-to-fine stages jointly with teacher forcing in one forward pass, and combine this with length-aware global batching and cross-GPU load balancing, raising training throughput by 30.8% over the original batching strategy. Built on Qwen3.5-4B and trained on 12.3M question-answer pairs converted from existing spatial datasets, MJev-Spatial achieves the highest overall score (65.64%) among eight tested models, ranks first on seven of twelve benchmarks, and outperforms its unmodified autoregressive base model on all twelve, while running 2.5 times faster than the fastest autoregressive baseline and 5.6 times faster than the base model.

Temporal Periodic Image Registration with Implicit Neural Representations
Authors: Mathias Lowes, Kristine Aavild Sørensen, Maxime Sermesant, Rasmus R. Paulsen
Venue: MICCAI 2025
First: 2026-10-15T13:24:47+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-15T13:24:47+00:00
Comments: Published at MLMI@MICCAI 2025
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2 · Code3
Abstract
Implicit Neural Representations (INRs) have recently gained popularity for their ability to model functions using simple and lightweight networks. These methods have also proven effective in deformable image registration. In this work, we extend INRs for deformable image registration to the domain of temporal image registration, focusing on periodic temporal image sequences. Our approach, Temporal-IDIR, optimizes a single INR to model deformations across all frames in a temporal image sequence simultaneously, allowing for self-regularization through its own deformation predictions. To achieve this, we introduce a temporal consistency loss that penalizes discrepancies between direct source-to-target transformations and those traversing intermediate frames. We evaluate our framework on the DIR-LAB dataset, using the target registration error (TRE) between annotated and moved landmarks as the metric. Here, we achieve a TRE of 1.03 mm, outperforming other INR-based registration methods. Additionally, our framework supports smooth interpolation between time frames by estimating deformations between the given input frames. Code is publicly available at https://github.com/MMLowes/Temporal_INR.

CNet: A Complex-Valued Deep Learning Framework with Wirtinger Autodifferentiation and FFT--Hadamard Convolution
Authors: Marcel Crasmaru
First: 2026-10-06T15:57:33+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-15T12:51:56+00:00
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
CNet is a C++/CUDA framework for deep complex-valued neural networks (CVNNs), trained by gradient descent with explicit Wirtinger (CR-calculus) derivatives. Complex models are underexplored yet natural where data is intrinsically complex -- RF/IQ, MRI k-space, radar/SAR, audio spectra -- and phase carries information real networks discard. CNet is physics-native: a network is a cascade of complex (often unitary) operations on an amplitude vector, and classification is a Born-rule measurement p_k = |z_k|^2 / ||z||^2 rather than a softmax over real logits. Every layer ships a CPU reference and a finite-difference-verified CUDA kernel; the static, batch-cloned computation graph captures cleanly into CUDA Graphs, outperforming eager and compiled torch.complex64 on an H100. We present three studies. (1) A fully complex causal language model, built on a new causal (triangular-masked) Fourier mixer with an O(N log N) Bluestein evaluation of the forward pass and its Wirtinger adjoint, matches a parameter-matched real causal FNet on character-level tiny-shakespeare in under half the steps. We introduce Born-rule attention, a content-based causal mixer whose weights are the quantum-measurement probabilities |<Q_k, K_j>|^2 -- to our knowledge the first classical, complex-valued attention to replace softmax with the squared-magnitude Born score; with rotary embeddings and per-token complex normalization it reaches 1.52 nats/char, matching softmax self-attention, and transfers unchanged to byte-level (1.31 nats/char) and sub-word WikiText-103. (2) On radio-modulation classification (RML2016.10a/RML22) we trace the pooled-power accuracy ceiling to discarded temporal structure and recover it with a learnable cyclostationary conjugate-lag operator. (3) A bottleneck analysis of the Fourier phase problem in coherent-diffraction imaging isolates where complex-valued networks still need new operators.

Single-stage joint diffusion of sky images and clear-sky index for ultra-short-term irradiance forecasting: what the frame-to-irradiance stage discards
Authors: Kanawut Suwandee, Suwichaya Suwanwimolkul
First: 2026-10-15T12:47:12+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-15T12:47:12+00:00
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Sky-image irradiance forecasting is dominated by a two-stage design: a video-prediction model forecasts future sky frames, and a separately trained regressor maps those frames to irradiance. We propose a single-stage alternative that co-denoises the future sky-image and clear-sky-index sequences in one diffusion process, with an irradiance head that reads a denoised preview of the future sky rather than the noised diffusion state. The forecaster needs no regression stage and runs from sky images and deterministic covariates alone, with no measured irradiance at its input --- the situation at a site with a camera but no pyranometer. On 2023 SIRTA data the model reaches a pooled RMSE of $104.1$~W\,m$^{-2}$ and a skill score of $+0.34$ on the continuous ranked probability score (CRPS) against smart persistence, higher than any of twelve baseline configurations, and retains that skill when retrained on 2021--2022. On the model's own generated frames, a separately trained regressor reaches $150.6$~W\,m$^{-2}$ against the joint head's $104.1$: roughly $46$~W\,m$^{-2}$ that joint training recovers and a cascade discards. Point accuracy is competitive but not best: a deterministic network that also reads measured irradiance attains $97.6$~W\,m$^{-2}$. The model's CRPS skill leads that network's in every sky-condition tertile, by $0.26$ to $0.30$.

3DTCR: Vortex-following generative reconstruction improves tropical cyclone intensity prediction from global AI weather forecasts
Authors: Jun Liu, Xiaohui Zhong, Kai Zheng, Jiarui Li, Yifei Li, Tao Zhou, Wenwen Zhang, Hao Li
First: 2026-03-13T15:00:07+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-15T08:20:50+00:00
Comments: 44 figures
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Global numerical weather prediction (NWP) and artificial intelligence (AI) models often underestimate tropical cyclone (TC) intensity, partly because coarse grids underresolve fine-scale three-dimensional inner-core structures that influence TC intensity evolution. Kilometer-scale NWP simulations can better resolve these structures but are too costly for operational use, which also makes high-resolution TC data limited. Here we construct a 3-km, TC-centered dataset for 2018--2024 using the Weather Research and Forecasting (WRF) model and develop 3DTCR, a vortex-following generative framework that reconstructs three-dimensional TC structure from FuXi global forecasts. 3DTCR combines conditional flow matching (CFM) with two-stage training to learn a coarse-to-fine mapping from center-aligned reanalysis simulation pairs and then adapt it to forecast inputs through latent space alignment. Evaluated on 2024 western North Pacific (WNP) forecasts, 3DTCR reduces the root mean square error (RMSE) and the magnitude of the mean bias error (MBE) in maximum 10-m wind speed by 36.5% and 60.7% relative to FuXi, and by 8.9% and 28.2% relative to the European Centre for Medium-Range Weather Forecasts high-resolution system (ECMWF-HRES), respectively. Case studies suggest that reconstructions capture wind gradients between the eye and eyewall, vertical structure of horizontal winds, and vortex asymmetries, while inheriting the FuXi-predicted TC tracks. 3DTCR generates 5-day TC-centered atmospheric fields in approximately 3 minutes on one GPU, whereas a full-basin 3-km WRF simulation takes about 400 hours on 32 CPU cores. These results demonstrate that such a vortex-following generative reconstruction can add localized three-dimensional detail to global forecasts and improve TC intensity prediction without basin-wide kilometer-scale numerical integration.

Full-Duplex Interaction with Speech, Expressions and Gestures
Authors: Siyin Wang, Wenyi Yu, Terumi Chiba, Xianzhao Chen, Xiaohai Tian, Jun Zhang, Lu Lu, Chao Zhang
First: 2026-10-15T07:54:33+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-15T07:54:33+00:00

Abstract
Human face-to-face conversation requires coordinating speech, gestures and expressions across both speaking and listening. We present ELLSA 2, an end-to-end full-duplex dialogue model that jointly optimizes speech interaction and streaming gesture-expression generation. An autoregressive, chunk-wise synthesizer combines pretrained acoustic features with distinct listening and speaking hidden states from the dialogue backbone and speech synthesizer, enabling continuous non-verbal generation even when the model is verbally silent. To translate dialogue understanding into vivid semantic gestures, we introduce contextual semantic calls and template guidance, separating whether to invoke an available semantic gesture from how to realize it. We also establish a curated data and evaluation pipeline for dialog-aware co-speech gesture generation and investigate reference-based multimodal large language models (MLLMs) as automatic judges. Experiments show that ELLSA 2 remains highly competitive with open-source full-duplex speech interaction models and outperforms cascaded baselines across dialog-aware gesture and expression metrics. Semantic template guidance further significantly improves semantic gesture quality. Strong system-level correlations with human judgments support these reference-based scores as useful preliminary metrics for full-duplex and semantic gesture assessment. Together, these results advance dialogue modeling from spoken responses toward coordinated multimodal interaction.

LEV: Thinking Fast and Deep with Looped Decision Models
Authors: Dingjie Song, Zhiling Yan, Yuxuan Zhang, Lichao Sun
First: 2026-10-14T21:20:03+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-14T21:20:03+00:00
Comments: 28 pages. Code: https://github.com/OpenLAIR/lev
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2 · Code3
Abstract
Decision models answer typed questions about an input with one probability per option, computed in a single forward pass without generating text. Like the fast System 1 of dual-process theory, they answer at once, but their depth is fixed at training time, so they spend the same computation on easy and hard questions. Models that reason in generated text can think longer, but they pay for every extra step in decoded tokens. We present LEV, a decision model that thinks deeper by rerunning its layers in a loop instead of generating tokens. LEV builds on a language model pretrained as a loop, reads the options after every loop step and trains all steps jointly, so one checkpoint can answer at any depth. On three looped backbones, accuracy rises with depth on the same weights, mostly on hard questions, and a 2.6B LEV goes from 63.2% at depth one to 76.2% at depth four on JevBench. On-demand depth, set per question by a small stopper with a threshold fitted on unlabelled requests, saves 15% to 24% of the computation at a cost of under one accuracy point, level with or slightly above a fixed mix of depths. On a locked test set, the 1.4B and 3B models are 5.4 to 8.8 points more accurate than dense (non-looped) models and cascades of the same latency below 50 ms.

Abstract
3D object composition is pivotal to digital content creation and robot simulation, while also serving as a critical testbed for physical intelligence in foundation models. Previous methods either leverage large language models to directly predict object coordinates, or utilize generative diffusion priors to synthesize composite images for visual alignment, both of which rely on static priors in digital spaces. However, these methods consistently struggle with complex compositions such as precise hanging, gravity-driven settling and hidden containment, as these tasks require interactive construction processes involving global planning, fine-grained alignment, and physical simulation. To address these challenges, we propose PhySGA, a Physics-Space Grounded Agentic Framework for 3D composition via agent exploration and physical simulation, enabling the universal interactive assembly of rigid, articulated, and deformable objects. Directly applying vanilla LLMs or VLMs to this task proves insufficient, as current models inherently lack grounded physical understanding and interactive construction experience. To overcome this limitation, we design a collaborative feedforward-feedback architecture: an Assembly Agent focuses on geometric and kinematic reasoning for global step planning and coarse adjustment, while a Critic Agent reasons over contact dynamics for simulator state diagnosis and fine-grained closed-loop correction. Furthermore, Action Routers map semantic planning and analysis onto executable physical primitives within the simulator, while a task-specific Self-Evolving Skill Library distills historical trial-and-error feedback into reusable experiences, boosting success rates and minimizing exploration attempts. Extensive experiments within Isaac Sim demonstrate that PhySGA significantly surpasses existing methods in relation fidelity, physical plausibility, and visual quality.

Selective Growing Q-Networks (SGQN): An Analysis of Adaptive Action Grids in Decoupled Q-Learning
Authors: Parham Nooranbakht, Seyed Mahdi Basiri Azad, Joschka Bödecker
First: 2026-10-14T16:45:10+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-14T16:45:10+00:00
Comments: 19 pages, 6 figures, 6 tables. Under review at TMLR
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Value-based Q-learning for continuous control can discretise the action space, but the resolution of the discretisation trades early exploration against final precision. Growing Q-Networks (GQN) grow the resolution uniformly from coarse to fine. We introduce Selective Growing Q-Networks (SGQN), which keeps decoupled Q-learning and replaces uniform growth with an adaptive action grid: bins are added next to bins with high estimated Q-value, bins with low Q-value are pruned, and actions are chosen epsilon-greedily. On eight DeepMind Control Suite (DMControl) tasks with 2 to 12 action dimensions, SGQN has the highest mean final return among the compared methods on four, is within 1.1% of the best method on three more, and is 3.4% below Bang-Bang MPO on Walker Run, using on average 7.5 of 9 available bins per dimension. We then test whether the adaptive grid explains these results, and find that it does not. Growth activates every candidate bin and behaves like uniform growth, and removing pruning changes the return by at most 1.1%. Two other choices matter: starting from a coarse grid, since starting with all 9 bins produces collapsed seeds, and epsilon-greedy selection, since an annealed softmax lowers the return by 23% to 100%. SGQN is insensitive to its growth and pruning hyperparameters. It also differs from the GQN and DecQN baselines in other respects, notably one gradient update per environment step instead of one per eight, which we did not control for, so we do not attribute its advantage to a single component. We present SGQN as a competitive value-based method and as an analysis of which ingredients of a growing action grid matter.
Summary / 总结

Authors: Miaobo Hu, Shuhao Hu, Xiaobo Guo, Xin Wang, Bokun Wang, Daren Zha, Jun Xiao

Abstract
We introduce CascadeLedger, a counterfactual execution-audit framework for budgeted language-model cascades. The protocol enforces call-before-read semantics, binds successful reads to service and cache identities, records availability and component-wise budget transitions, and freezes the event trace before any target or task scorer is joined. The central object is aggregate-equivalent trace validity: two executions can share a final answer, declared resource projection, and service histogram while differing in whether their routing decisions used only legitimately observable information. We complement single-trace invariants with counterfactual prefix non-interference (CPNI), which couples a visible prefix and policy randomness while changing only a declared hidden family. The counterfactual influence frontier (CIF) localizes the earliest violating prefix and a minimal hidden witness; projection-preserving adversarial mutation (PPAM) searches for invalid traces that remain acceptable to a non-counterfactual checker. Across frozen-cache, independently generated, and serving evaluations, the two-layer audit reports a clean false-positive rate of 0.0000, detects 0.9983 of serialized faults and 0.9982 of unlogged hidden-read faults, and changes 0.3403 of corrupted quality-resource conclusions after deterministic recomputation. The online recorder and offline CPNI audit have separate overhead measurements. Learned routing and semantic verification provide downstream compatibility studies under the same execution contract.

Abstract
Molecular design is often formulated as an optimization problem over existing molecular spaces. However, molecular optimization and the discovery of structurally distinct replacement molecules represent two fundamentally different search problems. LLM-based molecular design agents typically perform the former, conducting local refinement within the neighborhood of existing structures to improve target properties through structural modifications. In contrast, the latter requires exploration of a broader chemical space to discover molecules with entirely different scaffolds while preserving the functional characteristics of reference molecules. We propose MolHop, an agentic framework for cross-scaffold molecular discovery that enables models to navigate and edit within a pre-organized chemical space. These results demonstrate that effective molecular discovery beyond the traditional optimization paradigm requires structured chemical and action spaces, enabling agents to explore new structural regions rather than merely refine existing molecules.

Tracking large chemical reaction networks and rare events by neural networks
Authors: Jiayu Weng, Xinyi Zhu, Jing Liu, Linyuan Lü, Pan Zhang, Ying Tang
First: 2025-12-11T05:55:44+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-14T09:28:20+00:00
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Chemical reaction networks are widely used to model stochastic dynamics in biophysical processes. Their exact probabilistic description via the chemical master equation becomes intractable for high-dimensional systems due to the exponential growth of the state space. This difficulty is further exacerbated when metastable transitions and rare events dominate the dynamics. Here, we present an advanced neural-network approach that effectively tackles large reaction networks and rare-event dynamics within a unified framework. Building on autoregressive representations of the full joint probability distribution, we achieve efficient training through second-order optimization methods, including natural gradient descent and the time-dependent variational principle. We leverage enhanced-sampling strategies that enable accurate resolution of low-probability regions governing rare transitions. We demonstrate the method on a multistable genetic toggle switch and a mitogen-activated protein kinase (MAPK) cascade consisting of 16 species and 35 reactions with dense coupling. We further quantify rare events in spatially extended Schlögl models in both one- and two-dimensional lattices, reaching system sizes with extremely large state spaces up to $3\times10^{15}$. These results highlight the potential of neural modeling as a flexible and scalable framework for studying high-dimensional chemical reaction networks and rare events.

Can We Change "Stroke Size" to Simplify Regression Learning?

Abstract
Deep-learning-based image generation is increasingly useful but expensive. Across the different timesteps in the denoising chain, high-noise timesteps present distinct optimization and prediction challenges, which can increase the cost of real-world applications. Inspired by the varying stroke sizes used in human painting, in this work, we ask whether a similar stroke-size variation (i.e., rough stroke applied early) in diffusion can simplify existing image-generation algorithms.
