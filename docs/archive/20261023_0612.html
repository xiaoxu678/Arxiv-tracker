VX-One: Typed, Calibrated Decision Heads for Real-Time Perimeter Triage - An empirical study of unknown-attack honesty, cascade gray zones, and calibration under drift
Authors: Juan S. Santillana
First: 2026-10-21T17:39:12+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-21T17:39:12+00:00
Comments: 11 pages, 7 tables, 1 figure
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Perimeter triage faces a latency/semantics trade-off: signature engines are fast and format-safe but blind to unseen attacks, while generative models are semantic but slow and produce unparseable output. In late 2026 a class of commercial System One decision models (TypeSafe Jev; Microsoft-Decision-1) began selling typed decision primitives - binary gates, closed-set choices, ordinal scores - as product APIs, with no academic treatment. We present VX-One: a small multi-head network (100K-10M parameters) whose outputs are these primitives - a calibrated gate, a choice over a closed candidate set that always includes an explicit unknown option, and an ordinal score, trained multi-task, temperature-calibrated per head (~0.7-1.7 ms p50 on CPU). We evaluate it on UNSW-NB15, CIC-IDS2017, EMBER2024 against calibrated XGBoost, Random-Forest, MLP, Kitsune, and measure a two-phase cascade (Suricata with 52,674 Emerging-Threats rules -> VX-One) over five days of CIC-IDS2017. Findings: (i) VX-One wins detection (AUROC/FPR@95%TPR) on UNSW-NB15 and under temporal drift on CIC-IDS2017 (0.711 vs 0.637), but XGBoost wins multi-class attribution on all three benchmarks; (ii) leave-one-family-out gate detects never-seen families on 9/9 UNSW folds yet drops below chance on CIC recon family, and abstention threshold transfers poorly across benchmarks; (iii) on payload-less reconstructions signature engines resolve ~0.1% of flows - gray zone ~99.9% of traffic, recovered by Phase 2 (combined recall >=0.978); (iv) calibration collapses under temporal drift (ECE 0.04->0.40) for every method while ranking partially survives. We provide a measured account of where typed calibrated decisions help perimeter triage, and where they fail.

Circuits of LLM Classifiers

First: 2026-10-21T17:27:46+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-21T17:27:46+00:00
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Language models make it easy to build classifiers by simply prompting a model with the sample to classify. However, their predictions are neither deterministic nor uniformly reliable across samples. How can we combine predictions from different models to improve reliability? We view LLM classifiers as noisy measurement instruments of an underlying target label. Our goal is to maximize average classification accuracy at a given average query cost. To this end, we introduce circuits that organize LLM classifiers into an adaptive sequence of queries and decisions. We then characterize the optimal circuit, obtain the fundamental limits of achievable accuracy, and provide practical insights on realizing the optimal circuit. Across seven classification tasks spanning safety, health, political science, human preference, agent evaluation, decision-making and reasoning, our method improves the cost-accuracy frontier over routing and cascading baselines.

Abstract
Structured 3D representations such as keypoints and meshes offer compact, expressive descriptions of deformable objects, jointly capturing geometric and topological information useful for downstream tasks such as dynamics modeling and motion planning. However, robustly extracting such representations remains challenging, as current perception methods struggle to handle complex deformations. Moreover, large-scale 3D data collection remains a bottleneck: existing approaches either require prohibitive data collection efforts, such as labor-intensive annotation or expensive motion capture setups, or rely on simplifying assumptions that break down in unstructured environments. As a result, large-scale 3D datasets and benchmarks for deformable objects remain scarce. To address these challenges, this paper presents an affordable and autonomous framework for collecting 3D datasets of deformable objects using only RGB-D cameras. The proposed method identifies 3D keypoints and robustly tracks their trajectories, incorporating motion consistency constraints to produce temporally smooth and geometrically coherent data. TrackDeform3D is evaluated against several state-of-the-art tracking methods across diverse object categories and demonstrates consistent improvements in both geometric and tracking accuracy. Using this framework, this paper presents a high-quality, large-scale dataset consisting of 6 deformable objects, totaling 110 minutes of trajectory data. Cascading Bandits with Noisy Observations: Algorithms and Regret Bounds
Authors: Yashaswini Kaluguri, Nikhil Karamchandani
First: 2026-10-21T08:10:49+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-21T08:10:49+00:00
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
We propose a novel stochastic cascading bandit setup where probing an ordered shortlist of $K$ out of $L$ arms incurs position-dependent costs, and the learner observes only noisy feedback regarding each arm's true output. We identify a sub-class of noise models for which the optimal shortlist can be found by sorting arms according to a per-arm score: such a score exists for both selection and ordering when the channel has no false positives, and for ordering alone (but not selection) when measurements are costless; however in general neither selection nor ordering is separable once noise and cost are both present. For the case where both selection and ordering scores exist, we provide a UCB-based algorithm with $O((L-K)\log T)$ instance-dependent regret over a time horizon $T$, which matches an algorithm-agnostic lower bound (up to constant factors). For the more challenging scenario where scores don't exist, we develop a new algorithmic template, DP-UCB, that embeds optimism directly inside an exact dynamic program, yielding $O\big(LK\log T\big)$ regret. Finally, we validate our theoretical results through extensive numerical evaluations and simulations over both synthetic and real-world datasets.

Abstract
Zero-shot cross-lingual voice cloning often carries the reference speaker's accent into the target language. We propose closed-loop dual-reference guidance, which pairs the original reference with a reusable native anchor constructed by voice conversion (VC). The references initialize separate language-model (LM) states that guide a shared acoustic trajectory; each completed patch then updates both states, keeping their generated histories aligned with the emitted speech. Anchor construction runs once per speaker, and online synthesis uses a frozen TTS model without per-utterance VC. On 20 held-out French speakers, the fixed setting $λ^{\!*}=0.33$ reduces residual French LID mass by 73\% versus direct cloning, at an ECAPA similarity cost of only $0.042$; relative to anchor-only cloning, it recovers $0.155$. The method achieves the lowest mean WER ($0.009$) and highest mean GOP ($4.750$) among the held-out test arms. In blinded preference tests, native-English listeners favor it over direct cloning on pronunciation naturalness, and over both anchor-only cloning and a TTS$\to$VC cascade on speaker identity. Online RTF is approximately 26\% lower than the cascade's ($0.59$ vs.\ $0.80$), and the same setting reduces Spanish LID mass by 56\% without re-tuning.

RayCo4D: Ray-Conditioned Continuous Motion Model for Monocular 4D Dynamic Reconstruction
Authors: Yuanxiang Ni, Xianliang Huang, Ruihan Hou, Feng Xie, Hao Zhang
First: 2026-10-20T15:17:29+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-21T07:20:40+00:00
Comments: 25 pages, 13 figures
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Reconstructing dynamic 3D scenes from monocular video requires inferring motion from noisy and intermittently visible observations. Long-range tracks provide valuable geometric constraints, but integrating their fragmented temporal coverage into shared, continuous motion remains challenging. We introduce RayCo4D, a ray-conditioned continuous motion model for monocular 4D reconstruction that explicitly separates visual measurements from latent 3D motion states. Each track is associated with a reference-time 3D query constrained to its camera ray, and its image and depth observations constrain shared latent motion nodes through local many-to-many associations. Each node maintains an $\mathrm{SE}(3)$ pose and body-frame velocity at sparse support times. A white-noise-on-acceleration process prior couples neighboring states and defines continuous-time interpolation, allowing observations across visibility intervals to jointly constrain the same latent state sequence. The inferred shared motion is transferred to Gaussian primitives through dual-quaternion blending, while reference-anchored local $\mathrm{SE}(3)$ residuals capture fine-scale non-rigid deformation beyond the shared scaffold. Experiments on diverse monocular dynamic scenes show that RayCo4D achieves competitive novel-view synthesis quality, improves structural and temporal consistency under rapid motion and incomplete tracks, and supports continuous-time rendering.

Abstract
Dynamic local community detection tracks the evolving community of designated seed nodes across graph snapshots. However, it faces two critical challenges: temporal supervision uncertainty, where sparse and outdated labels fail to provide seed-specific guidance under topology changes, and unbounded error cascades, where sequential policy transfer accumulates exploration errors over long horizons. To address these challenges, we propose DISRL, a Dual-scale Incremental Semi-supervised Reinforcement Learning framework. Extensive experiments demonstrate that DISRL consistently outperforms state-of-the-art baselines under various dynamic settings.

Abstract
Recovering dynamic 3D scene geometry, appearance, and the physical motion states that govern scene evolution from multi-view videos is important yet challenging. A key difficulty is that appearance can be deceptive. Under rendering supervision alone, a model may fit the observed images by changing color or opacity to compensate for inaccurate deformation, rather than recovering the true evolving geometry and motion. This issue is especially severe in scenes with fast or locally complex motion, where inaccurate trajectories can still produce plausible images over the observed frames, but lead to unreliable reconstruction and discontinuous future prediction. In this paper, we propose PhyMo, a framework for learning evolving dynamics from multi-view videos. We introduce a Motion-Aware Trajectory Representation that drives the model to recover accurate motion from deformation, rather than relying on appearance shortcuts to explain observed motion. On top of this, we propose a Continuous Motion Evolution module with bidirectional kinematic continuity constraint to promote temporally consistent evolution of displacement, velocity, and acceleration. Together, these designs yield geometrically faithful interpolation and continuous motion extrapolation. Experiments on four dynamic scene benchmarks demonstrate state-of-the-art performance.

Abstract
True whole-body medical imaging captures a patient from head to toe and provides comprehensive anatomical and/or functional information. This coverage enables reconstruction of the external body surface, yielding patient body models. However, scanner geometry (e.g., bore diameter), patient characteristics, and positioning can leave body regions missing (e.g., lateral arms/torso, hands, forearms, elbows), producing incomplete patient body models. Such incompleteness limits downstream medical analyses, including derivation of body-habitus metrics (BHMs), such as body volume and surface area, for injected radiotracer activity regimens, cardiometabolic risk assessment, and computational anatomical modeling for medical imaging simulation and radiation dosimetry. We present the Medical Body model Optimization-based Estimator (MedBOE), an automated, training-free framework for estimating complete patient body models from whole-body images with missing body regions. Our innovation is to leverage the Skinned Multi-Person Linear (SMPL) template, a parametric body mesh representing a broad range of body shapes and poses, in medical imaging. MedBOE aligns the SMPL template to the incomplete patient body models via attribute-guided optimization and estimates missing body regions through geometrically and volumetrically constrained deformation. We evaluated MedBOE using two public datasets: AutoPET (91 whole-body PET-CT images) and Human Implicit Tissues (HIT; 40 whole-body segmentations from MRI images). MedBOE achieved the best overall volumetric and geometric performance among evaluated methods. BHMs derived from MedBOE were within 2% error, suggesting low percentage errors for downstream analyses. MedBOE offers an automated, training-free method for estimating complete patient body models from whole-body images with missing body regions.

Self-organizing reinforcement learning: a learner-free swarm approaches the explore-exploit optimum
Authors: Galen J. Wilkerson
First: 2026-10-18T08:45:51+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-20T19:20:05+00:00
Comments: 16 pages, 9 figures, code and data: https://github.com/galenwilkerson/self-organizing-reinforcement-learning
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2 · Code3
Abstract
The explore/exploit trade-off is the core problem of reinforcement learning (RL). We show that a minimal foraging swarm approaches its optimum - the ideal-free / Nash allocation of a multi-agent resource-sharing game (an equilibrium, not a return maximum) - with no learner and no reward signal. Agents explore by heavy-tailed (Levy) flights, recruit neighbours to a discovery through a threshold cascade on their proximity network, and disperse as a patch crowds; this cascade cycle balances exploration and exploitation and drives foragers toward the ideal free distribution. The swarm attains the shape of this optimum (committed foragers track richness with slope 0.94) but not its exact equilibrium - it stays measurably exploitable - reaching it dynamically (~150 steps) through the coupling of recruitment to movement: remove the coupling and matching collapses (0.94 -> 0.08). The RL sits at the level of the problem and the allocation reached, not the micro-rule: no step evaluates payoff (reward-free by construction, though the crowd signal is informationally tied to payoff), and the swarm reaches the allocation without learning it (quality plateaus and re-settles after resources move, no cumulative gain). Against a trained baseline the comparison is two-sided: in an easier, disembodied game a learner is more precise, while in the same embodied environment the reward-free swarm reaches ~65% of a validated oracle ceiling at zero training and leads until trained PPO and centralised-critic (CTDE) learners overtake it (~74-75%, 16 seeds, all above the swarm mean) after ~4,000 of 15,000 training steps - its advantage is in budget, not asymptote. In this one setting we read it not as beating RL but as a strong zero-shot, reward-free operating point: a demonstration that the self-organizing physics of a minimal swarm can carry a collective close to the optimum RL seeks, with none of RL's machinery.
Summary / 总结
The explore/exploit trade-off is the core problem of reinforcement learning (RL).

Abstract
We present Register Anything Model (RAM), a unified model for generalizable 3D point cloud registration that robustly estimates 6-DoF transformations across diverse domains spanning sensors and environments. Existing registration methods are typically tailored to a single domain, with carefully tuned architectures and hyperparameters, and thus degrade markedly when applied to novel domains. We bridge this gap by training a single model on diverse data from indoor, outdoor, and object domains. A central challenge is the severe geometric discrepancy across domains, i.e., differences in scene scale, sensor noise, point density, and structural patterns, which hinders processing with a unified point-based model. To address this, we introduce a geometry-aware normalization strategy that jointly voxelizes and normalizes point clouds into a standardized geometric space, adaptively based on their geometric properties to preserve salient local structures while standardizing resolution. Built on this normalization, RAM follows a hierarchical coarse-to-fine paradigm, and employs an efficient geometric transformer that models local geometric consistency instead of globally over all points. This design mitigates discretization noise introduced by voxelization, and improves computational efficiency. Extensive experiments on 12 benchmarks demonstrate state-of-the-art performance in terms of inlier ratio and registration recall. Notably, our method outperforms the counterparts trained on each separate domain, showing superior generalization capability.

Abstract
Visual tool use enables vision-language models (VLMs) to acquire task-relevant evidence, but its effectiveness can be limited by predefined visual operations and the tool-use strategies learned during training. Representing these operations and strategies as reusable external skills allows them to be refined through self-evolution without updating model parameters. However, skill refinement can introduce performance regressions. Meanwhile, inspecting accumulated task execution trajectories together with skill evolution history may lead to evolution evidence overload. To address these challenges, we propose VisualEvolve, a self-evolving framework that evolves executable visual operations and tool-use strategies for VLMs through a Select--Execute--Evolve--Evaluate cycle. Skill-Library Snapshot Selection uses performance on a fixed validation set disjoint from execution tasks to select an effective historical snapshot for subsequent evolution, limiting the propagation of performance regressions. Progressive Evidence Access (PEA) guides capability-gap diagnosis through a coarse-to-fine hierarchy comprising the Skill-Performance Report, Evolution Log, Trajectory Summaries, and relevant raw trajectory segments. Across four VLM backbones and six evaluation sets from three benchmarks, VisualEvolve improves over the Initial Skill Library in 23 of 24 settings, with a maximum gain of 15.1 percentage points on VisualProbe Hard using Qwen3.5-Plus. Ablation studies support the contributions of Snapshot Selection and PEA, while further experiments show that skills can be improved using experience from different data settings and directly reused across backbones. Moreover, VisualEvolve enables effective skill self-evolution with over an order of magnitude fewer task-solving trajectories than estimated for representative RL-based methods.

Abstract
Video world models must preserve the visual state of the world over time, but existing evaluation protocols often rely on generated histories, video reference, or selected revisit viewpoints that can confound the assessment of a model's true memory capability. Comments: IEEE Access, vol.14, 2026

Abstract
Large Vision-Language Models (VLMs) are successful in addressing a multitude of vision-language understanding tasks, such as Visual Question Answering (VQA), but their memory and compute requirements remain a concern for practical deployment. A promising class of techniques for mitigating this concern is Knowledge Distillation, where knowledge from a high-capacity Teacher network is transferred to a considerably smaller Student network. However, the capacity gap between the two networks is both a blessing and a curse: the smaller the Student network, the better its efficiency, and the stronger (typically, also larger) the Teacher, the more knowledge it carries; yet, beyond a point, the larger capacity gap between the two leads to worse knowledge transfer. To counter this effect, we propose a bottom-up cascaded knowledge distillation (CKD) framework. Instead of treating knowledge transfer as an activity involving one high-capacity Teacher (or an ensemble of such), inspired by human formal education systems, we introduce one (potentially, more) additional Teacher(s) of intermediate capacity that gradually bring the Student network to the next level, where the next (higher-capacity) Teacher can take over. We provide a theoretical analysis to motivate why progressive reduction of the Teacher-Student gap may improve distillation. We apply the proposed framework on models built upon the LLaVA methodology and evaluate the derived models on seven standard, publicly available VQA benchmarks, demonstrating competitive performance with recent compact VLMs. Source code for cascaded distillation and trained models are available at https://github.com/IDT-ITI/LLaVA-CKD.

Abstract
Carousel interfaces allow a recommender system to directly observe how far a user has browsed. This signal distinguishes displayed but unclicked items from items that were never displayed, whereas conventional ranking-bandit models, including cascade and position-based models, generally treat examination as latent. We formulate a ranking-bandit problem in which a learner presents a list of $L$ items, observes the user's maximum browsing depth, and receives click feedback only for positions up to that depth. EndoWorld-JEPA: Motion-Guided Global-Local Latent World Modeling for Endoscopic Video Representation Learning
Authors: Jie Wang, Shilong Yang, Tao Zhou, Juan Yu, Rong Qu, Jianfeng Ren, Zhicheng Zhang, Linlin Shen
First: 2026-10-20T09:11:49+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-20T09:11:49+00:00
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Endoscopic video contains state transitions induced by camera displacement, deformable tissue, and instrument interaction, yet existing pre-training methods treat dynamics implicitly. Inspired by action-conditioned joint-embedding predictive world models and by the observation that endoscopic optical flow contains both shared image-plane displacement and localized residual motion, we reformulate passive endoscopic video pre-training as causal motion-guided latent-state prediction. We present EndoWorld-JEPA, to our knowledge the first latent world-model pre-training framework designed for general-purpose endoscopic video representation learning. In place of recorded actions, calibrated poses, or robot kinematics, the model conditions prediction of the next latent state on motion observed up to the current state. Its Optical Motion-guided module separates dominant displacement from residual motion and converts the resulting evidence into an ordered temporal motion condition and spatial local-motion weights. Complementary global and motion-local predictors use these outputs: the temporal condition modulates both latent transitions, whereas the local weights define an auxiliary motion-local state that extends prediction from scene-level evolution to spatially selective dynamics. Patch-wise motion weighting emphasizes localized change while retaining anatomical context, and past-motion-window dropout randomly withholds the complete motion condition to reduce dependence on unreliable motion. This global--local formulation learns predictable visual--motion dynamics in representation space without reconstructing stochastic appearance artifacts. Evaluation shows that a common pre-trained checkpoint achieves strong transfer across four downstream benchmarks, supporting motion-guided global--local latent prediction as an effective pre-training paradigm for endoscopic video.

Abstract
Continual vision-language models (VLMs) encounter a stream of environments and must also reject inputs that do not belong to the environment at hand. Class information from earlier environments can aid out-of-distribution (OOD) detection, yet existing detectors score each environment in isolation, and when previous environments help or hurt is theoretically unclear. We develop a theory of environment-aware OOD detection built on contribution scores, pairwise cosine expectations measuring how the image and text features of one environment align with those of another. We propose a cross-environment maximum-concept-matching (MCM) score with two terms: a primary term matching the test image against the current class texts, and a secondary term matching it against the class texts of a previous environment, weighted by the intra-modal alignment between the two environments. We prove that, under a positive environment transition and a bounded class-matching margin for OOD inputs, this score attains a false-positive rate no larger than that of the standard MCM detector at an adjusted threshold. We then show that the score gap of an MCM detector between two environments equals the self-contribution of the in-distribution environment minus its cross-modal contribution into the OOD environment, which separates soft OOD environments, with explicit AUROC, true-positive, and false-positive guarantees, from hard ones, which no threshold that retains the in-distribution data can reliably reject. For cascaded detection, in which the text prototypes of all seen environments are pooled, the false-positive rate on every past environment is non-decreasing in time, and we characterize when detection provably fails. A contribution balance factor applied to the gradients of the classification loss and of a smoothed false-positive rate completes the framework.
