Understanding the Staged Dynamics of Transformers in Learning Latent Structure
Authors: Rohan Saha, Farzane Aminmansour, Alona Fyshe
First: 2025-11-24T17:20:42+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-29T16:56:31+00:00
Comments: Added ablation experiments
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Language models have proven to be capable of learning the intricate latent structures in language. However, the dynamics of how such models acquire the components of that structure remain poorly understood, leaving them open to the criticism that models just remix training data. In this work, we use the Alchemy benchmark as a controlled setting in which we investigate how models acquire the different latent structure components of the data. We train a small decoder-only transformer on three controlled task variants: 1) inferring missing steps from partial contextual information, 2) composing simple rules to solve multi-step sequences, and 3) decomposing complex multi-step examples to infer intermediate states. By factorizing each task into interpretable components, we show that the model learns the different latent structure components in discrete $\textit{stages}$ and in a $\textit{coarse-to-fine}$ manner. Using inference-time ablations, we demonstrate that when certain latent structures are masked or replaced, the model can use an alternative intermediate latent structure to solve the task. We also observe an asymmetry: the model composes single step transitions robustly, but struggles to decompose complex examples to discover the atomic steps. Our findings provide insight into $\textit{how}$ a transformer model acquires latent structure, offering a detailed view of how capabilities evolve during training.

Abstract
In this paper, we propose Concentrate and Concentrate (CaC), a coarse-to-fine anomaly reward model based on Vision-Language Models. During inference, it first conducts a global temporal scan to anchor anomalous time windows, then performs fine-grained spatial grounding within the localized interval, and finally derives robust judgments via structured spatiotemporal Chain-of-Thought reasoning. To equip the model with these capabilities, we construct the first large-scale generated video anomaly dataset with per-frame bounding-box annotations, temporal anomaly windows, and fine-grained attribution labels. Building on this dataset, we design a three-stage progressive training paradigm. The model initially learns spatial and temporal anchoring through single- and multi-frame supervised fine-tuning, and then is optimized by a reinforcement learning strategy based on two-turn Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO). Beyond conventional accuracy rewards, we introduce Temporal and Spatial IoU rewards to supervise the intermediate localization process, effectively guiding the model toward more grounded and interpretable spatiotemporal reasoning. Extensive experiments demonstrate that CaC can stably concentrate on subtle anomalies, achieving a 25.7% accuracy improvement on fine-grained anomaly benchmarks and, when used as a reward signal, CaC reduces generated-video anomalies by 11.7% while improving overall video quality.

Morphology-Aware Channel-Decoupled Mamba Network for Medical Image Segmentation

Abstract
Accurate multi-organ segmentation in computed tomography (CT) is essential for computer-aided diagnosis, treatment planning, and disease monitoring. However, substantial variations in organ shape and size, together with low tissue contrast and ambiguous boundaries, complicate the modeling of heterogeneous anatomical structures. To address these challenges, we propose a Morphology-Aware Channel-Decoupled Mamba Network (MCDNet), which integrates adaptive local structural enhancement with channel-decoupled long-range modeling. Specifically, we introduce a Morphology-Aware Channel-Decoupled State-Space (MCSS) block that divides feature channels into separate processing groups, allowing differentiated structural representations to be learned within an efficient state-space framework. Within each group, a Morphology-Aware Enhancement Module (MAEM) combines deformable convolutions with channel attention to adapt local feature extraction to anatomical shape variations. Group-wise state-space modeling then captures long-range spatial dependencies, and channel gating adaptively integrates the resulting features. Experiments on the Synapse abdominal CT dataset demonstrate the effectiveness of MCDNet, achieving an average Dice Similarity Coefficient of 81.61% for multi-organ segmentation.

Abstract
Assessment of the main pancreatic duct (MPD) from volumetric magnetic resonance cholangiopancreatography (MRCP) scans is an essential task for ductal dilatation diagnosis and pancreatic cancer analysis. However, manual assessment is affected by reader variability in which automated analysis may assist. Nevertheless, fully automated and uncertainty-aware MPD analysis from volumetric MRCP remains underexplored area of research. In this study, we propose an automatic and uncertainty-aware deep learning-based pipeline for MPD segmentation and maximum-width analysis from MRCP scans. A coarse localisation model first identifies the ductal region-of-interest, followed by a fine model that segments the MPD within the region. The dataset consists of 256 MPD-segmented MRCP scans from 69 patients. A randomly selected holdout test subset of 14 patients with 55 MRCP volumes was used for evaluation. We employed a five-model cross-validation ensemble for improved performance and uncertainty estimation. The best MPD segmentation performance was 0.634 DSC, 0.744 precision, and 0.593 recall. The mean absolute error for maximum MPD width prediction was 0.355~mm and dilated MPD ($\geq 5$~mm) was detected with 75.0\% sensitivity and 74.4\% specificity. These novel results suggest that automated MPD segmentation can be used to aid MRCP-based duct measurements.

Abstract
Understanding the internal routing of visual concepts is crucial for the safe and controllable adaptation of generative models. While concept localization has been widely studied in diffusion models, the emerging paradigm of next-scale visual autoregressive models remains largely unexplored. In this paper, we introduce LoCo, the first model-agnostic framework to localize where and when specific concepts emerge within autoregressive models. Driven by the native coarse-to-fine nature of next-scale generation, our method precisely maps conceptual knowledge across three distinct dimensions: Layer, Scale, and Position. To systematically localize and evaluate concept routing without context bias, we propose LoCoBench, a comprehensive dataset spanning 10 diverse categories. Extensive probing on image and video autoregressive models like Infinity, HunyuanImage-3.0, and InfinityStar shows that the localized positions are both interpretable and causally related to concept emergence. Building on these insights, we apply our localization method to three key applications: concept erasure, model personalization, and adversarial concept injection. Experiments demonstrate that our targeted intervention achieves state-of-the-art performance, substantially reducing computational overhead while preserving benign utility. Overall, our findings offer insights into how conceptual knowledge is routed during autoregressive generation, introducing a practical pathway for more interpretable, efficient, and secure adaptation.

Abstract
Generating interactive 3D assets from a single image requires realistic appearance, fidelity to the depicted object, and plausible physical behavior. Abstract
Non-rigid point cloud registration depends on accurate correspondences under deformation and partial visibility. Existing coarse-to-fine methods use coarse matches to select regions for fine matching, but incorrect selection can remove valid correspondences before refinement. We introduce \paper, a conditional transport framework that connects coarse correspondence estimation to high-resolution matching. A diffusion model first estimates a coarse matching matrix. We lift the complete estimate to a fine-level source matrix and refine its scores with a geometry-conditioned bridge. We consider two bridge formulations: a deterministic endpoint-parameterized flow and a stochastic paired Brownian bridge with endpoint and noise prediction. At each step, the current matrix provides an alignment for the next prediction. Both models reuse backbone features throughout inference. Experiments on 4DMatch and 4DLoMatch show improved correspondence and non-rigid registration accuracy. The models also transfer to CAPE and DeepDeform without target-domain training. Ablations examine the roles of candidate coverage, source initialization, and refinement budget.

Bayes-Consistent Losses for Adaptive Inference in Classification
Authors: Shirley Xiaoqi Liu, Florence Regol, Antonios Valkanas, Patrick Rebeschini, Mark Coates
First: 2026-10-29T09:44:20+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-29T09:44:20+00:00

Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Adaptive inference systems balance accuracy and inference cost by routing inputs to models with varying capabilities. Although jointly training models and routers is empirically more sample-efficient than training them separately, principled losses for joint training remain underexplored. We address this gap in a classification setting by proposing cost-sensitive hinge losses for jointly training classifiers with single-stage or cascade routers. Single-stage routers directly select a model per input, whereas cascade routers sequentially decide at each stage whether to predict with the current model or defer to the next stage, which uses a more accurate but more costly model. For both settings, we prove linear calibration bounds establishing Bayes consistency: minimizing the hinge risk recovers the Bayes-optimal risk under the target cost-sensitive zero-one loss. Moreover, since routers can require more labeled data to train than classifiers, we propose a novel post-training prediction-powered inference (PPI) procedure that uses unlabeled data to refine a trained single-stage router. Experiments on synthetic and real datasets demonstrate that the proposed hinge losses are effective for joint training and that PPI can correct routing errors when labeled data is scarce.

Adaptive inference systems balance accuracy and inference cost by routing inputs to models with varying capabilities.

Abstract
Rigging is inherently task-dependent because the same mesh may require different skeletons and deformation behaviors across animation tasks. In practice, artists often inspect an initial rig and repeatedly edit its skeletal structure and deformation behavior to meet specific animation requirements. Existing automatic methods primarily generate a plausible rig from geometry, offering limited explicit control over the resulting skeleton and deformation behavior. In this work, we present ViP-Rig, a visual-prompted framework that supports both prompt-first rigging and result-guided editing by injecting features extracted from user-drawn or edited 2D skeletal and rigidity prompts into frozen pretrained backbones. Specifically, ViP-Rig consists of two stages, Skeleton Generation and Skinning Prediction. In the first stage, the skeletal sketch is processed by the Dense-to-Compact Visual Prompt Encoding to produce compact, fixed-length conditioning tokens. The resulting tokens are injected into a frozen pretrained autoregressive generator through gated adapters to control joint placement and branching structure while preserving the generator's geometric prior. In the second stage, the rigidity map is processed using the same visual encoding design, while the pretrained skinning backbone remains frozen. The resulting tokens are symmetrically injected into the point and joint streams to modulate point-joint compatibility and the resulting skinning weights. Experiments on Articulation-XL2.0 and zero-shot evaluation on ModelsResource show that ViP-Rig more accurately recovers target skeletons and skinning weights than geometry-conditioned baselines under prompt-guided evaluation. Qualitative results further demonstrate explicit and localized control in both prompt-first rigging and result-guided editing.

Abstract
Existing image generation models face critical challenges regarding the trade-off between computation and fidelity. Specifically, models relying on a pretrained Variational Autoencoder (VAE) suffer from information loss, limited detail, and the inability to support end-to-end training. In contrast, models operating directly in the pixel space incur prohibitive computational cost. Although cascade models can mitigate computational cost, stage-wise separation prevents effective end-to-end optimization, hampers knowledge sharing, and often results in inaccurate distribution learning within each stage. To address these challenges, we introduce a unified multi-stage generative framework formulated under the stochastic interpolant formalism with our Conditional Dependent Coupling strategy as a Flow-Matching variant. Abstract
Aligning a partial, real-world 3D face scan to a canonical representation requires solving two coupled problems at once: estimating the rigid pose that brings the scan into a common frame, and completing the facial shape in regions the scan never observed. Existing pipelines typically decouple these steps, running a generic point-cloud registration front end followed by a separate non-rigid fitting or deformation stage. Building on the GeoTransformer superpoint-matching architecture, we propose a single network that predicts both a rigid pose and a shape-complete point cloud in one forward pass: a cross-attention regressor, conditioned on transformer point features and predicted coarse correspondences, maps a partial scan to per-patch coefficients of a 3D morphable face model learned offline from a corpus of 8,000 identities. The resulting morphed template is then used, in the same forward pass, as the target for fine-grained Sinkhorn matching and rigid pose estimation, supervised purely by geometric alignment to ground-truth morphed geometry, so no hand-labeled coefficients are required. Against six classical and learned non-rigid registration baselines on held-out identities, our method leads the strongest ones by 3-4 times on per-vertex error and baselines that collapse the head onto the visible region by more than an order of magnitude, and is simultaneously the most accurate and fastest method on ground-truth-free scans from two independent real-scan datasets, NoW and NPHM. Code will be made publicly available.

Abstract
Radio-frequency (RF) sensing enables robot ego-motion estimation in darkness, smoke, and fog, yet RF odometry remains challenging because multipath and moving reflectors make cross-frame matching highly ambiguous. Existing methods often rely on auxiliary inertial sensors or ground-truth pose supervision. We present RFWalk, a self-supervised, RF-only odometry framework that models dense correspondences across range-azimuth frames as random walks on a space-time graph and learns them from unlabeled sequences through temporal cycle consistency. Abstract
Reliable vocal-cord localization in emergency video laryngoscopy remains visually challenging due to rapid tissue deformation, specular highlights, and instrument occlusions. We propose Local Point Tracking (LPT), a robust framework that combines a single-frame detector, local point propagation, and stateless mask refinement. LPT follows a localize first, snap second pipeline. First, a single-frame semantic detector generates sparse anchors, within which LPT initializes local query points (SAM-supported by default, with a regular grid as fallback). A point-propagation module tracks these points through detector-missed frames and recovers temporal bounding boxes from their geometry. Second, because point-derived boxes can be loose around the visible boundary, a box-snap step refines the selected box to the target boundary. This decomposition keeps temporal state in local point coordinates rather than a stateful mask or whole-region representation, while the spatial refinement module handles single-frame boundary precision without feeding mask errors back into temporal memory. Extensive evaluations on two distinct datasets demonstrate that our proposed LPT method achieves strong overall performance against existing baselines, with the best mAP_50, success AUC, and missing rate on both datasets. Furthermore, ablation studies confirm that point tracking primarily enhances temporal tracking robustness, while box-snap refinement effectively improves boundary tightness.

Rethinking LLM Jailbreak Evaluation: Continuous Risk Landscapes Beyond Binary Outcomes
Authors: Yukai Zhou, Wenjie Wang
First: 2026-10-28T10:23:27+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-28T10:23:27+00:00
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
A binary success indicator is a natural representation for discriminative tasks such as image classification under adversarial perturbations, where each input is mapped to a single prediction. However, generative tasks are fundamentally different: an LLM jailbreak prompt induces a distribution over open-ended trajectories, while a sampled response reveals only one path. Yet LLM jailbreak evaluation typically retains the binary response indicator underlying attack success rate, obscuring the surrounding structure of possible generations. We introduce a continuous, context-conditioned risk landscape over first-token branches, estimated by fixing candidate openings and repeatedly sampling their continuations. This continuous risk-landscape perspective reveals two complementary properties: current decoder exposure and safe rerouting capacity, which have been obscured by binary-indicator-based ASR. The resulting representation also provides a common basis for comparing clean prompts, attacked variants, and attack strength against a known adaptive defense. Across multiple target models and jailbreak attacks, we uncover risky branches beneath apparently safe outputs, identify qualitatively different ways attacks reshape the model's opening distribution, and show that attacks strongest before a defense do not necessarily remain the strongest after the adaptive defense reacts. These findings recast jailbreaks as deformations of the surrounding continuation-risk landscape, establishing a more informative foundation for understanding jailbreak behavior and developing safer language models.

Authors: Motasem Alkayid, Yimin Zhu, Naser El-Sheimy, Lincoln Linlin Xu

Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Subpixel mapping (SPM) of crop types from \SI{250}{m} Moderate Resolution Imaging Spectroradiometer (MODIS) time series to field-scale Agriculture and Agri-Food Canada (AAFC) labels supports acreage estimation and agricultural monitoring. Three difficulties remain: (i)~spectral-temporal coupling and mixed pixels that break direct coarse-to-fine classifiers; (ii)~intra-class endmember variability that a single spectrum per crop cannot capture; and (iii)~uniform refinement on the high-resolution (HR) grid, which treats every subpixel the same, so most of the effort goes to easy pixels while errors remain in the minority class. UnMixMamba addresses them as follows. For~(i), a temporal-spectral-spatial decoupled (TSSD) factorization estimates simplex fractions and drives AAFC classification from the same mixture field, instead of mapping low-resolution (LR) observations directly to HR logits.
