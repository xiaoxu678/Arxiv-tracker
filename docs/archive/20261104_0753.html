Pixel-UMI: Learning Environment-Aware Whole-Body Manipulation from Sensorized Human Demonstrations
Authors: Jason Jingzhou Liu, Tony Tao, Deepak Pathak, Ruslan Salakhutdinov
First: 2026-11-02T18:59:32+00:00 · Latest: 2026-11-02T18:59:32+00:00
Comments: Video results are available at https://pixel-robot.com/pixel-umi
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Sensorized human demonstrations offer scalable data for mobile manipulation, but embodiment differences complicate policy learning. We present Pixel-UMI, a pipeline for learning environment-aware whole-body policies from human data. We first introduce Environment-Aware Retargeting (EAR), which combines reconstructed scene geometry with demonstrated head and gripper trajectories to determine robot posture and generate collision-aware joint and base targets. Delay-calibrated action assignment converts these trajectories into actions. However, kinematic differences can leave residual pose errors, creating viewpoint mismatches between human demonstrations and robot observations. We therefore introduce Retargeting-Error-Aware Masking (REAM), which uses these errors during training to reduce reliance on mismatched views at deployment. Finally, because estimated actions cannot fully capture contact-rich interactions, we collect on-policy human corrections through DAgger to improve performance. We evaluate Pixel-UMI on five challenging tasks spanning articulated-object manipulation, deformable-object handling, constrained-space reaching, whole-body collision avoidance, and contact-rich bimanual coordination, achieving 87.0% average task progress. Progressive Multi-Scale Super-Resolution via Cascaded Bicubic Anchors and Inter-Scale Mutual Refinement
Authors: Chun-An Lin, Yu-Chia Chang, Chi-Mao Fan, Yen-Chieh Ouyang, Tsung-Jung Liu
First: 2026-11-02T16:28:38+00:00 · Latest: 2026-11-02T16:28:38+00:00

Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2 · Code3
Abstract
Super-resolution networks are trained for one magnification factor, so several output resolutions require several models. We propose PMS-SR, a progressive single-input multiple-output network that produces x2, x4, and x8 results in one forward pass, with a Transformer backbone on the low-resolution grid. In Stage 1, each scale is predicted as a residual over the detached bicubic upsampling of the previous scale's prediction. With Dirac and zero initialization, training starts exactly from the bicubic cascade, and each head adds only the band the previous scale lacks. In Stage 2, inter-scale mutual refinement couples the scales in both directions through space-to-depth feedback, deformable alignment, and per-pixel fusion, so x8 features refine the x2 output. Trained on DF2K, the 7.95M-parameter model achieves a higher PSNR than every compared multi-scale method at x4 and x8 on all five standard benchmarks. At x8, it outperforms the x8-specific DRN-L on all of them, leads all compared methods on Urban100, and is within 0.13 dB of the best result elsewhere. Against three single-scale SwinIR-M models, PMS-SR uses 22% of the parameters, runs 2.9x faster on BSD100 inputs, and its outputs agree 6.7-12.4 dB more closely. Code and pretrained models will be released at https://github.com/julian135707/PMS-SR upon acceptance.

Empirical Ciphertext Distinguishability Testing of KEMs and Hybrid Encryption via Deep Learning
Authors: Simon Calderon, Niklas Johansson, Onur Günlü
First: 2026-04-08T11:06:05+00:00 · Latest: 2026-11-02T16:05:43+00:00
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
We model indistinguishability under chosen-plaintext attack (IND-CPA) games as binary classification tasks for deep neural networks (DNNs). We apply this methodology to the public-key encryption (PKE) schemes used to construct PQC key encapsulation mechanisms (KEMs) such as ML-KEM and HQC, and to cascaded symmetric algorithms, demonstrating methodological generality. Moreover, we present a novel extension of this approach to empirical distinguishability testing of hybrid KEMs. For IND-CPA-secure schemes and their combinations, no DNN achieves accuracy significantly different from random guessing (measured via Bonferroni-corrected binomial tests), consistent with theoretical security guarantees. For insecure schemes, such as plain RSA, AES-CTR and AES-CBC with IV or counter reuse, or RC4, the DNNs classify the ciphertexts better than random guessing. Thus, this framework is shown to serve as a practical, scheme-agnostic tool for flagging distinguishable ciphertexts in both individual and composed constructions.

Abstract
Parametric human body models such as the Skinned Multi-Person Linear (SMPL) model provide compact representations of human shape and pose, but their reconstructions remain affected by both errors in estimated latent parameters and limitations in the decoder's expressive capacity. We propose a framework for refining these reconstructions by exploiting the geometric discrepancy between an initial decoded surface and a target mesh, without requiring vertex correspondences between them. The framework comprises three complementary approaches. Latent-VaRefine uses varifold-distance gradients to correct the estimated latent code while preserving the original parametric representation. Vertex-VaRefine processes multiscale varifold-gradient features with a DiffusionNet backbone to predict vertex displacements in a canonical pose, yielding an approximately pose-equivariant refinement pipeline. ElaOpt directly optimizes surface deformations under elastic regularization and normal-direction constraints.

Abstract
Most image-based 3D reconstructors primarily recover visible surfaces, producing partial point clouds with large holes and leaving unobserved geometry unresolved. To address this limitation, we introduce AnchorComplete, a framework that reconstructs complete scene meshes from partial point clouds while preserving observed geometry and supporting scenes of varying scales. Building on TRELLIS.2, our method combines partial-point conditioning, which integrates global scene tokens with local spatial evidence to anchor generation to the input, and metric-scale coarse-to-fine generation, which progressively refines scene geometry within fixed-size windows while maintaining global structural consistency. Experiments on Replica and SCRREAM demonstrate that AnchorComplete consistently outperforms strong baselines in complete-scene reconstruction, particularly under sparse-view settings.

Abstract
Variable fonts enable continuous variation of glyph geometry along semantic design axes such as weight, width, slant, and optical size. However, constructing a variable font from a static font remains a labor-intensive process requiring expert typographic design and manual specification of glyph variation data. We introduce NIV (Neural Axis Variations), a method that automatically converts a static font into a fully functional variable font. Given glyph outlines and a set of desired design axes, NIV predicts per-point displacements. The model operates directly on vector glyph geometry and employs a novel Property Embedding mechanism that captures interactions between multiple axes, enabling consistent multi-axis variation within a unified framework. We train NIV on a newly constructed dataset derived from variable Google Fonts, comprising over one million variation tuples. The resulting model generalizes across unseen code points, unseen font styles, high-complexity CJK glyphs, and even out-of-distribution handwriting inputs. The generated outputs are standard variable font files supporting continuous interpolation via existing rendering engines. To facilitate research, we release the dataset, the complete training and inference implementation, and trained models at https://github.com/ndvbd/NIV. Beyond typography, our approach demonstrates how structured geometric objects with continuous parametric variation can be synthesized using neural deformations.

CFD-driven symbolic regression of RANS transition models
Authors: Louenas Zemmour, Xavier Gloerfelt, Paola Cinnella
First: 2026-11-02T14:34:00+00:00 · Latest: 2026-11-02T14:34:00+00:00
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Laminar-turbulent transition governs skin friction, heat transfer and losses in turbomachinery, yet Reynolds-averaged Navier-Stokes (RANS) transition models rely on empirical correlations and hand-calibrated activation functions whose structural deficiencies cannot be resolved by recalibration. We propose a CFD-driven sparse symbolic regression framework to learn interpretable, closed-form corrections to the production and destruction activation functions in the intermittency equation of the local, Galilean-invariant $k$-$ω$-$γ$ model of Ge et al. Each correction combines a polynomial of a local pressure-gradient parameter, controlling amplitude and sign, with a bounded exponential of local flow features, controlling spatial support. This construction bounds the corrections without ad hoc limiters. Coefficients are identified by gradient-free optimization with the RANS solver in the loop, accelerated by POD-Kriging field surrogates, using mean-velocity data from wall-resolved large-eddy simulations (LES) of the ERCOFTAC T3 flat-plate cases. Elastic-net hyperparameters are selected automatically through bi-level optimization with leave-one-case-out cross-validation. The resulting model retains 11 of 21 candidate terms, compared with 20 under conventional grid-search tuning, while achieving comparable or slightly better accuracy on training and unseen cases. Both approaches recover the same leading-order correction. Applied without retraining to the transonic LS89 turbine cascade, far beyond the training envelope, the augmented model remains numerically robust and improves heat-transfer predictions at all four operating points, reducing errors by up to 70% where the baseline predicts premature transition under high freestream turbulence. Residual errors in transition onset and length highlight the limits of training on flat-plate data alone.

Abstract
High-fidelity head avatar reconstruction plays a crucial role in AR/VR, gaming, and multimedia content creation. Recent advances in 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) have demonstrated effectiveness in modeling complex geometry with real-time rendering capability and are now widely used in high-fidelity head avatar reconstruction tasks. However, existing 3DGS-based methods still face significant challenges in capturing fine-grained facial expressions and preserving local texture continuity, especially in highly deformable regions. To mitigate these limitations, we propose a novel 3DGS-based framework termed EAvatar for head reconstruction that is both expression-aware and deformation-aware. Experimental results demonstrate that our method produces more accurate and visually coherent head reconstructions with improved expression controllability and detail fidelity.

Generative diffusion in long-range correlated landscapes: Speciation cascade and the foam structure of the score
Authors: Jerome Garnier-Brun, Giulio Biroli, Marc Mézard, Jean-Philippe Bouchaud
First: 2026-11-02T11:22:51+00:00 · Latest: 2026-11-02T11:22:51+00:00
Comments: 25 pages, 7 figures
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
While state of the art for image and video generation, the fundamental reasons for the effectiveness of generative diffusion remain unclear. In particular, the observed advantage of following an entire reverse process with respect to single-shot or autoregressive methods, and for what type of data it may hold, is not well understood. In this work, we aim to better understand the role of the reverse process by considering a complex yet analytically tractable data model, the Gibbs measure of a high-dimensional Gaussian random potential with long-range correlations. Focusing on the exact score, we show that the speciation threshold, in which a trajectory commits to one class of the data, becomes a speciation window, in which the reverse process progressively resolves the Parisi tree of pure states of the full replica-symmetry breaking measure, committing to ever finer branches down to a single pure state. Importantly, these commitments take place at noise levels where the noisy sample distinguishes the pure states only through an energy of order one, beyond the reach of standard replica saddle points. Constructing a Larkin argument suited to such $O(1)$ energy gaps, we describe the score as a foam, with slab-like cells separated by shock-like boundaries. In the variance-exploding process we consider, this window is followed by a shrinkage of the samples towards the data support, and finally by reconstruction within the selected pure state. The denoising error of the exact score follows these stages, dropping during the speciation window with a profile set by the Parisi function alone, and then within the selected state. We believe that the generative foam we describe, and in particular the boundaries between cells, could play an important role in determining where the errors of trained diffusion models redistribute the mass of the generated samples when the data are long-range correlated.

While state of the art for image and video generation, the fundamental reasons for the effectiveness of generative diffusion remain unclear.

Abstract
Humanoid rigging for modern characters with complex garments remains challenging due to the need for dense auxiliary skeletons and precise skinning control. Existing automatic methods primarily recover rigs from geometry, providing limited artist control over auxiliary articulation and often lacking explicit semantic or kinematic-chain structure for dense garment bones, leading to instability and weight bleeding. We present TailorRig, a framework that reformulates garment-aware humanoid rigging as a structured, controllable full-rig generation problem. This formulation shifts the goal from automatic rig recovery to artist-driven rig authoring, where both body-template compatibility and auxiliary articulation are explicit design choices. TailorRig exposes two explicit axes of artist control within a unified framework: a user-specified body skeleton template that preserves body-rig compatibility, and artist-provided semantic labels that drive an autoregressive generator to synthesize requested category-specific auxiliary bone chains. To robustly assign skinning weights under dense skeletal configurations, we further introduce a hierarchical chain-to-bone formulation: vertices are first associated to a kinematic chain, and bone weights are then refined within that chain, reducing weight bleeding between spatially adjacent but semantically unrelated structures. We evaluate TailorRig on a diverse set of characters and introduce Human-Mix, a large-scale dataset of over 38,000 humanoid models curated for garment-aware rigging. Experiments show that TailorRig improves skeleton accuracy, deformation stability, controllability, and practical usability over existing methods.

Authors: Kai Shao, Ling Jian, Liming Liu, Serguey V. Bogomolov, Xinmin Wang

Abstract
Multiproduct pipelines transport refined products to delivery stations in sequential batches, and their detailed scheduling must meet market demand while respecting nonlinear hydraulic constraints. Retaining the full hydraulic nonlinearity yields a mixed-integer nonlinear program that is computationally intractable, while the linearization commonly adopted for solver-based optimization degrades hydraulic accuracy without resolving the heavy computational burden. This paper develops an action-decoupled proximal policy optimization (PPO) framework that efficiently produces detailed schedules while preserving hydraulic fidelity. The scheduling process is cast as a semi-Markov decision process (SMDP) with flexible action durations. To handle the discrete-continuous hybrid action space, a cascaded dual-actor architecture decouples the discrete operating duration from the continuous flow decisions, eliminating the gradient interference that destabilizes conventional coupled-action policies and thereby yielding high-quality schedules far more efficiently. Validated on a real-world multiproduct pipeline in China and benchmarked against a single-actor baseline and a linearized MILP solved by Gurobi, the proposed framework achieves delivery accuracy close to the exact optimum while substantially improving flow rate stability and substantially reducing solution time.

Authors: Shuiwen Shen, Jinhua Zhang, Chenlei Hou, Peng Shi, Yong Han, Bo Gao, Xiaoxiao Na, Zhihui Lin, Pengtao Du
First: 2026-11-02T04:02:13+00:00 · Latest: 2026-11-02T04:02:13+00:00

Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Online Active Learning (OAL) aims to extend deep learning from static inference after offline training to continuous adaptation during real-time interaction with a changing physical environment. This transition is essential for safety-critical applications, but it raises a central theoretical question: whether the online parameter update process remains stable when scaled to multilayer neural architectures. This paper develops a stability-focused OAL framework. The framework uses prediction error as a feedback signal to update a structured deep model, thereby linking online learning with Lyapunov-based energy minimization. We show that prediction error convergence alone is insufficient for global parameter convergence because multilayer networks may admit high-dimensional manifolds of equivalent output solutions. The analysis therefore separates output error regulation, internal zero dynamics, interlayer coupling, and parameter convergence. Persistency of excitation and active damping are then used to remove the residual null-space modes that remain after output error convergence. A cascaded Lyapunov analysis establishes asymptotic stability of the overall OAL learning process, while monotonic activation functions and diffeomorphic layer mappings provide the uniqueness condition for the target parameter trajectory. The resulting proof provides a theoretical basis for applying online adaptive learning to large-scale neural systems in nonstationary and safety-critical settings.

Abstract

Abstract

Generative forecasters may produce visually plausible samples, but it is hard to tell whether they sample the right conditional distribution, since trajectory data contain a single future for each initial state.
