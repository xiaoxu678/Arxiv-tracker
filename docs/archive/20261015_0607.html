RoboSimStudio: An Interactive Toolkit for Rapid Real2Sim of Robot Manipulation Tasks
Authors: Kaiyuan Chen, Shuangyu Xie, Uksang Yoo, Xinyue Zhu, Cael Magner, Alvin Zhu, Daehwa Kim, Eric Heiden, Erwin Coumans, Miles Macklin, Spencer Huang, Ken Goldberg
First: 2026-10-13T17:55:14+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-13T17:55:14+00:00
Comments: 13 pages, 7 figures, 1 table. Project website: https://robosimstudio.github.io
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2 · Project1
Abstract
It is difficult to accurately model the physics of the interaction between a robot and the objects it manipulates, including contact mechanics, coupled contact dynamics, deformations, stiffness and friction. We present RoboSimStudio (RSS), an interactive toolkit that makes choices of solver, contact model, and physical parameters explicit and editable, allowing both humans and coding agents to iteratively refine simulations based on observed physical behavior. We implement RSS with the open Newton physics simulation system and describe experiments with human-guided real2sim using (1) visual evaluation, (2) force-feedback and (3) agentic Real2Sim2Real. Code, video and additional information can be found on the project website.

Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Raw image intensities are a poor surrogate for similarity across images in correspondence problems. Further, while auxiliary segmentation labels can provide semantic identification and improve matching, they can assign only coarse identities to fine-grained anatomy and often cover only a subset of structures, particularly in whole-body settings. We present an approach that instead establishes correspondence using complementary representations of anatomy and demonstrate its utility for longitudinal whole-body PET/CT registration in the Learn2Reg 2026 PSMAReg Challenge. Our 3D foundation model, anatomix, is used to derive fine-grained, per-voxel semantic representations that are consistent across volumes, and we further employ off-the-shelf segmentation models to obtain coarse anatomical identities. Together, these representations drive a GPU-accelerated iterative registration solver (FireANTs) in a hybrid multistage framework: the first stage estimates alignment from dense multi-channel features, after which a second refines the deformation using both anatomix- and segmentation-based losses. Lastly, because registration should not introduce non-biological tumor volume changes across time, we develop lesion-specific component-wise volume preservation schemes. Without any task-specific training, our system won first place in the PSMAReg Challenge leaderboard. Code and interactive visualizations are available at http://www.neeldey.com/reps-for-reg.

RadarGSOcc: Doppler-Aware 3D Semantic Occupancy Prediction Using 4D Radar Tensor with Geometric-Semantic Feature Extraction
Authors: Christofel Rio Goenawan, Dong-Hee Paek, Seung-Hyun Kong
First: 2026-10-13T16:40:59+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-13T16:40:59+00:00

Abstract
3D semantic occupancy prediction is crucial for autonomous driving, but camera-based methods degrade in low-light conditions and LiDAR-based methods are sensitive to adverse weather. 4D radar is more robust, yet its sparsity and the high cost of radar tensor processing limit its use. We propose Radar Geometric-Semantic Occupancy Prediction (RadarGSOcc), an efficient framework that directly processes 4D radar tensors by separately extracting geometric and semantic features, including Doppler information, with a deformable attention-based geometric refinement module as its key component. We also introduce the K-Radar 3DSemOcc benchmark, the first large-scale benchmark for 3D semantic occupancy prediction using 4D radar tensors, built on the K-Radar dataset with refined LiDAR-based annotations. RadarGSOcc predicts 15 classes within a 51.2m range and achieves 38.2% IoU, 7.6% mIoU, and 25.5% weighted mIoU, outperforming RadarOcc by 17% relative (25.5 vs. 21.9) in weighted mIoU while using fewer parameters and lower computation. It substantially improves truck prediction while trading off car accuracy, outperforms camera-based approaches, and narrows the gap with LiDAR-based methods. To assess robustness without per-frame ground truth in adverse weather, we further propose a novel proxy metric, Environment-Based KL Divergence (EBKL), under which 4D radar proves a reliable and efficient modality for 3D semantic occupancy prediction. The dataset and code will be publicly available.

Abstract
Reinforcement learning (RL) has emerged as the primary paradigm for optimizing Large Language Model (LLM) agents, yet efficient critic-free algorithms (e.g., GRPO) suffer from a fundamental uniform credit assignment dilemma that broadcasts trajectory-level advantages equally to all actions. Our analysis reveals that this uniformity inherently reinforces structurally invalid actions in preferred trajectories and ignores the downstream cascading influence of early pivotal deviations in unpreferred ones. To resolve this without introducing critic overhead, we propose Progress-Aware Advantage Shaping (ProAS), a plug-and-play framework that leverages a dynamically evolving Agentic Reference Graph (ARG) to achieve fine-grained credit assignment via Masking Unreasonable Incentives (MUI) and Asymmetric Penalty Redistribution (APR). Extensive experiments across Qwen2.5-1.5B-Instruct and Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct demonstrate that ProAS universally elevates mainstream group-based baselines to new state-of-the-art performance, achieving peak success rates of 91.9\% and 94.3\% on ALFWorld, alongside 66.7\% and 70.8\% on WebShop, respectively. Furthermore, it accelerates training convergence by up to $2.44\times$ while maintaining strong robustness against imperfect or distilled ARGs. Codes: https://github.com/LINs-lab/ProAS.

Abstract
A patient's pathology case spans tens of slides from distinct specimens and anatomical sites, each carrying non-redundant, site-specific evidence. Forming a patient-level conclusion therefore requires locating the slides relevant to a given question and integrating complementary findings across them. However, existing pathology agents analyze one slide at a time and describe morphology rather than measure it, leaving evidence acquisition across a patient's specimens and patient-level quantification underexplored. To address this gap, we propose PlanPath, an agentic framework for patient-level pathology visual question answering (VQA). PlanPath turns the question into an exploration plan that specifies the evidence required, routes to the slides likely to carry it, and drives coarse-to-fine acquisition from tissue architecture down to individual cells. Tissue- and cell-level tools supply the counts, proportions, and extents, which PlanPath aggregates across slides into a traceable answer. To enable evaluation, we build two patient-level VQA datasets for renal and gastric cancer, averaging 15 WSIs per patient, with pathologist-verified question-answer pairs derived from diagnostic reports. Experimental results show that PlanPath outperforms the strongest baseline by 9.4% and 12.1% in overall accuracy on the two datasets and further improves existing slide-level agents when plugged into our framework. The dataset and code are available at: https://github.com/JSGe-AI/PlanPath_1.

End-to-End Spoken Grammatical Error Correction
Authors: Mengjie Qian, Rao Ma, Stefano Bannò, Mark J. F. Gales, Kate M. Knill
First: 2025-06-23T11:40:04+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-13T16:12:12+00:00

Abstract
Grammatical Error Correction (GEC) and feedback play a vital role in supporting second language (L2) learners, educators, and examiners. While written GEC is well-established, spoken GEC (SGEC), aiming to provide feedback based on learners' speech, poses additional challenges due to disfluencies, transcription errors, and the lack of structured input. SGEC systems typically follow a cascaded pipeline consisting of Automatic Speech Recognition (ASR), disfluency detection, and GEC, making them vulnerable to error propagation across modules. This work examines an End-to-End (E2E) framework for SGEC and feedback generation, highlighting challenges and possible solutions when developing these systems. Cascaded, partial-cascaded and E2E architectures are compared, all built on the Whisper foundation model. A challenge for E2E systems is the scarcity of GEC labeled spoken data. To address this, an automatic pseudo-labeling framework is examined, increasing the training data from 77 to over 2500 hours. To improve the accuracy of the SGEC system, additional contextual information, exploiting the ASR output, is investigated. Candidate feedback of their mistakes is an essential step to improving performance. In E2E systems the SGEC output must be compared with an estimate of the fluent transcription to obtain the feedback. To improve the precision of this feedback, a novel reference alignment process is proposed that aims to remove hypothesised edits that results from fluent transcription errors. Finally, these approaches are combined with an edit confidence estimation approach, to exclude low-confidence edits. Experiments on the in-house Linguaskill (LNG) corpora and the publicly available Speak & Improve (S&I) corpus show that the proposed approaches significantly boost E2E SGEC performance.

Forecasting Without Full Identification: Reliability and Computational Cost in Clot-Growth Simulation

Abstract
We forecast clot growth from sparse early measurements with incomplete biochemical information. Using 1,000 simulated trajectories, we compare inverse search, which weights candidate inputs by their agreement with early observations, with a direct multilayer perceptron (MLP) predicting future clot size. Inverse search is more accurate when tissue factor alone, or tissue factor and fibrinogen, are unknown. When all five inputs are unknown, direct prediction achieves 2.16% pooled relative $L_2$ forecast error, outperforming search with 8,192 candidates using a forward MLP (3.25%) or latent neural ordinary differential equation model (3.36%). A conditional error bound separates candidate disagreement, weight on poor early fits, surrogate error, and candidate coverage. Increasing randomized quasi-Monte Carlo candidate budgets from 512 to 32,768 reduces numerical variation. A covariance score ranks additional assays by expected forecast value while accounting for measurement noise. At 10% range-scaled assay noise, incorporating assay uncertainty reduces forecast error from about 5% to below 3% for both surrogates. An independent reaction cascade verifies candidate integration against tensor quadrature, with mean normalized forecast-integration error below $10^{-5}$ at 32,768 candidates. These results demonstrate accurate clot-growth forecasting without full biochemical identification and support selecting prediction strategies and additional measurements according to accuracy, reliability, and computational cost.

Abstract
Rapid advances in artificial intelligence-generated content (AIGC) have significantly promoted using text-to-image (T2I) models to generate images with specific affective intentions. Abstract
Deep unrolled models (DUM) have become the state-of-the-art for accelerated MRI reconstruction, yet their robustness under domain shift remains a critical barrier to clinical adoption. In this work, we identify coil sensitivity map (CSM) estimation as the primary bottleneck limiting generalization. To address this, we propose UEPS, a novel DUM architecture featuring three key designs: (i) an Unrolled Expanded (UE) design that eliminates CSM dependency by reconstructing each coil independently; (ii) progressive resolution, which leverages k-space-to-image mapping for efficient coarse-to-fine refinement; and (iii) sparse attention tailored to MRI's 1D undersampling nature. Authors: Tingjun Li, Yuxi Wang, Guoqiang Zhong, Zhaoxiang Zhang

Abstract
Dynamic 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) provides an efficient framework for dynamic scene reconstruction. Most existing approaches model scene dynamics with deterministic deformation fields, assuming each Gaussian follows a distinct geometric trajectory over time. This assumption breaks down underwater, where light scattering, refraction, marine snow and low visibility create substantial physical ambiguity in captured images. We present PUGS, a physics-guided uncertainty model for underwater dynamic 3DGS. PUGS learns inherent uncertainty induced by underwater image formation and leverages it as a physical signal for dynamic scene modeling. Rather than estimating a single deterministic displacement, it parameterizes each Gaussian's motion as a probability distribution with expected deformation and physical uncertainty. We adopt stochastic deformation optimization to sample Gaussian trajectories via local reparameterization, exploring multiple physically plausible solutions during training. We further propose uncertainty stochastic annealing and a dual-path self-consistency distillation objective, converting stochastic exploration to deterministic reconstruction while preserving temporal coherence. Extensive experiments on underwater dynamic datasets and physics-aware benchmarks demonstrate superior rendering quality, temporal consistency and reconstruction robustness. Our method achieves 1.16 dB PSNR gain over the strongest baseline on UVEB and 3.99 dB on DRUVA.

Abstract
Clinical screening for diabetic retinopathy proceeds in stages, yet most automated systems grade fundus images with a single black-box network. Three gaps remain: (i) lesions differ widely in size and contrast, but segmenters typically apply the same blocks to all; (ii) ungradable images are usually excluded or treated as an extra class, and lesion-aware graders seldom test whether lesions drive the grade; (iii) staged systems often report each stage only on the images reaching it, leaving propagated errors unmeasured. HierarchiRetina, a screening, segmentation and grading cascade with inspectable stage outputs, addresses each gap. First, HSMoE-AUNet is a U-Net whose skip, bottleneck and decoder blocks are sparse mixtures of heterogeneous experts, with lesion-matched types, counts and losses; SwinHRUNetPP, a dual-encoder vessel segmenter, uses a field-of-view-restricted loss. Second, LG-DRG separates gradability from ordinal severity and lets lesion masks query the image through gated cross-attention. Third, an end-to-end evaluation counts screening misses as errors and traces each error to its stage. Pooled Dice ranged from 0.184 (microaneurysms) to 0.579 (exudates); vessel Dice was 0.731. On 58,689 held-out images from six datasets, end-to-end quadratic weighted kappa was 0.804, with referral sensitivity 0.832 at specificity 0.947, and the screening gate caused 66% of errors. In ablations, the grade ignored the masks, even with the cross-attention gate started open. On all 4,105 official DDR test images, six-class accuracy was 85.6%, above the best published six-class result we found (83.8%). Staged screening systems should be evaluated end-to-end, with stage attribution and mask ablation, before clinical deployment.

Re-uploading quantum data: a universal function approximator for quantum inputs
Authors: Hyunho Cha, Daniel K. Park, Jungwoo Lee
First: 2025-09-23T01:50:37+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-13T06:28:43+00:00
Comments: 29 pages, 11 figures
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Quantum data re-uploading has proved powerful for classical inputs, where repeatedly encoding features into a small circuit yields universal function approximation. Extending this idea to quantum inputs remains underexplored, as the information contained in a quantum state is not directly accessible in classical form. We propose and analyze a quantum data re-uploading architecture in which a qubit interacts sequentially with fresh copies of an arbitrary input state. The circuit can approximate any bounded continuous function using only one ancilla qubit and single-qubit measurements, provided that sufficiently many copies of the input state and, in general, sufficiently many layers are available. By alternating entangling unitaries with mid-circuit resets of the input register, the architecture realizes a discrete cascade of completely positive and trace-preserving maps, analogous to collision models in open quantum system dynamics. Our framework provides a space-efficient and expressive approach to designing quantum machine learning models that operate directly on quantum data.

Abstract
The design of ergonomic products and workspaces requires a deep understanding of human anatomy and posture. In particular, the head-neck-shoulder region is critical for many applications such as cervical orthoses, helmets, and other head-worn or neck-worn devices. This paper presents a framework that integrates posture neutralization with statistical shape modeling (SSM) for the head-neck-shoulder (HNS) region.
