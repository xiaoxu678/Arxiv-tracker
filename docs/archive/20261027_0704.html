OpenStripe supports open-set identification and spatial stripe profiling in wild Amur tigers
Authors: Yichun Peng, Yansong Li, Jin Hou, Qi Song, Xi Guo, Ning Zhang, Degang Wei, Huang Yang, Yu Guan, Jianping Ge, Lei Bao, Tianming Wang
First: 2026-10-23T16:21:38+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-23T16:21:38+00:00
Comments: 14 pages, 4 figures. Code and nonspatial data: https://github.com/Yichun-Peng/OpenStripe
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2 · Code3
Abstract
Non-invasive camera-trap monitoring of endangered wildlife requires open-set individual identification---grouping field images into identities without predefined classes or a known population size---yet pose deformation and cluttered backgrounds complicate image comparison. Here, we present OpenStripe, a multi-agent framework that reconstructs canonical two-dimensional (2D) tiger torso patterns and encodes four interpretable local stripe structures: breakpoints, bifurcations, irregular structures, and round structures. We evaluated OpenStripe on 100 camera-trap records from 28 wild Amur tigers (Panthera tigris altaica), representing nearly one-third of the documented population in the Northeast China Tiger and Leopard National Park. Combining all four complementary structures enabled automatic identity grouping with 0.90 matched accuracy and a 0.85 adjusted Rand index (ARI), outperforming 49 baseline configurations under the same backend (up to 0.43 accuracy and 0.14 ARI). In addition, results from OpenStripe indicate that stripe similarity in Amur tigers declines with geographic distance and suggest that traits such as fractal dimension and inter-stripe spacing are associated with geographic proximity. OpenStripe thus provides an interpretable representation that connects automated individual grouping with spatial phenotypic profiling in wild populations.

Abstract
Real-world 3D reconstruction pipelines often rely on multi-view images degraded by lossy compression. While compression artifacts have been extensively studied in single-image or video restoration, their impact on multi-view geometry remains largely overlooked. In this work, we identify compression-induced cross-view geometric inconsistency as a critical failure mode, where view-dependent artifacts disrupt feature matching, dense correspondence, camera parameter estimation, and downstream 3D reconstruction tasks such as novel view synthesis. To address this problem, we propose CompMVR, a compression-aware multi-view diffusion restoration framework that jointly refines compressed views while preserving cross-view consistency, and construct compressed datasets for systematic evaluation. Unlike conventional restoration methods that optimize each image independently for 2D perceptual quality, CompMVR leverages learned compression priors and cross-view correspondence to recover visual details while improving geometric reliability across views. Experiments across diverse datasets, codecs, and compression levels demonstrate gains in restoration quality and downstream 3D tasks, including novel-view synthesis, camera pose estimation, and view matching. These results establish compressed multi-view restoration as a distinct problem and highlight its importance for 3D reconstruction under lossy compression. Multilevel Stochastic Plug-and-Play for Sparse-View CT Reconstruction
Authors: Antoine De Paepe, Alexandre Bousse, Dimitris Visvikis
First: 2026-06-23T13:34:30+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-23T15:03:41+00:00
Comments: 13 pages, 7 figures, 3 tables
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2 · Code3
Abstract
Sparse-view computed tomography (SVCT) reduces radiation exposure and acquisition time, but the limited number of projection views makes the reconstruction problem severely ill-posed and leads to streak artifacts when analytical methods are used. Plug-and-Play (PnP) methods combine data fidelity with learned image priors, while stochastic PnP methods use re-noising to reduce the mismatch between denoiser training inputs and reconstruction iterates. However, these methods often require many iterations, which limits their practical efficiency. In this work, we propose multilevel (ML) Stochastic Plug-and-Play (ML-SPnP), a ML stochastic PnP method for SVCT reconstruction formulated on wavelet multiresolution analysis (MRA) approximation spaces. The method combines coarse-to-fine initialization with a finite sequence of ML corrections using resolution-specific denoisers. Under suitable assumptions, the fine-level iterations following the finite ML phase inherit the convergence guarantees of the underlying stochastic PnP algorithm. Experiments on two CT datasets with 20-50 projection views show that ML-SPnP achieves reconstruction quality comparable to the evaluated stochastic PnP methods, while generally requiring less reconstruction time. These results support ML acceleration as an effective strategy for reducing the computational cost of stochastic PnP reconstruction. The code will be made publicly available at https://github.com/antoinedepaepe/ml-spnp.

Let's Do Turbulence Right: In Space and Time!
Authors: Maximilian Plattner, Fabian Paischer, Johannes Brandstetter
First: 2026-10-23T13:26:50+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-23T13:26:50+00:00
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
The three-dimensional Navier-Stokes equations govern fluid turbulence, a chaotic and non-linear physical phenomenon characterized by complex eddies interacting across vast spatial and temporal scales. A numerically accurate modeling of this multi-scale energy cascade requires fully resolving both the fine spatial structures and the continuous temporal evolution of the flow field. In this paper, we leverage massive, time-resolved direct numerical simulations, which exceed 28 TB per run, to assess how numerical choices govern the resolved (3+1)-dimensional physics of turbulent flows. Building on this analysis, we study how neural networks represent the recorded turbulent motion. Our results highlight that spatial and temporal assessments can disagree for both numerical records and learned representations. We therefore call on the machine learning community to treat (3+1)-dimensional turbulence as a four-dimensional phenomenon.

Abstract
Deploying Vision-Language Models for video anomaly detection confronts a fundamental constraint. Their semantic reasoning demands computational budgets incompatible with the scale of real-world video streams. We present Signal-Aware Adaptive Fusion (SAAF), a framework that achieves efficient zero-shot anomaly detection through three key innovations. First, we introduce a dual-signal adaptive sampler operating in semantic embedding space: a delta signal captures sudden events via frame-to-frame cosine distance, while a drift signal detects static and gradual anomalies through deviation from rolling temporal context. Their OR-gated combination with adaptive EMA-based thresholding provides comprehensive anomaly coverage without training data. Second, we propose a threshold-gated cascaded architecture where lightweight frame-level scoring precedes expensive video-level temporal analysis, invoking it only when confidence warrants additional context. Third, we design a signal-aware weighted fusion mechanism that dynamically adapts score combination based on which signal triggered frame selection. Across four benchmarks, SAAF delivers competitive accuracy at substantially lower cost. Among training-free zero-shot methods, SAAF sets a new state of the art on XD-Violence (92.07%), MSAD (86.70%), and ShanghaiTech (80.54%) AUC-ROC, with XD-Violence and MSAD also surpassing supervised baselines. SAAF sustains 44.95, 48.61, and 41.06 FPS on XD-Violence, UCF-Crime, and MSAD, a 2.43$\times$, 2.63$\times$, and 2.22$\times$ throughput improvement over the prior best zero-shot method. Abstract
Photonic crystal fibers (PCFs) underpin critical applications in telecommunications, sensing, and high-power laser delivery, yet their design remains a manual, expert-intensive process that couples geometric intuition with deep waveguide physics. Vision-language models (VLMs) offer a promising path toward automating PCF design, but can they truly reason about photonic physics from microscopy images, or do they merely recognize visual patterns? We introduce PCFBench, the first comprehensive benchmark for evaluating VLMs on the perception, reasoning, and decision steps of the photonic inverse-design loop. PCFBench spans 26 tasks organized into six categories of increasing cognitive demand (Geometry Perception, Physics Understanding, Text Generation, Multi-modal Reasoning, Inverse-Design Decision, and Code Generation), built from over 220K FDTD simulation samples across 18 structurally diverse fiber families. We evaluate both individual VLMs on isolated tasks and multi-agent systems (MAS) on the full sequential pipeline from perception to design. Our results reveal a striking perception-design gap: current models can identify fiber geometry with moderate success, yet consistently fail to translate that visual understanding into physics-aware design actions. Moreover, cascading agents through the pipeline exposes significant error propagation, where upstream perception mistakes compound into downstream design failures. These findings suggest that neither model scaling nor naive agent chaining suffices, and that closing the loop demands explicit physical grounding. PCFBench provides the community with a rigorous, multi-dimensional testbed to measure progress toward AI-assisted photonic engineering. The project is available at https://pcfbench.github.io/.

Abstract
Accurate spatio-temporal cellular traffic analysis is fundamental to the reliable operation of modern mobile networks, as it underpins load forecasting for congestion avoidance and missing-data imputation for continuity despite transmission errors. While deeply connected, forecasting and imputation have historically evolved as separate sub-fields. Existing models are typically task-specific, decouple temporal and spatial modeling, and rely on cascaded designs that accumulate errors and latency. Meanwhile, adapting pre-trained LLMs for this domain often leaves spatial structure under-guided and tends to overfit on limited data. To bridge these gaps, we propose U-STS-LLM, a unified framework built on a partially frozen spatio-temporally attentive (PFSTA) LLM backbone. A Dynamic Spatio-Temporal Attention Bias Generator synthesizes a persistent functional graph with transient nodal states to steer attention, while a lightweight parallel branch yields a stable local estimate fused via a Gated Adaptive Fusion, confining the LLM to residual correction. Trained under a unified multi-task objective, the model attains state-of-the-art results with low trainable-parameter overhead. Extensive experiments on real-world cellular traffic datasets demonstrate the effectiveness and transferability of U-STS-LLM, achieving average RMSE improvements of 23.7% over the strongest baselines in forecasting and 13.1% in imputation, with further gains exceeding 33% in zero-shot transfer to unseen network regions.

Abstract
Text-to-image (T2I) diffusion models have advanced notably, but still fail to satisfy prompt conditions, resulting in attribute mismatches and missing objects. These errors normally fall into two categories: concept loss, when an object or attribute (i.e., concept) goes missing, and concept confusion, when an attribute is assigned to the wrong object or multiple objects blend together. Recent studies suggest that these challenges are caused by the limited capacity of CLIP text encoder to capture fine-grained semantic details. Although several methods have been proposed, concept loss and concept confusion persist in recent T2I models when handling multi-object, multi-attribute prompts. In this paper, we conduct an embedding-level analysis to develop a training-free, inference-time solution for concept loss and concept confusion across CLIP-based T2I models. We find that (1) concepts mentioned later exhibit higher embedding entropy, reflecting less concentrated representations that are more often associated with concept loss, and (2) the first CLIP attention layer captures the strength of binding between each object and its attribute. Guided by our findings, we introduce TIE, a method that improves semantic alignment through a single text-embedding update. TIE addresses concept loss via entropy-aware singular value amplification and resolves concept confusion through interpolation-extrapolation binding based on CLIP attention scores, all in a training-free manner. Extensive experiments demonstrate that TIE enhances semantic fidelity in multi-concept scenarios with minimal sampling overhead. The project page is available at https://hong-sujung.github.io/TIE/.

A Regularity Condition Under Which a CPAB Transformation is a Diffeomorphism
Authors: Amit Aflalo, Oren Freifeld
First: 2026-10-23T08:59:16+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-23T08:59:16+00:00
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Continuous Piecewise-Affine Based (CPAB) transformations, introduced by Freifeld et al. (2015, 2017), are flows obtained by integrating continuous piecewise-affine (CPA) velocity fields. These expressive transformations have proven useful in applications including nonlinear temporal warping and trainable activation functions in 1D, as well as nonrigid spatial deformations in 2D and 3D. While computational costs were not an issue in 1D, they originally limited the practical expressiveness of CPAB transformations in higher dimensions, especially in 3D. Our recent work (Aflalo et al., 2026) removed this bottleneck, enabling efficient optimization with much finer tessellations and substantially greater expressiveness. There is, however, a mathematical subtlety that was overlooked in the original CPAB papers (Freifeld et al., 2015, 2017). CPAB transformations are always bi-Lipschitz homeomorphisms, implying that both the transformations and their inverses are differentiable almost everywhere. They are not, however, necessarily diffeomorphisms. This note remedies the omission by identifying sufficient regularity conditions under which a CPAB transformation is guaranteed to be a diffeomorphism. We explain how differentiability can fail and how the proposed conditions prevent this failure. In 1D and 2D, we show that these conditions can be easily enforced and, for commonly used tessellations, hold with probability one under standard sampling assumptions. We expect analogous behavior in 3D.

Abstract
Point-supervised Temporal Action Localization (PTAL) aims to localize action instances using only a single annotated timestamp for each instance. Such sparse supervision offers limited guidance for modeling the structural organization of an action instance. Existing PTAL methods primarily infer action regions from classification responses, leaving the structural organization of multiple action-related parts within each instance insufficiently modeled. To address this problem, we propose DeformStruct, an instance-level deformable structure modeling framework for PTAL. DeformStruct progressively constructs the structure of each action instance through three stages. First, Structural Anchor Mining discovers multiple anchors from annotated points and action-responsive regions, providing structural nodes grounded in the current instance. Second, Deformable Part Alignment associates latent structural parts with these anchors through deformation-aware matching, allowing their temporal supports to adapt to instance-dependent variations. Third, Relational Structural Organization explicitly models the relative temporal relations among the aligned parts, forming a relational representation that characterizes how different action-related parts are organized together. Extensive experiments on THUMOS14, ActivityNet-v1.3, GTEA, and BEOID demonstrate consistent improvements in point-supervised temporal localization and validate the effectiveness of deformable structural modeling.

Abstract
Remote sensing change detection identifies land-cover changes from bi-temporal images. Most existing methods follow a one-shot dense prediction paradigm, directly regressing a change mask from fused features, but overlook the intrinsic frequency characteristics of change patterns. We propose Freq-RemoteVAR, a frequency autoregressive framework that reformulates change detection as structured generation in the frequency domain. Instead of predicting the change mask in one step, we introduce next-frequency prediction, where change information is progressively generated from coarse to fine. We design a frequency-aware mask tokenization strategy that decomposes change supervision into multi-frequency token targets via Fourier transformation and quantization. To mitigate unreliable signals, a Change-quality Control module adaptively modulates generation through dynamic normalization, attention biasing, and spatial offset adjustment, suppressing pseudo-change responses and improving robustness. Experiments on CDD, GZ-CD, and LEVIR-CD show that Freq-RemoteVAR consistently outperforms existing methods, especially in challenging scenarios with complex appearance variations and noisy disturbances.

Transient Amplification in Cascaded Gradient Methods: Exact Minimax Laws and Tracking without Resonance
Authors: Ahanaf Hasan Ariq, Davide Wiest
First: 2026-06-01T20:42:04+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-23T02:46:28+00:00
Comments: 16 pages, 4 figures, 2 tables
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2 · Code3
Abstract
Leader-follower gradient iterations, including bilevel and two-time-scale methods, can exhibit large finite-time excursions despite asymptotic stability. For cascaded iterations with block matrix J = [[A,0],[C,D]], symmetric diagonal blocks of norm at most gamma < 1, and coupling norm at most c, we prove an exact, dimension-free minimax formula: at every horizon t, the worst-case amplification equals the spectral norm of [[gamma^t,0],[c t gamma^(t-1),gamma^t]], attained by an aligned two-dimensional quadratic. Consequently, the worst transient is on the order of 1 + c/(1-gamma), and eigenvalues alone cannot predict finite-time gain. Under martingale noise, the aligned system has stationary follower variance sigma^2 c^2 (1+gamma^2)/(1-gamma^2)^3, establishing cubic inverse-gap scaling. For quadratic followers, the coupling is linked to follower sensitivity R by C = (I-D)R. When the leader is isotropic and the follower map is positive semidefinite, amplification is at most 1 plus the operator norm of R, independently of the spectral gaps. A look-ahead Newton update eliminates follower memory and is minimax-optimal among linear updates that track the leader exactly. Experiments validate the bounds and show that accelerated or inexact follower solvers can exceed the sensitivity bound when their effective maps have negative eigenvalues. Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2 · Project1
Abstract
Static neural point reconstructions capture a subject at high fidelity from posed images. Given such a reconstruction, we aim to animate it to follow a monocular fixed-viewpoint driving video of the subject, whether captured or produced by image-to-video (I2V) generation, and to recover a rigged, re-posable 3D asset. Existing methods deform Gaussian splats through direct linear blend skinning (LBS) or mesh proxies, both of which are prone to joint-boundary artifacts under articulation, even with per-primitive corrections. We trace the artifact to the representation: each splat carries an individual shape calibrated in the canonical pose to tile with its neighbours. Under LBS each splat can be moved, rotated, or rescaled but never bent, so the canonical tiling comes apart at joint boundaries. Proximity attention point rendering (PAPR) instead carries no per-primitive shape; each pixel is recomposed at render time from the deformed primitives' positions, so the surface re-forms naturally with the articulation. We present RigPAPR, which auto-rigs a static PAPR cloud and drives it under direct LBS from a single fixed-viewpoint video, without a category template or a pose-dependent correction network. RigPAPR is competitive with the strongest baseline at the supervised view and outperforms splatting-based and mesh-based baselines at novel views, by 3~dB PSNR on synthetic subjects and about 2~dB on real-world subjects, with cleaner joint-boundary renderings.

Abstract
In decoder-only (causal) transformers, the computation graph created by causal masking routes information through both direct-path attention and indirect paths formed by intermediate tokens. We denote these indirect paths between token pairs as their runways. We argue that certain failure modes of causal transformers as observed by a growing body of recent works are likely exacerbated by a misalignment between these two information propagation modes. We formalize runway cascade as a phenomenon whereby this misalignment results in redundancies and irrelevant information cascading to token representations despite adequately learned attention patterns. As a solution, we propose runway-aware rewiring as a more explicit way of incorporating runway context directly into each token's direct-path attention. This mechanism re-wires the attention pattern for each token based on a summary of its runway landscape, enabling awareness of accumulating representational influences and allowing for more balanced information propagation. Empirically, our rewired transformer results in steady improvements in general language modeling as well as noticeably stronger information retrieval and extrapolation abilities compared to standard transformers.

Abstract
Large language models (LLMs) differ in capabilities and costs, motivating routing methods that adaptively choose a model for each query. These methods rely on performance prediction: given a query, estimate whether a model will answer it correctly. Most routing methods train separate predictors of candidate models' performance to guide model selection. Our work shows that performance prediction only beats a simple baseline when the candidate model is suboptimal on the task or the performance predictor adds significant capacity relative to the candidate model. Specifically, we take a model's self-confidence, the probability it assigns to its chosen answer, as a baseline and study how much a performance predictor can improve on it. First, we show that any performance predictor that outperforms a model's self-confidence can be used to improve the model's loss: the predictor's advantage transfers exactly in log loss through recalibration. Experiments with 16 language models on four benchmarks show that predictors gain a larger advantage over self-confidence on more suboptimal models. This advantage shrinks as models improve through finetuning. Cascading exhibits the same two patterns: predictor-based routing offers larger gains over confidence-based routing for more suboptimal models, and these gains diminish after finetuning.
