Post-hoc Disentanglement of Textual and Acoustic Features in Pre-trained Speech Encoders
Authors: Hosein Mohebbi, Grzegorz Chrupała, Willem Zuidema, Afra Alishahi, Ivan Titov
Venue: ACL
First: 2024-10-03T22:48:04+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-19T17:12:57+00:00
Comments: Accepted to Transactions of ACL (TACL)
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Pre-trained speech models build entangled internal representations, which capture a variety of features (e.g., pitch, loudness, syntax, or semantics of an utterance) in a distributed encoding. This entanglement makes it difficult to track how such representations rely on textual versus acoustic information when used in downstream applications, limiting their interpretability and transparency. In this paper, we explore an approach to post-hoc disentanglement that builds on the Information Bottleneck principle. We take representations from pre-trained speech encoders as input, and separate them into two distinct components in a cascaded fashion: first we learn a vector encoding content (i.e., what can be transcribed as text), and nothing else; second, we learn a vector encoding all complementary acoustic features relevant to a target task. We apply and evaluate our proposed setup to emotion recognition and speaker identification target tasks, quantifying the relative contribution of textual and acoustic features at each model layer. Finally, we use the approach for disentangled feature attribution as it allows us to identify the most salient speech frames from both the textual and acoustic perspectives.

SAR2Net: Learning Spatially Anchored Representations for Retrieval-Guided Cross-Stain Alignment
Authors: Tianle Shen, Fang Yan, Xiaofan Zhang
Venue: CVPR 2026
First: 2026-10-19T11:45:04+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-19T11:45:04+00:00
Comments: 10 pages, CVPR 2026
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2 · Code3
Abstract
Achieving spatial alignment between whole-slide images(WSIs) across stains is essential for integrating morphology and molecular information, yet it remains challenging due to extreme resolution, tissue fragmentation, and large nonlinear deformations. Conventional registration pipelines depend on global pre-alignment and spatial consistency, which often collapse under such distortions. We present SAR2Net, a framework that learns spatially anchored representations and reformulates cross-stain alignment as a region-level feature retrieval problem. Instead of estimating explicit transformations, SAR2Net learns pointwise representations encoding the relative spatial relationships to tissue landmarks. Given landmarks and arbitrary coordinates, it predicts spatially anchored features that serve as deformation-invariant descriptors of tissue topology. A twostage retrieval framework then establishes correspondences between slides, even when global alignment is infeasible. Experiments on biopsy-oriented HE-IHC datasets show that SAR2Net achieves robust region-level alignment under severe tissue distortions, outperforming previous methods. Code is available at https://github.com/Shentl/SAR2Net.

Abstract
Generalizing real-time physics-based animation across diverse 3D shapes and spatial discretizations remains challenging. In this paper, we introduce PhysSkin++, a physics-informed framework that learns continuous neural skinning fields from static geometries to construct compact deformation subspaces. Specifically, we propose a Neural Skinning Fields Autoencoder that predicts shape-dependent deformation subspaces without per-object network training. To learn these subspaces without simulation trajectories, we develop a Physics-Informed Self-Supervised Learning strategy that combines skinning-field normalization with conflict-aware gradient correction, stabilizing joint optimization under physical and orthogonality constraints. The learned representation supports efficient reduced dynamics with user control and contact interactions, together with direct animation binding to meshes, point clouds, and 3D Gaussians. For prescribed rig animation, we further introduce a self-supervised complementarity objective to learn complementary elastic responses without enforcing complementarity constraints at runtime. Experiments demonstrate cross-shape generalization of the learned skinning fields, real-time reduced simulation, and applications to controllable, collision-aware, and rig-complementary animation.

Abstract
Financial agents are expected to answer factual investment queries through open search, but financial multi-hop retrieval differs from general multi-hop QA in a crucial way: each intermediate answer is an exact typed constraint, such as an entity, date, event, or numerical indicator, that determines the next query. This strict answer-conditioned dependency makes small intermediate errors cascade into plausible but wrong search trajectories. We introduce FinHopper, a benchmark for complex financial agents with controlled reasoning depth from 1 to 6 hops, covering stocks, funds, and macroeconomic facts, with gold intermediate answers for process-level evaluation. To construct FinHopper at scale, we design a state-transition-based synthesis framework that composes verified atomic financial QA patterns into dependency-preserving chains, followed by targeted in-synthesis verification, model-ensemble checking, and public-search validation by financial experts. Experiments with 20 LLM-agent configurations show that public search tools are necessary but insufficient: performance drops sharply as hop depth increases, the best model remains far below human experts, and process-aware scoring reveals partial reasoning progress hidden by final-answer-only evaluation. The benchmark is available at https://github.com/justajustin/FinHopper/.

Abstract
In modern cancer research, the vast volume of medical data generated is often underutilised due to challenges related to patient privacy. The OncoReg Challenge addresses this issue by enabling researchers to develop and validate image registration methods through a two-phase framework that ensures patient privacy while fostering the development of more generalisable AI models. NIOCIS: Typed-Decision Next-Hop Routing with Node-Conditioned Option Spaces for Tool-Use Agents
Authors: Jin Han, Mingfeng Xin, Feng Liang, Jingtao Yu
First: 2026-10-19T08:10:26+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-19T08:10:26+00:00
Comments: 34 pages, 10 figures. Code and the pseudonymized derived corpus: https://github.com/geilihan/niocis
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2 · Code3
Abstract
Tool-use agents pay per decision: at each hop a language model re-reads the state and thinks for seconds, though its actions are finite and decisions recur. We present NIOCIS, a routing layer that folds execution logs into a loop-collapsed action graph (forward transitions plus recovery back-edges) answering one typed question: which declared option at a state, plus stop, does the fleet take next? A small model reads a calibrated distribution over them in one pass, emitting no text. On 1,615 production security-operations episodes we measure what the layer replaces, returns and is bounded by. Replaces: the typed encoder matches the logged next hop on 32.2% of the 3,875 held-out decisions (34.9% within the declared options) in 10 ms of encoder time (19.5 ms for the typed-choice form), against 2.2 s per LLM hop; two feature readouts reach 32.8% and 32.3%, an ordering the paired test does not resolve, so the typed layer is chosen for cost, not a separated lead. Tuning: jpt-4b reaches 9.9% zero-shot and 31.9% fine-tuned, a gap an exact paired test does not resolve (p=0.68), at 244 ms. Returns little: the realised hop falls outside the declared options for 7.9% of decisions and outside the model's top twenty for 9.3%; the most confident 15% holds 91.9% accuracy. Bounded: a cross-trace leave-one-out test puts the released state's ceiling at 25.6--44.9% as the state key grows more specific, i.e. repeated states disagree more often than they agree; the option-space ablation attributes the gain to per-node candidate restriction (+3.5 points over a flat catalog) rather than graph traversal (zero within its interval); a gated self-evolution loop lifts every arm above its anchor yet ends below full supervision; a confidence-gated cascade loses agreement when the escalation arm is the zero-shot stand-in; and agreement past a third of the fleet needs a state carrying the plan and the observation.

Abstract
Negation-aware image generation remains challenging for text-to-image diffusion models, which often struggle with negation due to implicit linguistic biases that are deeply entangled within text representations. As a result, bias-aligned semantics frequently dominate over intended negative constraints. To overcome this limitation, we propose a simple training-free method of Decompose and Recompose text embedding (Dero), which constructs a semantic-preserving subspace and a bias subspace using auxiliary prompts and performs semantic disentanglement based on bidirectional SVD. By decomposing entangled representations and selectively recomposing disentangled semantic components, Dero realigns text representations toward intended negation semantics while restoring residual stylistic features to preserve semantic fidelity and image quality. As a model-agnostic inference-time framework operating solely on text representations, Dero can be seamlessly applied to diverse diffusion architectures, including both U-Net and DiT based models. Extensive experiments on both object- and concept-level negation demonstrate consistent improvements in negation-aware image generation, achieving stronger text-image alignment, better semantic fidelity, and higher human preference scores.

ACorD: Adaptive Correspondence Discovery for Medical Image Registration
Authors: Arghavan Rezvani, Kun Han, Xiaohui Xie
First: 2026-10-19T01:31:04+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-19T01:31:04+00:00
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Medical image registration is a fundamental task for aligning anatomical structures across images. Exhaustively searching for point-wise correspondences is prohibitively expensive in both computation and memory, particularly for high-resolution 3D medical images. Most recent learning-based methods directly predict correspondences in a coarse-to-fine manner, but often propagate intermediate estimates across scales without explicitly weighting correspondence evidence, which can allow coarse-scale errors to persist during refinement. To address these challenges, we propose ACorD, an adaptive correspondence discovery framework designed to mitigate error propagation with an attention-guided process. ACorD first predicts multiple potential correspondence candidates through Correlation-guided Cross-scale Offset Prediction (CCOP), which combines correlation maps as spatial matching priors with cross-scale features as global deformation context. Then, Multi-Head Deformable Aggregation (MHDA) aggregates these candidate correspondences using attention, producing a learned, non-uniform weighting over competing matches rather than treating them equally. The multi-head design further captures complementary deformation patterns, ensuring both global alignment and local precision. By integrating adaptive offset prediction with selective, attention-based aggregation of correspondence evidence, ACorD enables accurate deformation refinement. Extensive experiments on four public medical registration benchmarks demonstrate that adaptive correspondence discovery within a multi-scale refinement framework improves registration robustness across diverse anatomical structures.

Abstract
Time series are ubiquitous across different domains, and the growing use of synthetic data for privacy-preserving sharing, data augmentation, and stress testing makes time series generation increasingly important. Early generative approaches are predominantly autoregressive, which can suffer from low sample diversity, error accumulation, and difficulty modeling long-range dependencies. In this paper, we introduce FracTS, a hierarchical, coarse-to-fine autoregressive model for multivariate time series generation. Inspired by fractal generative modeling developed for images, FracTS adapts the paradigm from 2D to 1D sequences by learning a multi-scale representation that captures global trajectory structure at coarse scales and local dynamics at fine scales while following the autoregressive nature of time series. In contrast to unstructured images, time series are structured, allowing easy automated or designated temporal feature processing optimized for the model, and global (static) feature extraction describing the instances that generate the corresponding time series in the collection. We incorporate them in the generation process to further improve the generation quality. The design naturally handles multivariate dependencies and improves long-horizon temporal coherence without requiring fixed-length inputs. Experiments on five datasets (two conditional, three unconditional) conducted against seven baselines show that FracTS consistently outperforms baselines across marginal distribution and predictive ML utility, and correlation preservation in the unconditional setting. In particular, the predictive ML utility is averagely improved by 100.0% and 75.0-81.0% for unconditional and conditional time series datasets compared to the best baseline (relative error computed on dataset-averaged raw values). Abstract

Abstract
Raw image intensities are a poor surrogate for similarity across images in correspondence problems. Further, while auxiliary segmentation labels can provide semantic identification and improve matching, they can assign only coarse identities to fine-grained anatomy and often cover only a subset of structures, particularly in whole-body settings. We present an approach that instead establishes correspondence using complementary representations of anatomy and demonstrate its utility for longitudinal whole-body PET/CT registration in the Learn2Reg 2026 PSMAReg Challenge. Self-organizing reinforcement learning: a swarm approaches the explore-exploit optimum
Authors: Galen J. Wilkerson
First: 2026-10-18T08:45:51+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-18T08:45:51+00:00
Comments: 16 pages, 9 figures, code and data: https://github.com/galenwilkerson/self-organizing-reinforcement-learning
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2 · Code3
Abstract
The explore/exploit trade-off is the core problem of reinforcement learning (RL). We show that a minimal foraging swarm approaches its optimum - the ideal-free / Nash allocation of a multi-agent resource-sharing game (an equilibrium, not a return maximum) - with no learner and no reward signal. Agents explore by heavy-tailed (Levy) flights, recruit neighbours to a discovery through a threshold cascade on their proximity network, and disperse as a patch crowds; this cascade cycle balances exploration and exploitation and drives foragers toward the ideal free distribution. The swarm attains the shape of this optimum (committed foragers track richness with slope 0.94) but not its exact equilibrium - it stays measurably exploitable - reaching it dynamically (~150 steps) through the coupling of recruitment to movement: remove the coupling and matching collapses (0.94 -> 0.08). The RL sits at the level of the problem and the allocation reached, not the micro-rule: no step evaluates payoff (reward-free by construction, though the crowd signal is informationally tied to payoff), and the swarm reaches the allocation without learning it (quality plateaus and re-settles after resources move, no cumulative gain). Against a trained baseline the comparison is two-sided: in an easier, disembodied game a learner is more precise, while in the same embodied environment the reward-free swarm reaches ~65% of a validated oracle ceiling at zero training and leads until trained PPO and centralised-critic (CTDE) learners overtake it (~74-75%, 16 seeds, all above the swarm mean) after ~4,000 of 15,000 training steps - its advantage is in budget, not asymptote. In this one setting we read it not as beating RL but as a strong zero-shot, reward-free operating point: a demonstration that the self-organizing physics of a minimal swarm can carry a collective close to the optimum RL seeks, with none of RL's machinery.

The explore/exploit trade-off is the core problem of reinforcement learning (RL).

Abstract
Predicting single-cell responses to genetic and chemical perturbations is important for understanding regulatory mechanisms and prioritizing therapeutic interventions. Existing methods have made progress by modeling perturbation-conditioned expression shifts with neural networks, graph priors, foundation-model representations, or distributional generative models. However, combinatorial perturbation prediction remains challenging because much of the response can be explained by additive single-perturbation effects, while the biologically informative signal often lies in a smaller non-additive residual induced by perturbation interactions. Directly generating the full post-perturbation expression profile may therefore allocate substantial capacity to the dominant additive component and underemphasize synergy-specific structure. To address this issue, we propose \textbf{SynGeneFlow}, a synergy-aware flow matching framework that anchors the source distribution at an additive prior and learns a residual flow toward the true perturbed state. SynGeneFlow further incorporates perturbation-conditioned gene interaction modeling, where a static gene-gene mask provides biological structure and a perturbation-dependent deformation bias dynamically modulates gene-gene coupling. To preserve population-level response structure, we align both endpoint distributions and biological residual distributions with MMD-based regularization. We evaluate SynGeneFlow on genetic and drug perturbation benchmarks under observed-single, held-out, and cross-modality settings. The results demonstrate that residualizing flow matching around additive perturbation effects provides a biologically grounded and robust framework for single-cell perturbation response prediction.

Authors: Yu Zhang, Xudong Xu, Xingang Pan
First: 2026-10-06T01:53:56+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-18T02:13:28+00:00
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2 · Project1
Abstract
Neural simulation of high-fidelity deformable bodies is a foundational challenge in computer graphics and physical AI. Long-horizon prediction for high-resolution 3D volumetric meshes is hard: autoregressive methods are prone to error accumulation, while direct multi-frame prediction at native resolution is computationally prohibitive. This motivates a compact spatiotemporal latent representation, which is largely unexplored for mesh-based volumetric physics. Meanwhile, it remains unclear whether deterministic regression or generative diffusion is the more suitable predictive paradigm. To address these coupled challenges, we introduce PhysLDM, a unified latent-diffusion paradigm for one-shot deformable simulation. Its core is a holistic spatiotemporal VAE that avoids the "staircase" artifacts of standard temporal compression (e.g., video VAEs), achieving ~2.48 mm reconstruction precision on meter-scale scenes at up to 78x token compression.
