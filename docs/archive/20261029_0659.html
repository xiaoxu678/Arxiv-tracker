Abstract
Structured state-space models (SSMs) achieve linear-time inference and logarithmic-time parallel training via associative scans, yet homogeneous diagonal transitions generate solvable semigroups unable to track non-solvable group words or 3D rigid-body manifolds. We develop a theoretical and empirical framework for Non-Commutative Lie-Algebraic State-Space Models (Lie-SSMs), parameterizing recurrent updates on Lie algebra tangent spaces via the matrix exponential. First, via an o-minimal multi-rate cylindrical flag collar, we establish an unconditional single-layer finite-division criterion for homogeneous contracting $SO(3)$ and $SU(2)$ heads, proving odd-prime Sylow abelianity, odd-$p$-core centrality $[u^2,x]=e$, exponent-30 commutator laws ($[u^{30},v^{30}]=e$ in $SO(3)^m$ and $[u^{30},v^{30}]\in Z(G),[u^{30},v^{30}]^2=e$ in $SU(2)^m,SO(4)^m$, excluding $S_5$), and sharp threshold $d^*(S_5)=d^*(A_6)=5$ in $SO(5)$. Second, Depth-2 semidirect and Schreier $2$-cocycle cascades resolve $S_5$, $2I\cong SL_2(F_5)$, and $\mathrm{Heis}(F_p)$ in $O(\log T)$ depth, whereas unnormalized contracting coupling collapses exponentially without Directional RMSNorm. Third, we characterize the $O(\varepsilon^{c-1})$ Chen--Magnus--Hall--Witt commutator saddle and $1/|[G,G]|$ coset-centroid dilution, proving $\varepsilon=0$ symmetry breaking and local Polyak--Łojasiewicz convergence ($θ_{\max}<π$), solving $S_5$ ($99.90\%/99.40\%$ at $T=128$) and $D_{16}$ ($100.00\%$) via dense prefix loss and factored readouts. Across $Q_8,A_5,S_5,D_{16}$, Dyck-2, $SE_2(3)$ Left-Jacobian odometry, GPU scans ($26.03\times$ speedup), and three 3D IMU cohorts (UCI HAR, HAPT-PT 30-subject CV, and REALDISP), Depth-2 Lie-$SO(3)$ cascades reach $95.69\%\pm 0.14\%$ on HAR, $87.16\%\pm 0.64\%$ ($88.32\%\pm 0.93\%$ 30-subject episodes) on HAPT-PT, and $84.23\%\pm 1.00\%$ under displacement on REALDISP.

CraftSPH: A composable framework for differentiable smoothed particle hydrodynamics implemented in PyTorch

Abstract
Smoothed particle hydrodynamics (SPH) is well suited to large-deformation fluid problems, and the growing use of automatic differentiation in engineering applications has motivated interest in differentiable SPH solvers. In this study, a composable differentiable SPH framework, CraftSPH, has been developed. In CraftSPH, major computational operations are implemented as independent modules that users can combine to construct solvers for different purposes. This design supports high-accuracy discretization and both explicit and implicit computation within a common framework, while allowing individual components to be maintained and extended. Selected numerical operations also support automatic differentiation (AD) for physical-parameter estimation and potential coupling with deep learning models. These capabilities were demonstrated through forward and inverse analyses. Forward analyses using an implicit SPH scheme with high-accuracy discretization showed good agreement with analytical and reference solutions for Poiseuille flow and a rising bubble, while capturing the key experimental trends in two- and three-dimensional dam-break problems. In inverse analyses, explicit and implicit SPH schemes were used to estimate kinematic viscosity in Taylor-Green vortex flow and Reynolds number in lid-driven cavity flow. The results also showed that the choice of operations included in the computational graph affects the computed gradients. Together, these findings demonstrate that CraftSPH enables users to assemble problem-specific solvers for forward and inverse analyses within a common framework.

Abstract
3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) has established itself as an efficient representation for real-time, high-fidelity 3D scene reconstruction. However, scaling 3DGS to large and unbounded scenes such as city blocks remains difficult. Existing divide-and-conquer methods alleviate memory pressure by partitioning the scene into blocks and training on multiple, non-communicating GPUs, but introduce new bottlenecks: (i) partitions suffer from severe load imbalance since uniform or heuristic splits do not reflect actual computational demands, and (ii) coarse-to-fine pipelines fail to exploit the coarse stage efficiently, often reloading the entire model and incurring high overhead. In this work, we introduce LoBE-GS, a Load-Balanced and Efficient 3D Gaussian Splatting framework, that re-engineers the large-scale 3DGS pipeline. Specifically, LoBE-GS introduces a load-balanced KD-tree scene partitioning scheme with optimized cutlines that balance per-block camera counts. To accelerate preprocessing, it employs depth-based back-projection for fast camera assignment, reducing processing time from hours to minutes. It further reduces training cost through two lightweight techniques: visibility cropping and selective densification. On large-scale urban and outdoor datasets, LoBE-GS reduces scene-partitioning time by up to an order of magnitude and worst-block fine-training time by up to $3.2\times$ relative to CityGaussian. It also substantially reduces end-to-end and post-coarse training time under matched GPU budgets while maintaining competitive reconstruction quality. LoBE-GS thus provides a communication-free alternative with favorable quality-efficiency trade-offs. The associated code is available at: https://github.com/Sunnyhong0326/lobe-gs

Abstract
Infrared--visible (IR--VIS) multi-modal perception supports robust scene understanding under challenging illumination. Infrared and visible images are often misaligned due to platform motion, viewpoint changes, and modality-dependent distortions, causing registration errors to propagate into subsequent fusion and detection stages. We propose TriDifNet, a unified three-task diffusion network for end-to-end RGB-T perception. To the best of our knowledge, TriDifNet is the first diffusion-based network that jointly optimizes registration, fusion, and detection for misaligned RGB-T pedestrian perception. TriDifNet consists of three diffusion-based modules, i.e., the Registration Diffusion Module (RegDM), the Fusion Diffusion Module (FusDM), and the Detection Diffusion Module (DetDM). RegDM aligns the unaligned infrared image to the optical reference by estimating a dense deformation field. FusDM performs residual conditional diffusion to generate a detection-oriented fused representation with optical structural cues and complementary infrared saliency. DetDM formulates pedestrian detection as an iterative denoising process in bounding-box space. To couple the three tasks, TriDifNet introduces a closed-loop optimization mechanism, where detection semantics guide fusion and registration, and the fusion result further regularizes geometric alignment. A stage-wise progressive training strategy and a lightweight Nash multi-task learning (Nash-MTL)-based late-stage refinement are further adopted to balance registration, fusion, and detection optimization. Extensive experiments on the KAIST multispectral pedestrian benchmark show that TriDifNet achieves superior downstream detection performance under misaligned RGB-T settings. Against strong system-level competitors, TriDifNet achieves 65.44\% Recall, 57.55\% mAP$_{50}$, and 26.83\% mAP$_{50:95}$.

UltraFlash: Accelerating Megapixel Visual Synthesis
Authors: Phuc Lai, Anh Nguyen, Phong Nguyen, Anh Tran
Venue: NeurIPS 2026
First: 2026-10-24T10:19:20+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-27T11:09:49+00:00
Comments: Accepted to NeurIPS 2026. Project page: https://qualcomm-ai-research.github.io/UltraFlash/
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2 · Project1
Abstract
Megapixel visual synthesis with latent diffusion models requires operating far beyond the resolutions at which these models are typically trained. Cascade refinement is a leading strategy for this setting: it starts from a base image at a native resolution and progressively upsamples it to the target resolution through multiple refinement stages. Each stage typically performs a pixel-space resolution transition followed by latent-space denoising. While prior acceleration efforts mainly focus on reducing denoising cost, we identify a complementary and underexplored bottleneck: the repeated movement of representations through the VAE. We call these operations pixel-space transitions. In cascade pipelines, intermediate latents are often decoded to RGB, resized, and re-encoded before refinement, while final latents are decoded patch by patch with dense overlap to suppress boundary artifacts. We propose UltraFlash, a transition-efficient approach that accelerates these VAE-heavy operations. UltraFlash has two components. Latent Hyperloop replaces intermediate RGB round trips with a learned latent-space shortcut that approximates the decode-resize-encode transformation without repeated VAE decoding and encoding. Sparse Patch-based Decoding accelerates final reconstruction by addressing patch seams caused by inconsistent GroupNorm statistics and reusing cached statistics across patches, enabling sparse VAE decoding with much smaller overlap. Integrated into a state-of-the-art cascade baseline, UltraFlash reduces 4K generation latency by approximately 2x while improving image quality. These results show that optimizing pixel-space transitions can improve both efficiency and fidelity, offering an effective route toward fast, high-quality megapixel visual synthesis.

Abstract
Spine landmark detection from spinal X-ray is a key step for estimating spine deformation severity, for building biomechanical models, and for pre-operative planning. We reframe anatomical landmark detection as a pixel-level contrastive representation learning problem, matching dense pixel embeddings with landmark prototypes. Surprisingly, even with a generic U-Net backbone, this formulation results in more discriminative heatmaps compared to the traditional heatmap regression paradigm, leading to improved discrimination between vertebral corners on distinct vertebra. Furthermore, we combine the resulting heatmaps with learnable shape priors over the global configuration of spinal landmarks to resolve ambiguities in landmark locations, yielding a simple and practical approach to spinal landmark detection. Our approach is competitive, in terms of landmark accuracy and clinical parameter estimation, with the state-of-the-art on the public AASCE dataset and on two private spinal X-ray datasets. Our code and pre-trained weights are available at https://github.com/BioMedTP/Pix2Point.

Abstract
Achieving constant accuracy in object detection is challenging due to the inherent variability of object sizes. One effective approach to this problem involves optimizing input resolution, referred to as a multi-resolution strategy. Previous approaches to resolution optimization have often been based on pre-defined resolutions with manual selection. However, there is a lack of study on run-time resolution optimization for existing architectures. This paper introduces DyRA, a dynamic resolution adjustment network providing an image-specific scale factor for existing detectors. This network is co-trained with detectors utilizing specially designed loss functions, namely ParetoScaleLoss and BalanceLoss. Abstract
Animals exhibit diverse shape-changing behaviors, from the undulatory swimming of eels to the arching movements of inchworms. Reproducing these biological shapes with modular soft robots offers a promising approach to developing versatile bioinspired robotic systems. However, translating a desired shape into a feasible robotic configuration remains challenging. Existing cable-driven modular soft robots rely on computationally expensive physics-based modeling and combinatorial search for inverse design, making it difficult to efficiently reproduce diverse target shapes. To address this challenge, we present a learning-based real-to-sim-to-real framework that translates biological curves into robotic configurations through surrogate-assisted inverse design and uncertainty-guided refinement. Physical demonstrations with four bioinspired robots further illustrate the translation of biological curves into realizable robotic shapes.

Abstract
Deformable image registration relies on accurate anatomical correspondences. Abstract
Reconstructing the mass of a heavy resonance from its decay products with missing energy is one of the central tasks that directly determine the sensitivity in new physics searches at collider experiments. Abstract
Dyadic relationship recognition aims to identify the latent social relationship between two individuals from their multimodal interactions. Existing methods typically project heterogeneous cues into a shared representation and optimise a flat classification objective, overlooking that relationships may rely on different interaction patterns. For instance, distant relationships can often be inferred from direct behavioural correspondence, whereas close relationships are better reflected by coordinated affective, behavioural, and multimodal responses. To address this limitation, we propose HiRe-DRR, a Hierarchical Multimodal Interaction Reasoning framework that organises relationship evidence from three complementary perspectives: feature level, interaction type, and relationship hierarchy. HiRe-DRR decomposes facial, bodily, acoustic, and textual cues into low- and high-level representations and constructs homogeneous and heterogeneous cross-participant interactions. We further introduce the Mixture-of-Interaction Transformer (MoI-Former), which treats diverse interactions as specialised experts and employs relation-aware sparse routing to identify relationship-relevant evidence while preserving contextual interaction information. The proposed Hierarchical Conditional Loss (HC-Loss) exploits the hierarchical nature of social relationships through coarse-to-fine supervision, enabling structured relationship reasoning. Extensive experiments on three dyadic relationship benchmarks demonstrate that HiRe-DRR outperforms recent representative image-, video-, and multimodal methods. Our routing analysis further reveals that distant relationships tend to rely on low-level homogeneous interactions, whereas closer relationships focus on high-level heterogeneous interactions. Our code is publicly available at: https://github.com/tw-repository/HiRe-DRR.

Abstract
Due to the inherent discrepancies in imaging viewpoints and field-of-view mismatches among multi-modal sensors, existing fusion methods have long struggled to achieve complete scene-level information integration beyond the limitation in sensor fields of view. Abstract
Evaluating deformable 3D reconstruction requires ground truth linking camera motion, surface deformation, material correspondence and image appearance. We introduce the Synthetic Deformable Tubular Benchmark, generated with LumenMorph, an open-source toolkit for creating, annotating and evaluating tubular scenes. Abstract
Recovering an object's 3D structure and camera motion from a few uncalibrated views is a fundamental problem in computer vision. Abstract
This paper addresses the problem of dynamic scene surface reconstruction using Gaussian Splatting (GS), aiming to recover temporally consistent geometry. While existing GS-based dynamic surface reconstruction methods can yield superior reconstruction, they are typically limited to either a single object or objects with only small deformations, struggling to maintain temporally consistent surface reconstruction of large deformations over time. We propose ``\textit{4DSurf}'', a novel and unified framework for generic dynamic surface reconstruction that does not require specifying the number or types of objects in the scene, can handle large surface deformations and temporal inconsistency in reconstruction.
