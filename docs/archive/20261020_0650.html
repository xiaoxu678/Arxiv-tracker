Beyond Multi-Stage Pipelines: A Label-Correlation Ensemble for Multi-Label Retinal Fundus Disease Classification
Authors: Md Asif Uddin, Jannatul Ferdous, Asif Islam, Zobaer Mahmud Zisan, Promit Ghosh, Shah Ahafik Arman, Niloy Farhan, Rafeed Rahman
First: 2026-10-16T16:54:48+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-16T16:54:48+00:00
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Retinal fundus images frequently carry more than one disease, and public fundus datasets are heterogeneous and severely long-tailed. Clinical triage suggests a staged design, in which a screening gate, a disease-group router and group-specific classifiers operate in sequence, but it is unclear whether this hierarchy earns its complexity. We present a controlled comparison of three architectures on a unified 39-target label space built from RFMiD 1.0, RFMiD 2.0 and ODIR-5K (12,652 images): (i) a three-stage gated cascade of eight models, (ii) a flat model with 39 independent sigmoid outputs, and (iii) a label-correlation model that replaces the flat linear head with 39 learned label queries and a two-layer transformer decoder. All three share one Swin-V2-B backbone, one preprocessing pipeline and one training protocol, so differences are attributable to the architecture. Staging offers no consistent advantage: tracing every missed disease instance to the stage that lost it shows that the two coarse gates discard 34.2% of true instances while the fine-grained classifiers lose only 6.7%, and the gates' hard zeros depress the cascade's macro-AUC to 0.747. Label-correlation modeling gives the largest single gain, raising the detection rate from 0.621 to 0.682 (0.689 with test-time augmentation). Blending the flat and label-correlation models under four-view test-time augmentation is the best configuration on six of eight metrics (exact match 0.694, micro-F1 0.678, macro-F1 0.456, macro-AUC 0.972). On definition-matched class subsets it matches or exceeds four recent studies on macro- and micro-F1, most clearly on low-support pathologies. We also show that ensembling a gated cascade with continuous-output models is harmful, and that per-source negative sampling is needed to remove a dataset shortcut that otherwise yields a spurious AUC of 1.000.

GeoReason: Bridging Logical Reasoning and Spatial Fidelity in Remote Sensing Segmentation
Authors: Cheng Fan, Han Wu, Tingzhang Luo, Minjing Dong, Yehui Tang, Jianyuan Guo
First: 2026-10-16T13:07:22+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-16T13:07:22+00:00
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2 · Code3
Abstract
Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) have significantly advanced instruction-driven perception through the "Embedding-as-Mask" paradigm. However, extending this capability to Remote Sensing (RS) reasoning segmentation remains exceptionally challenging under cluttered geographical contexts and extreme scale variations, often leading to Spatial Fidelity Degradation and Target Misidentification. We identify a critical yet underexplored bottleneck: the sequential alienation of the segmentation token [SEG] from its initial visual grounding within the autoregressive generation paradigm. Produced only at the end of long reasoning chains, [SEG] relies on visual evidence that has been progressively diluted by linguistic abstraction and autoregressive noise. This information decay is particularly catastrophic for RS targets with minuscule pixel footprints, where even marginal fidelity loss leads to localization failure. To address these bottlenecks, we propose GeoReason, a novel framework designed to fortify logical deduction and preserve spatial integrity. First, we introduce an Anticipatory Prior mechanism that injects the [SEG] token directly into the initial user query, shifting it from a terminal output to a primary condition. This paradigm shift transforms the token into a persistent spatial anchor, preventing information decay during long-form linguistic inference. Second, we enhance visual reasoning via a Location-aware Chain-of-Thought (CoT) that enforces coarse-to-fine localization using bounding boxes, coupled with a Latent Patch-to-Token Alignment (PTA) Loss that explicitly aligns the latent segmentation token with physical RS textures. Extensive experiments demonstrate that GeoReason consistently outperforms state-of-the-art methods on the Earthreason and RRSIS-D benchmarks. Code is available at https://github.com/Fanc0126/GeoReason.

Abstract
Adaptive multi-verifier systems are commonly compared through endpoint quality-cost gaps, even when the verifier catalog, availability, accounting, information filtration, or scorer changes with the policy. We formulate verifier routing as a contract-conditioned identification problem. The contract records request support, verifier catalog, realized availability, resource accounting, online filtration, and post-trace scoring; a matched route contrast changes only the policy coordinate. ROUTEAUDIT adds three measurable objects to this contract. A contract lattice averages coordinate increments over every admissible bridge order and reports the resulting attribution together with its path sensitivity. A policy-independent response tape identifies paired sequential contrasts when adaptive policies reveal different observations. For incomplete matching, request-level bounds use whichever potential outcome remains observed and give a sharp finite-population interval. The protocol commits paid observations and ledger events before the oracle join and returns an attribution certificate for each comparison. On two held-out raw-tail caches, matched static SF+SA equals the cascade, assigning the apparent gains of 0.1797 and 0.1250 over full static to the verifier-set edge. On 1,319 held-out task requests, the learned and RLVR studies report quality 0.9522 and 0.9553 versus 0.9484 for matched static; the RLVR-static paired difference is +0.0068 with a request-paired interval $[0.0015,0.0122]$ and a training-seed-by-request hierarchical interval $[0.0006,0.0131]$. Controlled attribution recovery yields route mean absolute error 0.0011 and endpoint reconstruction error 0.0004. Factorial, bridge-order, and stochastic-provider studies evaluate the certificate interface; RLVR supplies a learned-policy stress test under the same identification contract.

Abstract
Creating high-fidelity, animatable 3D avatars from a single image remains a formidable challenge. We identified three desirable attributes of avatar generation: 1) the method should be feed-forward, 2) model a 360° full-head, and 3) should be animation-ready. However, current work addresses only two of the three points simultaneously. To address these limitations, we propose OMEGA-Avatar, the first feed-forward framework that simultaneously generates a generalizable, 360°-complete, and animatable 3D Gaussian head from a single image. Starting from a feed-forward and animatable framework, we address the 360° full-head avatar generation problem with two novel components. First, to overcome poor hair modeling in full-head avatar generation, we introduce a semantic-aware mesh deformation module that integrates multi-view normals to optimize a FLAME head with hair while preserving its topology structure. Second, to enable effective feed-forward decoding of full-head features, we propose a multi-view feature splatting module that constructs a shared canonical UV representation from features across multiple views through differentiable bilinear splatting, hierarchical UV mapping, and visibility-aware fusion. This approach preserves both global structural coherence and local high-frequency details across all viewpoints, ensuring 360° consistency without per-instance optimization. Extensive experiments demonstrate that OMEGA-Avatar achieves state-of-the-art performance, significantly outperforming existing baselines in 360° full-head completeness while robustly preserving identity across different viewpoints.

Abstract
This study investigates CT-to-PET image synthesis using TransNSNet, a Transformer-enhanced multi-scale adversarial framework. A paired CT-PET dataset was curated from the public non-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC) Radiogenomics collection through patient screening, slice-level matching, preprocessing, and patient-level train-test separation. The proposed framework generates PET-like predictions at multiple spatial scales and applies coarse-to-fine reconstruction supervision, while TransPatchGAN introduces contextual feature interaction into adversarial evaluation. A progressive ablation study compares Pix2Pix, NSNet-Base, NSNet-D, and TransNSNet. Image-level performance was summarized over a fixed convergence window (epochs 80-101) from one training run per model. TransNSNet achieved the best mean value in nine of ten metrics, whereas NSNet-Base achieved the highest visual saliency-induced index. For fixed-fraction high-intensity regions, NSNet-D was marginally higher in slice-level recall and balanced accuracy, but the equal-weight patient-level values were nearly identical and the highest Dice values shifted to TransNSNet. These differences were small relative to between-patient variability. In the separate adaptive patient-level analysis (n=10), no pairwise model difference remained statistically significant after Holm correction. TransNSNet also had a substantially smaller parameter footprint than the original next-scale models. These findings indicate promising image-quality trends, but they do not establish statistical superiority or clinical validity; synthesized PET-like images should not be interpreted as substitutes for clinically acquired PET.

Authors: Miaobo Hu, Shuhao Hu, Xiaobo Guo, Xin Wang, Bokun Wang, Daren Zha

Abstract
We introduce CascadeLedger, a counterfactual execution-audit framework for budgeted language-model cascades. The protocol enforces call-before-read semantics, binds successful reads to service and cache identities, records availability and component-wise budget transitions, and freezes the event trace before any target or task scorer is joined. The central object is aggregate-equivalent trace validity: two executions can share a final answer, declared resource projection, and service histogram while differing in whether their routing decisions used only legitimately observable information. We complement single-trace invariants with counterfactual prefix non-interference (CPNI), which couples a visible prefix and policy randomness while changing only a declared hidden family. The counterfactual influence frontier (CIF) localizes the earliest violating prefix and a minimal hidden witness; projection-preserving adversarial mutation (PPAM) searches for invalid traces that remain acceptable to a non-counterfactual checker. Across frozen-cache, independently generated, and serving evaluations, the two-layer audit reports a clean false-positive rate of 0.0000, detects 0.9983 of serialized faults and 0.9982 of unlogged hidden-read faults, and changes 0.3403 of corrupted quality-resource conclusions after deterministic recomputation. The online recorder and offline CPNI audit have separate overhead measurements. Learned routing and semantic verification provide downstream compatibility studies under the same execution contract.

Structural Affinity: An Algorithm for Coherent Cage-Based Shape Design
Authors: Aviraj Newatia, Shengan Yang, Hantao Zhong, Rika Antonova
First: 2026-10-16T09:10:31+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-16T09:10:31+00:00
Comments: 27 pages, 16 figures
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Cage-based deformation (CBD) parametrises 3D geometry through a small set of control handles, effectively enabling a variety of tasks from animation and interactive modelling to tool-based co-design, neural deformation transfer, and aerodynamic shape optimization. Many optimization approaches relying on CBD employ regularisation strategies to reduce the distortion of the deformed mesh by encouraging smooth, regular cage deformations. These techniques offer soft guidance to an optimization algorithm towards desirable deformations, but compete with the task objective rather than changing the deformation parameterization itself. In this work we introduce Structural Affinity for CBD, an explicit regularisation method which enables smooth deformations through control cages by exploiting a two-cage structure to couple cage-handle motion directly. Structural Affinity precomputes an affinity matrix using mean-value coordinates, binding the inner control-cage to a coarser outer-cage. This matrix defines how much a cage handle displacement propagates to neighbouring handles which substantially decreases and often eliminates cage self-intersections, reduces mesh distortion, and adds linear overhead at deformation time. Across robotic control--morphology co-design, source-to-target neural deformation transfer, and aerodynamic wing shape optimization, Structural Affinity produces more regular deformations and substantially reduces reliance on hand-designed structural loss terms. We report consistent gains on three complementary diagnostics: the cotangent Laplacian, a proxy for surface smoothness; cage distortion, capturing whether control handles move coherently; and surface roughness, which measures undesirable oscillation in wing geometry.

Replay Pruning and Few-Bit Integer Inference for Hardware-Efficient Speed-Sign Recognition
Authors: Se Un Park, Kyungkeon Chung, Huigeol Kim, Junyoung Park
First: 2026-10-16T01:41:06+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-16T01:41:06+00:00
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Speed-limit sign recognition on an automotive camera system-on-chip (SoC) must fit a detector and a classifier (the reader) into the memory, arithmetic, and energy budgets of the device. In our two-stage recognizer, the detector, a YOLO-family network with ReLU and hard-sigmoid operators, is compressed by channel replay: an iterative pruning cascade identifies the channels to remove, the recorded deletions are replayed on the original task-trained network, the parent, and one continuous knowledge-distillation recovery follows. On a YOLOv5n, replay removes 60.3% of the parameters at the accuracy of the parent and, in validation mAP50-95 over five matched seeds, outperforms iterative pruning and training from scratch, and its per-step variant (per-step replay) is the most accurate pruning method. On a YOLOv8n, replay and per-step replay outperform every other method and the unpruned parent. The reader, a five-layer CNN with 18k parameters, and the post-processing of the YOLOv5n and YOLOv8n heads use integer arithmetic only. With 4-bit weights and 8-bit activations (W4A8), the reader matches FP32 accuracy in 12.1 KB of on-chip memory, and an executable streaming model reproduces the INT8 class predictions of its runtime on all 9,850 test crops. On a Jetson AGX Orin, INT8 is faster than FP16 only in the implicit formulation of TensorRT, and the replay detector runs fastest on the deep-learning accelerator (DLA). Trained on public data only, the combined system of 0.72 M parameters recognizes 95.2% of the signs of a held-out automotive camera at 90% precision and 94 frames per second.

Abstract
Gaussian-splatting Simultaneous Localization and Mapping (SLAM) augments conventional SLAM with a dense, renderable 3D scene representation, enabling high-quality reconstruction and novel-view synthesis. However, its localization, reconstruction, and rendering performance depends strongly on which observations are retained for mapping. Existing systems commonly rely on fixed temporal intervals or simple pose- and overlap-based criteria for keyframe selection. In colonoscopy, non-uniform camera motion, repetitive anatomy, specularities, and visual artifacts can make these criteria unreliable, leading to either redundant keyframes or missed informative viewpoints. Accumulated motion guides adaptive keyframe selection, with complementary cues suppressing redundant or unreliable candidates. Density-aware mapping with anatomical contour supervision (colon folds) emphasizes structurally informative regions, while restricted temporal deformation accommodates local tissue dynamics without unconstrained geometric drift. We evaluate KFS$^4$LAM against Gaussian-SLAM baselines on C3VD and C3VD-v2, together with cross-backbone and component ablations. KFS$^4$LAM achieves strong overall performance across trajectory, rendering, and depth metrics, with pronounced gains on more challenging C3VD-v2 dataset. In the matched comparison with SplaTAM on C3VD-v2, it reduces rotational relative pose error by 20.3% and depth RMSE by 38.4%, while improving PSNR by 0.78 dB. Cross-backbone experiments further show that the framework is not tied to a single underlying SLAM architecture. Code and visualization results are available at https://xinweiju.github.io/KFSLAM/.

Abstract
We introduce Material Coating, a novel image editing task that simulates applying a thin material layer onto an object while preserving its underlying coarse and fine geometry. Material coating is fundamentally different from existing "material transfer" methods, which are designed to replace an object's intrinsic material, often overwriting fine details. To address this new task, we construct a large-scale synthetic dataset (110K images) of 3D objects with varied, physically-based coatings, named DataCoat110K. We then propose CoatFusion, a novel architecture that enables this task by conditioning a diffusion model on both a 2D albedo texture and granular, PBR-style parametric controls, including roughness, metalness, transmission, and a key thickness parameter. Experiments and user studies show CoatFusion produces realistic, controllable coatings and significantly outperforms existing material editing and transfer methods on this new task.

Dense4D: Pixel-Level 4D Human Correspondence
Authors: Rıza Alp Güler
First: 2026-10-15T17:52:14+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-15T17:52:14+00:00
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Reconstructing human motion in 4D requires identifying the same surface points through articulation, deformation and changes in visibility. We introduce Dense4D, to our knowledge the first feed-forward model dedicated to dense 4D correspondence across the body, clothing and hair from a set of monocular RGB images, without a body template. Trained specifically for human geometry, Dense4D jointly processes person-centred crops with known image-to-crop transforms to predict Human Point Maps (HPMs) and depth for each image. An HPM assigns each person pixel the 3D position of its observed surface point at a common reference time. These maps support amodal correspondence: points hidden in the reference image can be localised from observations in other frames. Matching HPM coordinates across crops establishes correspondence without predicting camera poses or large scene displacements; per-image depth then lifts the matches into 3D tracks. Experiments on real people demonstrate substantially higher tracking accuracy and closer agreement with scanned surfaces than the evaluated baselines. Points from different frames assemble into coherent human geometry in the reference pose, providing visible evidence of consistent dense correspondence through articulation and clothing deformation. Additional qualitative results, videos, and an interactive viewer are available at https://alpguler.com/dense4d.

Abstract
As joint audio-video generation models become more capable, they can generate audio-visual scenes exhibiting three forms of complexity: multi-event composition, multi-layer audio mixture, and asymmetric cross-modal relations. However, current evaluations largely reuse metrics from earlier simple and unidirectional tasks. Abstract
Spatial intelligence lets an agent perceive and act in the physical world, and many of its judgments must be made at interaction rates: which spatial relation holds, how far an object is, or where to place it. Vision-language models (VLMs) produce these answers by free-form text generation, paying for autoregressive decoding and answer parsing even though the output space is compact: one of a few options, a scalar, or a 2D point. Abstract
Generative models now synthesise video that is hard to distinguish from genuine footage, and learned detectors that key on a generator's artefacts degrade sharply on unseen models. This paper develops a forensic framework that authenticates video by a signal that generation does not control: the electric network frequency (ENF), the mains-driven hum in audio and flicker in video. Abstract

Abstract
CNet is a C++/CUDA framework for deep complex-valued neural networks (CVNNs), trained by gradient descent with explicit Wirtinger (CR-calculus) derivatives. Complex models are underexplored yet natural where data is intrinsically complex -- RF/IQ, MRI k-space, radar/SAR, audio spectra -- and phase carries information real networks discard. CNet is physics-native: a network is a cascade of complex (often unitary) operations on an amplitude vector, and classification is a Born-rule measurement p_k = |z_k|^2 / ||z||^2 rather than a softmax over real logits. Every layer ships a CPU reference and a finite-difference-verified CUDA kernel; the static, batch-cloned computation graph captures cleanly into CUDA Graphs, outperforming eager and compiled torch.complex64 on an H100. We present three studies. Abstract

Abstract
